## One-Sentence Summary

Pegaspargase is a long-acting, pegylated form of L-asparaginase, originally used as part of multi-agent chemotherapy for acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, pegaspargase is a pegylated E. coli-derived L-asparaginase. It depletes circulating asparagine. Lymphoblasts have low asparagine synthetase expression and depend on external asparagine, so depletion selectively kills them. This mechanism is well established.

Lymphoblastic lymphoma and ALL are treated with the same asparaginase-containing regimens, such as BFM-type and hyper-CVAD protocols. The prediction therefore mostly confirms an existing use.

Other predictions in the data (such as chronic lymphocytic leukemia, follicular lymphoma and methylcobalamin deficiency cblE) have no trials or literature and a weak mechanistic rationale. They are not covered in detail here. One exception is extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma, which has promising Phase 2 and retrospective data for pegaspargase-based regimens. It was mapped under "Hodgkin lymphoma" in the data and should be evaluated as a separate indication.

## Safety Considerations

- **Key Warnings and Contraindications**: Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the package insert.
- **Toxicities described in the literature**: hypersensitivity reactions, pancreatitis, hepatotoxicity, thrombosis, hypertriglyceridemia and hyperglycemia. Tolerability decreases with age and obesity, and dose capping and levocarnitine for hepatotoxicity are being studied.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found, and those displayed are all of moderate level. Examples include corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone, budesonide), glucose-lowering agents (metformin, acarbose, canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, alogliptin, saxagliptin, dulaglutide, albiglutide, chlorpropamide), aspirin, bupropion, and antibiotics (doxycycline, tetracycline, clarithromycin). The glucose-lowering agent interactions are consistent with the reported hyperglycemia during pegaspargase therapy.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
The predicted indication is essentially the original one (ALL and lymphoblastic lymphoma). Multiple completed Phase 3 trials and a large body of literature support pegaspargase-containing regimens, so efficacy evidence is strong. The main concerns are known toxicities, missing label safety data, and the fact that the only US license (BLA103411) is cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Verification of current US product availability and licensing status
- A monitoring plan for hypersensitivity, pancreatitis, hepatotoxicity, thrombosis and hypertriglyceridemia, with age-adjusted dosing
- Separate evaluation of extranodal NK/T-cell lymphoma as its own indication; the other predicted diseases (CLL, follicular lymphoma, cblE deficiency, CML blast phase) lack supporting evidence and should remain on hold
