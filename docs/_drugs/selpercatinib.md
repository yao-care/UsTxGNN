# Selpercatinib: From RET-Driven Cancers to Pulmonary Hypertension

## One-Sentence Summary

Selpercatinib is a selective RET kinase inhibitor used in RET-driven cancers such as RET fusion-positive non-small-cell lung cancer and medullary thyroid carcinoma. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **pulmonary hypertension**, but **0 clinical trials** and **0 directly relevant publications** support this direction. The three publications compiled concern cancer or safety, not pulmonary hypertension.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general knowledge, selpercatinib is a selective RET kinase inhibitor. Its use in RET-altered cancers is reflected in the literature compiled here (RET fusion NSCLC and RET-mutated medullary thyroid carcinoma).

The link to pulmonary hypertension is only indirect. Kinase signaling is involved in pulmonary vascular remodeling, and some other kinase inhibitors have been studied in pulmonary arterial hypertension. Conversely, some kinase inhibitors are associated with pulmonary vascular toxicity. No direct mechanistic or clinical evidence for RET inhibition in pulmonary hypertension is available.

The score is very high, but it comes from a graph-based computational prediction only. Two other predictions, migraine disorder (99.17%) and migraine with brainstem aura (99.05%), also have no trials or literature. The brainstem aura prediction is a subtype of the first, so it is not independent evidence.
