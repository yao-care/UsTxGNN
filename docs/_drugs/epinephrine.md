## Epinephrine: From Open-Angle Glaucoma, Asthma and Anaphylaxis to Obstructive Lung Disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Epinephrine is a non-selective adrenergic agonist, originally used for conditions such as anaphylaxis, asthma, bronchitis, hypotensive disorder and open-angle glaucoma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Obstructive Lung Disease**,
with **15 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction. Because the original indications already include asthma and emphysema, this is largely an on-label or near-label extension rather than true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, epinephrine is a non-selective adrenergic agonist. Beta-2 receptor stimulation relaxes bronchial smooth muscle, and alpha-1 stimulation reduces mucosal oedema. Together these reduce airway obstruction.

Obstructive lung disease covers asthma and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD). Asthma, bronchitis, emphysema and bronchiectasis are already among epinephrine's known indications, so the prediction is consistent with its established use.

Two caveats apply. The Phase 3 evidence is for an inhaled HFA-MDI epinephrine formulation (E004) in asthma, and the evidence in bronchiolitis is mixed.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph** (not label text): hypertension, coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction, atrial fibrillation, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease, hyperthyroidism, diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, angle-closure glaucoma, epilepsy, liver and kidney disease, respiratory failure, myasthenia gravis, and benign prostatic hyperplasia, among others.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were recorded. Those listed include major interactions with tricyclic antidepressants such as amitriptyline and amoxapine. Moderate interactions include beta-blockers (atenolol, acebutolol, betaxolol), other beta-agonists (formoterol, arformoterol, salbutamol), stimulants (amphetamine, methylphenidate, atomoxetine), antidiabetic agents (acarbose, alogliptin, albiglutide, acetohexamide) and antipsychotics (aripiprazole, asenapine, amisulpride).

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Three completed Phase 3 trials of inhaled epinephrine (E004) in asthma support the prediction (L1), and asthma is already an established indication. However, the evidence is specific to one inhaled formulation, bronchiolitis data are mixed, and all US licenses are cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirm current US regulatory status and formulation, since no valid license exists
- Obtain the package insert warnings and contraindications
- Obtain detailed mechanism-of-action data
- Review the cardiovascular and drug-interaction risks for the intended population
