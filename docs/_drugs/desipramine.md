## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, desipramine is a tricyclic antidepressant with strong norepinephrine reuptake inhibition. That action is plausibly relevant to ADHD, where noradrenergic modulation in prefrontal circuits is thought to matter.

Its original indications (anxiety, neurotic and dysthymic disorders) are psychiatric conditions treated through monoaminergic modulation. ADHD is a neurodevelopmental disorder, and non-stimulant treatments such as atomoxetine share the noradrenergic mechanism. This makes the prediction mechanistically consistent.

However, the evidence for the inattentive subtype itself is a model prediction only. The supporting literature concerns ADHD in general, and it also flags cardiovascular safety concerns with tricyclics in children, including reported sudden-death cases.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related conditions (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: long QT syndrome, familial long QT syndrome, torsades de pointes, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease, epilepsy, bipolar disorder, major affective disorder, schizophrenia, kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, benign prostatic hyperplasia, substance abuse/dependence, stroke and cerebrovascular disorders, cerebral infarction, angle-closure glaucoma, glaucoma, hyperthyroidism, Graves disease and thyrotoxicosis.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were recorded. Major-level examples include epinephrine, bupropion, lorcaserin, potassium citrate, cisapride and dexfenfluramine. Moderate-level examples include famotidine, cimetidine, clarithromycin, morphine, loperamide, and anticholinergics such as atropine, hyoscyamine and glycopyrronium.
- **Pediatric cardiovascular concern**: the literature notes sudden-death cases with tricyclics in children, which is especially relevant for ADHD.

Please refer to the package insert for official warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The inattentive-subtype prediction rests on a model score alone, with no trials and no literature. The parent ADHD indication has only review-level support, and desipramine carries cardiovascular risks and a heavy interaction burden. Safer, better-established alternatives exist, and the drug has no active US licenses. For ADHD overall, the evidence supports a research question rather than active advancement.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Controlled trial evidence of desipramine in ADHD, ideally including the inattentive subtype
- A cardiovascular (ECG) safety assessment for the target population, especially children
- A review of how the cancelled market status affects practical feasibility

### Other Predictions Noted

For context, the two other predictions with literature support are weaker or comparable:
- **Agoraphobia / panic disorder (L3, research question)**: it has a small placebo-controlled RCT ([PMID 8290663](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8290663/)) and open-label studies, but modern guidelines prefer SSRIs and SNRIs.
- **Obsessive-compulsive disorder (L3, research question)**: controlled studies show desipramine is inferior to serotonergic agents ([PMID 2686576](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2686576/), [PMID 2112374](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2112374/)), so it is unlikely to be a preferred therapy.
