# Methylprednisolone: From Systemic Corticosteroid Therapy to Alopecia Areata

## One-Sentence Summary

Methylprednisolone is a corticosteroid marketed in the US as oral tablets and injectable suspensions. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **alopecia areata**. Of the 17 trials retrieved, only **1 directly tests methylprednisolone in this disease** (Phase 4, n=42). The **20 publications** are mostly retrospective or open-label studies and reviews, with no Phase 3 RCT.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the source record. Based on general pharmacology, methylprednisolone is a glucocorticoid receptor agonist with broad anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects.

Alopecia areata is a T-cell-mediated autoimmune attack on the hair follicle, so suppressing that immune response is a plausible treatment approach. Pulse-dose corticosteroids (oral or intravenous) are already used clinically in severe or extensive cases.

The main concerns raised in the pulse-therapy reviews are relapse after tapering and the adverse effects of steroids. The mechanistic link rests on general glucocorticoid pharmacology, not on drug-specific mechanism data.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information. The pulse-therapy literature identifies relapse after tapering and steroid adverse effects as the main concerns.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Alopecia areata has a plausible mechanism, a completed Phase 4 study, and a body of pulse-therapy studies (L3). However, the evidence is mostly retrospective or open-label, with high relapse rates reported. No Phase 3 RCT exists. The package insert safety review is a blocking data gap, so the candidate cannot proceed to safety screening yet.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking gap)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- The design and results of NCT01167946 (controlled or single-arm)
- A controlled trial, or a systematic review, of methylprednisolone pulse therapy against placebo or another treatment
- A plan for relapse after tapering and for steroid safety monitoring
- Assessment of route compatibility for IV pulse regimens

**Other predictions:** The other predictions in the pack are much weaker. Idiopathic steroid-sensitive nephrotic syndrome (rank 10) also reaches L3, but it is already a steroid-standard-of-care disease, so it is not a strong repurposing signal. Ranks 2 to 9 are L4 to L5 (model prediction only or off-target literature) and should stay on Hold.
