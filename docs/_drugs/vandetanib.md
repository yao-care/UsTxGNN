## One-Sentence Summary

Vandetanib is an oral multi-target tyrosine kinase inhibitor that blocks VEGFR2, EGFR and RET signalling.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **renal cell carcinoma**, supported by **4 registered clinical trials** (all small Phase 2 or Phase 1/2 studies) and **5 publications**, none of which reports efficacy results in this disease.
The evidence level is **L3**: the most informative study is a completed Phase 2 trial in von Hippel-Lindau disease with renal tumours, while the other renal cell carcinoma trials were terminated early with very few patients.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on what is known, vandetanib is a multi-target kinase inhibitor acting on VEGFR2, EGFR and RET.

Clear cell renal cell carcinoma is typically characterised by loss of the VHL gene and HIF-driven overexpression of VEGF, so blocking VEGFR2-dependent angiogenesis is mechanistically coherent. Combination with metformin has also been explored in fumarate hydratase- or SDH-deficient kidney cancers, which depend on altered metabolism.

There are important caveats. The available trials are small, two were terminated early (3 and 7 patients), and no Phase 3 data exist. Established VEGFR-targeted agents (sunitinib, pazopanib, axitinib, cabozantinib) are already standard of care in this disease, so vandetanib would need to show a clear advantage.

## Safety Considerations

- **Conditions of concern**: Records compiled for this report flag caution in the following conditions: hypertension, long QT syndrome (including familial long QT syndrome and torsades de pointes), hypothyroidism, potassium deficiency, hypercalcaemia and other phosphorus/calcium disorders, impaired renal function, interstitial lung disease, heart failure, brain ischaemia or transient ischaemic attack, and posterior leukoencephalopathy syndrome. These are not taken from label text, so the package insert should be checked.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found. Major-level interactions include clarithromycin, levofloxacin, dolasetron, palonosetron, picosulfuric acid, sodium sulfate and polyethylene glycol (3350 with electrolytes). Moderate-level interactions include famotidine, metformin, loperamide, dexamethasone, bisacodyl and lactitol. Several of these are likely QT-related or electrolyte-related.
- **Class safety literature**: Meta-analyses of VEGFR tyrosine kinase inhibitors report hepatotoxicity, proteinuria and treatment-related mortality risks. Long-term follow-up in medullary thyroid cancer highlights QT prolongation as a tolerability concern.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic rationale is plausible, but the evidence is thin: one completed Phase 2 trial in VHL-associated renal tumours without reported results, and other trials terminated at 3 and 7 patients. Established VEGFR inhibitors already serve this population, and vandetanib carries notable cardiac (QT) risk.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results of the completed VHL Phase 2 trial (NCT00566995)
- Mechanism-of-action data and the full package insert warnings and contraindications
- A comparison against current standard VEGFR inhibitors for renal cell carcinoma
- A cardiac and QT monitoring plan, and review of interacting medications
