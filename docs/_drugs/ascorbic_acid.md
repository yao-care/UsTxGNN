## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, ascorbic acid is an antioxidant that supports endothelial barrier function, collagen synthesis, and catecholamine synthesis.

Its original indication, scurvy, reflects failed collagen formation and fragile vessels. The same functions are relevant to tissue repair, capillary leak, and oxidative damage after injury. This gives a rationale for burn resuscitation, sepsis-induced lung injury, ischemia-reperfusion injury, and wound healing.

"Injury" is a heterogeneous category, and the results are not consolidated into one specific indication. The next step is to narrow to a single subtype, such as burns or sepsis-induced acute lung injury.

## Safety Considerations

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the package insert for these.

- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (not label text): hypertension, epilepsy, kidney disease, liver disease, peptic ulcer disease, diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, hypercalcemia, nephrocalcinosis, anuria, primary hyperoxaluria, hereditary hemochromatosis, sickle cell anemia, blood coagulation disease, and others (30 conditions in total). These should be verified against the label.
- **Drug interactions:** 25 interactions were found, all minor or moderate.
  - Moderate: amphetamine, dextroamphetamine, lisdexamfetamine, metamfetamine, benzphetamine, aluminum hydroxide, bortezomib, deferoxamine.
  - Minor (examples): paclitaxel, cisplatin, doxorubicin, ephedrine, pseudoephedrine, flecainide, fluphenazine, indinavir, mexiletine, ethinylestradiol.
- **Preclinical signal:** One rat study (PMID 17953708) found enhanced esophageal carcinogenesis when ascorbic acid was combined with sodium nitrite under acid reflux conditions.
