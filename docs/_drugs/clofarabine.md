## Clofarabine: From Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia to Myeloid Leukemia
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Clofarabine is a purine nucleoside analog originally used to treat relapsed or refractory acute lymphoblastic leukemia (ALL).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **myeloid leukemia**,
with **45 clinical trials** (including 2 completed Phase 3 trials) and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Clofarabine is a second-generation purine nucleoside analog. It inhibits ribonucleotide reductase and DNA polymerase, which depletes the nucleotides available for DNA replication, and it also induces mitochondrial apoptosis. These actions hit rapidly dividing leukemic blasts, whatever their lineage. Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank was not available for this report; the mechanism described here comes from the published literature compiled for the report.

ALL and acute myeloid leukemia (AML) are both acute leukemias driven by proliferating blasts, so the mechanism is shared between lymphoid and myeloid disease. Clofarabine is already used off-label in adults with relapsed or refractory AML, in salvage regimens and in transplant conditioning, which supports the model's prediction.

## Safety Considerations

No label-based warnings or contraindications were available for this report. Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

- **Conditions flagged in the knowledge graph (not label text):** liver disease, gallbladder disease, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, anemia, hyperbilirubinemia (including Gilbert syndrome), kidney failure, uremia, hypotensive disorder, capillary leak syndrome, and active infectious disease including fungal infections.
- **Reported kidney toxicity:** a published case describes clofarabine-induced kidney injury in an adult with refractory AML.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were found, and all 20 listed in the compiled data are rated Moderate. They include amphotericin B (several formulations), acarbose, pioglitazone, bupropion, mesalazine, balsalazide, clarithromycin, kanamycin, neomycin, levofloxacin, minocycline, naltrexone, nicotinamide, nitisinone and olsalazine.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Two completed Phase 3 randomized trials (n=727 and n=326) plus many Phase 2 studies and multiple reviews support clofarabine in AML. However, the evidence is largely in older or relapsed/refractory populations and in combination regimens. Safety data from the label is missing, and the US market status is cancelled-only.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Phase 3 results (efficacy and survival outcomes) for NCT02085408 and NCT00317642, since the compiled data holds only the trial designs
- The FDA package insert warnings and contraindications
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- A monitoring plan for myelosuppression, kidney and liver toxicity, and infection risk
- Clarification of regulatory and supply status, because all US generic licenses are cancelled
