## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on what is known, acitretin is a second-generation oral retinoid. It normalizes keratinocyte differentiation and proliferation and reduces follicular hyperkeratinization, which is the process behind follicular plugging in acne.

Its direct effect on sebaceous glands is weaker than that of isotretinoin, the retinoid usually used for severe acne. A review of retinoids and sebaceous gland activity notes that the anti-acne effect of newer oral retinoids is hard to predict from experimental models. The plausibility therefore rests mainly on retinoid class effects on the pilosebaceous unit.

The strongest signals in the retrieved data concern hidradenitis suppurativa (also called acne inversa), a different follicular occlusion disease. The "acne (disease)" label appears to blend these conditions, so the high score of 0.9994 should be read cautiously. Acitretin is not a standard therapy for acne vulgaris. Its teratogenicity, with a very long pregnancy-avoidance period, is a major safety constraint.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-type relations in the compiled data** (not label text): liver failure, drug-induced liver injury, pancreatitis, hypertriglyceridemia, diabetes mellitus, diabetic ketoacidosis, obesity disorders, pseudotumor cerebri, intracranial hypertension, vision disorders, night blindness, hyperostosis, substance abuse/dependence, anxiety and dysthymic disorders.
- **Teratogenicity**: acitretin is a potent teratogen, requiring long-term pregnancy avoidance with a prolonged washout (etretinate can form in the presence of alcohol).
- **Drug Interactions** (100 interactions recorded; main ones):
  - Major: doxycycline, minocycline, tetracycline, vitamin A
  - Moderate: sulfonylureas (acetohexamide, chlorpropamide, glimepiride, glipizide, glyburide, tolazamide, tolbutamide) and naltrexone

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only direct evidence for acne is a single case report; the trial and most of the literature concern hidradenitis suppurativa, a different disease. Together with acitretin's teratogenicity and hepatic and metabolic risks, the high score alone does not justify advancing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clarify whether the prediction refers to acne vulgaris or to acne inversa (hidradenitis suppurativa)
- Controlled studies of acitretin in acne itself
- Mechanism-of-action data for acitretin in the pilosebaceous unit
- Review of the package insert warnings and contraindications
- A pregnancy-prevention and hepatic and lipid monitoring plan
