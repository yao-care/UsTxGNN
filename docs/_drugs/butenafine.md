# Butenafine: From Superficial Fungal Skin Infections (Tinea) to Cutaneous Candidiasis

## One-Sentence Summary

Butenafine is a topical benzylamine antifungal, originally used for tinea pedis, tinea corporis, and pityriasis versicolor.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **cutaneous candidiasis**, but **no clinical trials** and only **3 publications** (none of them direct clinical evidence for this use) are available, so the prediction rests mainly on the model score and mechanistic plausibility.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Butenafine is a benzylamine antifungal that inhibits squalene epoxidase, an enzyme in the fungal ergosterol synthesis pathway. Blocking it disrupts the fungal cell membrane. Detailed mechanism-of-action text is not available in the data compiled for this report, but this mechanism is well established for the drug class.

Candida species also depend on ergosterol, so a mechanistic link to cutaneous candidiasis exists. Cutaneous candidiasis is also a superficial skin fungal infection, like the tinea conditions butenafine is already used for.

There are important caveats. Butenafine's activity against Candida is generally weaker and more variable than against dermatophytes. The retrieved literature consists of antimycotic reviews and a topical therapy review, with no butenafine-specific clinical study in cutaneous candidiasis. The high model score is a prediction and is not backed by direct clinical data.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high (99.33%), but it is not supported by any clinical trial or butenafine-specific study in cutaneous candidiasis. Butenafine is also known to be less reliable against Candida than against dermatophytes. In addition, no US product is currently valid, so availability would need to be confirmed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- In vitro susceptibility data (MIC) for butenafine against clinically relevant Candida species
- Any controlled or comparative clinical data in cutaneous candidiasis, compared against established agents such as azoles
- Package insert warnings, contraindications, and detailed mechanism-of-action data
- Confirmation of current US availability, since all three authorizations are cancelled

**Other predictions for context:**
- **Superficial mycosis** has the strongest literature support (including a vehicle-controlled RCT in tinea pedis and a randomized comparison with bifonazole). It overlaps heavily with the drug's already-approved indications, so it is not a true repurposing opportunity.
- **Endothrix infection, ectothrix infection, and Majocchi granuloma** have no trials or literature, and topical therapy penetrates hair follicles poorly. These remain model predictions only (Hold).
