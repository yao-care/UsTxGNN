# Black Cohosh: From Menopausal Symptom Relief to Heart Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Black cohosh (*Cimicifuga racemosa*) is a herbal product on the US market, and the literature describes it mainly as a remedy for menopausal symptoms.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **heart disease**, but this is a very broad label.
Of **3 registered trials** and **10 publications** retrieved, only one Phase 3 trial (terminated early, surrogate endpoints only) and a few small menopausal-cohort studies bear on the question. There is also a **bradycardia safety signal**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Black cohosh is a botanical product, widely used for menopausal symptoms, and its efficacy in heart disease has not been established. A mechanistic link is plausible but unproven.

Preclinical work suggests that cimicifugamide, a constituent of *Cimicifuga* rhizomes, acts as a non-selective beta-adrenergic agonist (PMID 28347916). Small studies in menopausal women report effects on hemodynamic parameters, quality of life, and markers such as asymmetric dimethylarginine (ADMA) (PMIDs 30176632, 17430734). Menopause is also linked to higher cardiovascular risk, which gives the prediction some biological context.

Two caveats limit this reasoning. "Heart disease" is a very broad label, and the TxGNN score alone does not identify a specific cardiac indication. The other nine top predictions (Laubry-Pezzi syndrome, Pierre Robin syndromes, chromosome 7q deletion, Jeune syndrome, orofacial clefting, pulmonary valve disease, and others) are prediction-only (L5). They are mostly congenital or structural conditions with no plausible mechanism, and likely reflect knowledge-graph connectivity rather than biology.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: No DrugBank interaction records were found.
- **Cardiac signal**: A case report describes reversible complete heart block and bradycardia after starting a black cohosh supplement (PMID 20955128). This needs assessment before any cardiac use.
- **Liver**: Case series and biopsy studies link black cohosh to rare idiosyncratic hepatotoxicity (PMIDs 19339903, 24657312).
- **Animal toxicology**: A National Toxicology Program mouse study reported hematologic alterations with black cohosh extract (PMID 36373576).

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is high (99.19%), but direct evidence is thin. The only Phase 3 trial was terminated and used surrogate endpoints, and supporting studies are small or preclinical. A cardiac safety signal (bradycardia and heart block) is unresolved, and "heart disease" is too broad to define a testable indication.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data (e.g., from DrugBank) to test the beta-adrenergic hypothesis
- Package insert warnings and contraindications, which block the safety screening step
- A specific cardiac indication (e.g., menopause-associated hypertension) to replace the broad "heart disease" label
- Controlled trials with clinical cardiac endpoints in a defined population
- A cardiac safety assessment covering bradycardia and conduction effects, plus a liver safety review
