## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on general knowledge, polymyxin B is a cationic polypeptide antibiotic that disrupts the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria. This makes a link plausible to bacterial tracheobronchitis or pneumonia caused by multidrug-resistant organisms such as *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and *Acinetobacter baumannii*.

However, the supporting evidence is weak and the signals are mixed. Some studies examine inhaled polymyxin B in respiratory infections (a ventilator-associated pneumonia/tracheobronchitis comparison, and a small case series of 19 patients). Others show that inhaled or endobronchial polymyxin B can provoke bronchoconstriction, is used as a non-specific bronchial provocation agent in asthma and chronic bronchitis, and induces airway eosinophil accumulation in animal models. This raises a respiratory safety concern for airway delivery. The very high TxGNN score is not backed by trial data, and ventilator-associated pneumonia is not equivalent to bronchitis.

The other two predictions fare differently. Laryngotracheitis (croup) is mostly viral, and the prediction rests on the model score alone, with no trials or literature. Conjunctivitis is mechanistically coherent, but polymyxin B/trimethoprim ophthalmic solution is already used for bacterial conjunctivitis, so this is closer to an established use than a novel repurposing.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 73 interactions were recorded. Examples of Major-level interactions include:
  - Amikacin
  - Cisatracurium
  - Cidofovir
  - Deferasirox
  - Botulinum toxin type A
  - Intravenous human immunoglobulin G
  - Iodinated contrast agents (iothalamic acid, diatrizoate)
  - Live cholera vaccine
- **Moderate-level examples**: capreomycin, mesalazine, balsalazide, ibandronate, clofarabine, estradiol, ethinylestradiol.
- **Respiratory concern from the literature**: inhaled or endobronchial polymyxin B can cause bronchoconstriction and airway eosinophilic inflammation, which is directly relevant to the bronchitis prediction.
- **Ocular concern from the literature**: a case report describes anaphylaxis to polymyxin B-trimethoprim eye drops.

Please refer to the package insert for the full warnings and contraindications, which were not available in the data compiled for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
For bronchitis, the evidence is limited to indirect, small, or old studies and animal models, with no registered trials, and the literature raises airway safety concerns. The high TxGNN score alone is not enough. Laryngotracheitis has no supporting evidence at all. Conjunctivitis is supported by randomized comparisons, but these involve the polymyxin B/trimethoprim combination, which is already an established use, so it is not a new repurposing finding.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data and the original approved indication text
- For bronchitis: controlled clinical data on inhaled polymyxin B with a respiratory safety assessment (bronchospasm risk), and a clear distinction between bronchitis and ventilator-associated pneumonia or tracheobronchitis
- For conjunctivitis: confirmation of the current US status of polymyxin B ophthalmic products, since the injectable licenses listed are all cancelled, and evaluation of polymyxin B alone versus the combination
- Route and formulation compatibility assessment (currently only injectable forms are listed)
