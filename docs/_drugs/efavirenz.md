# Efavirenz: From HIV Infection to Feline Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Efavirenz is a non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor (NNRTI) originally used to treat HIV infection and AIDS.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **feline acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (FIV infection in cats)**,
but currently only **0 clinical trials** and **1 publication** (a preclinical biochemical study) relate to this direction, so the evidence is very weak.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, efavirenz is an NNRTI that blocks the reverse transcriptase (RT) enzyme of HIV-1. Feline immunodeficiency virus (FIV) is also a lentivirus that depends on its own reverse transcriptase, so a mechanistic link is plausible.

However, the link is uncertain. NNRTIs bind a pocket that is quite specific to HIV-1 RT and generally work poorly against the RT of other lentiviruses. A 2023 biochemical and structural study (PMID 38031646) compared NNRTIs, including efavirenz, against feline and human immunodeficiency virus RT. The data compiled for this report do not include its results, so efavirenz's activity against FIV cannot be confirmed. Cross-species activity should not be assumed.

Two other predictions were also generated by the model:

- **Simian immunodeficiency virus (SIV) infection** (score 99.80%): Native SIV is intrinsically resistant to NNRTIs. The macaque studies use RT-SHIV, a chimeric virus carrying HIV-1 reverse transcriptase, where efavirenz acts on the HIV-1 RT it was designed for. This largely restates the existing HIV indication and does not show efficacy against native SIV.
- **Neurodevelopmental disorder with ataxic gait, absent speech, and decreased cortical white matter** (score 99.77%): There are no trials and no literature. Efavirenz has known CNS effects, but its neuropsychiatric side effects would be a safety concern for this kind of indication.

- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals (not label text)**: liver failure and liver disease, epilepsy, kidney disease, anxiety and other psychiatric disorders (neurotic, dysthymic, delusional, paranoid schizophrenia), chronic hepatitis B and C infection, fatty liver disease/NASH, hypertriglyceridemia, lactic acidosis, Fanconi syndrome, hypophosphatemia, osteomalacia and rickets, gallbladder disease, and obesity disorders.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on model output alone plus one preclinical study. There are no clinical trials, and the top predicted indication is a veterinary disease. NNRTIs generally work poorly against non-HIV-1 lentiviral RTs, and the SIV evidence only reflects efavirenz acting on HIV-1 RT. In addition, all US licenses are cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The actual results of the 2023 FIV RT study (PMID 38031646), especially efavirenz's inhibition data against FIV RT
- Detailed mechanism of action data (MOA)
- FDA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking data gap for safety screening)
- Confirmation of the target market, since FIV is a veterinary indication and would need veterinary regulatory and formulation considerations
- Review of the neuropsychiatric and hepatic safety signals before considering any new indication
