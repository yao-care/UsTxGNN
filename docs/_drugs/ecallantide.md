## Ecallantide: From Hereditary Angioedema to C1 Inhibitor Deficiency
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Ecallantide is a recombinant plasma kallikrein inhibitor, originally used to treat acute attacks of hereditary angioedema (HAE).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **C1 inhibitor deficiency**, a condition closely related to its original indication.
Currently, **7 clinical trials** (3 phase 3 studies, 2 of them randomized and placebo-controlled) and **20 publications** support this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, ecallantide is a recombinant, highly potent and reversible inhibitor of human plasma kallikrein.

In C1 inhibitor deficiency, the loss of C1-INH control leaves plasma kallikrein unchecked. This drives excess bradykinin production and increased vascular permeability, which causes angioedema. Blocking kallikrein directly reduces bradykinin generation and targets the core of the disease.

This predicted indication largely overlaps with the drug's original use (hereditary angioedema due to C1-INH deficiency), so it is closer to an on-label use than a true repurposing. The acquired form of C1-INH deficiency is a closely related extension. A published case series (PMID 23406939) describes ecallantide for acute attacks in acquired C1-INH deficiency.

## Safety Considerations

- **Hypersensitivity and anaphylaxis**: Ecallantide is associated with a risk of hypersensitivity reactions, including anaphylaxis (PMID 25609335), and carries a boxed warning. It should be administered by a healthcare professional.
- **Coagulation**: The long-term observational study (NCT01059526) specifically examined immunogenicity, allergic reactions and hypercoagulability/hypocoagulability risk.
- No drug interaction records were found.

Please refer to the package insert for the full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Two randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 3 trials (EDEMA4 and NCT00262080) support efficacy in HAE with C1-INH deficiency, which supports an L1 evidence level. However, the prediction essentially restates the original indication. Evidence for acquired C1-INH deficiency is limited to a case series. The anaphylaxis risk and the cancelled-only US market status also require caution.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (not yet obtained; this is a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation of current US availability, since the only license (BLA125277) is cancelled
- Controlled data in acquired C1-INH deficiency beyond the published case series
- An anaphylaxis risk management plan, including administration under healthcare professional supervision

**Other predicted indications:** Serpinopathy with toxic serpin polymerization, Peyronie disease, pancreatitis, and esophageal varices (with and without bleeding) are supported only by model scores, with no clinical trials or literature. Their recommendation is **Hold**.
