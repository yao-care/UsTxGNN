## One-Sentence Summary

Sertraline is an SSRI antidepressant, originally used for anxiety disorders, obsessive-compulsive disorder, post-traumatic stress disorder, social phobia and related conditions.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Agoraphobia**, supported by **2 clinical trials** (only one of them relevant) and **19 publications**, including several randomized trials and network meta-analyses in panic disorder with or without agoraphobia.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, sertraline is a selective serotonin reuptake inhibitor (SSRI). SSRIs are thought to reduce anxiety and panic by modulating serotonergic signalling in fear circuitry (amygdala, prefrontal cortex and brainstem pathways).

Sertraline's original indications already cover several anxiety-spectrum conditions, including social phobia, phobic disorder and anxiety disorder. Agoraphobia usually occurs together with panic disorder and belongs to the same family of conditions. This prediction is therefore a close extension of existing use, not a distant repurposing.

The published evidence supports this link. It includes a double-blind multicenter trial of sertraline in panic disorder, comparator trials against imipramine and alprazolam, and recent network meta-analyses of drug treatment for panic disorder with or without agoraphobia.
