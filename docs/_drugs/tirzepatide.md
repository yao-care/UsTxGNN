# Tirzepatide: From Type 2 Diabetes and Weight Management to Osteoarthritis

## One-Sentence Summary

Tirzepatide is a once-weekly injectable dual GIP/GLP-1 receptor agonist, marketed as Mounjaro and Zepbound for glycaemic control in type 2 diabetes and for weight management.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **osteoarthritis** (the strongest-supported of its top 10 predictions), with **1 clinical trial** and **19 publications** currently supporting this direction.
The only trial is a Phase 4 study that is still recruiting, so direct efficacy evidence is not yet available.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action text is not available in the data compiled for this report. Pharmacology data show that tirzepatide acts on two targets, the **GIP receptor (GIPR)** and the **GLP-1 receptor (GLP1R)**. Its efficacy in type 2 diabetes and obesity is established: in the SURMOUNT-1 trial, patients achieved roughly 15-20% body-weight reduction depending on dose.

Obesity is a key modifiable risk factor for knee osteoarthritis, acting through mechanical joint loading and systemic inflammation. Substantial weight loss with tirzepatide could therefore reduce pain and improve function. Reviews also propose a weight-independent "incretin-joint axis", in which incretin-based therapies may influence synovial inflammation and cartilage catabolism, though this remains a hypothesis.

Caveat: most of the supporting literature consists of reviews and cost or modelling analyses, not primary efficacy trials in osteoarthritis.

Other predictions with some literature support:
- **Gout** (score 96.75%, L3): a post hoc analysis of the SURMOUNT-1 RCT ([PMID 41198460](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41198460/), *Annals of the Rheumatic Diseases*, 2026) examined whether tirzepatide lowers serum uric acid through weight reduction. A narrative review ([PMID 42469142](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42469142/)) discusses weight management in gout. No gout-specific trials are registered.
- **Rheumatoid arthritis** (score 93.60%, L4): evidence is indirect, mainly an observational study of GLP-1 RAs in RA patients ([PMID 40932015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40932015/)) and reviews. There are no registered trials.
- Other predictions (pseudoachondroplasia, osteoarthritis susceptibility, benign prostatic hyperplasia, Hunter-Thompson acromesomelic dysplasia, brachyolmia, brachyolmia-amelogenesis imperfecta syndrome, myosclerosis) have no trials or literature and are model predictions only (L5). The rare skeletal dysplasias have no plausible mechanistic link to incretin signalling.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction query completed, but it returned only pharmacological target information (GIP receptor and GLP-1 receptor), not drug-drug interactions. No clinically relevant interacting drugs are listed.
- **Musculoskeletal adverse events**: A FAERS pharmacovigilance study (PMID 41748946) examined musculoskeletal adverse events with incretin-based drugs, including tirzepatide. This is relevant to a musculoskeletal indication and should be reviewed.

Please refer to the package insert for key warnings and contraindications, which are not available in the data compiled for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Osteoarthritis has a plausible mechanism (weight loss reducing joint load and inflammation), a high TxGNN score, and a dedicated randomised, placebo-controlled Phase 4 trial. However, that trial is still recruiting (expected completion 2037) and has no results, and most of the literature consists of reviews and modelling. Gout is a reasonable secondary research question. The remaining predictions should be on Hold.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results from NCT06191848 (STOP KNEE-OA), or other primary efficacy data in osteoarthritis
- FDA package insert warnings and contraindications, which are currently missing and block safety screening
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Verification of the current US market and authorization status (the data show both NDAs as cancelled)
- For gout, a dedicated outcome trial (flares, uric acid) beyond the SURMOUNT-1 post hoc analysis
