## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, asenapine is a multi-receptor antagonist (including dopamine D2 and serotonin 5-HT2A receptors), and its efficacy in bipolar mania and schizophrenia is established.

For retinal dystrophy, no mechanistic link has been identified. Asenapine's receptor pharmacology has no documented relevance to inherited retinal degeneration pathways. The high score (0.998) reflects the model's output only and is not backed by trials or literature. A rank of 6,413 for this score also suggests that many drug-disease pairs receive similarly high scores, so the number alone should not be over-interpreted.

The other top predictions (congenital glycosylation disorder, hydranencephaly, syndromic and X-linked myopia, Charcot-Marie-Tooth type 1G, polymicrogyria, atypical glycine encephalopathy) are likewise model predictions with no supporting studies. Dopaminergic signalling has a theoretical role in eye growth, and glycine/NMDA modulation is conceptually adjacent to antipsychotic research, but both remain speculative here.

The one prediction with literature, **major affective disorder**, mostly reflects asenapine's existing bipolar I indication. It is a re-detection of known use rather than repurposing.
