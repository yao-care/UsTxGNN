# Mechlorethamine: From Cutaneous T-Cell Lymphoma (Mycosis Fungoides) to Lymph Node Cancer

## One-Sentence Summary

Mechlorethamine is a nitrogen mustard chemotherapy drug, marketed in the US as a topical gel (VALCHLOR) and used for skin-based T-cell lymphoma (mycosis fungoides).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **lymph node cancer** (lymphoma).
Support is mostly historical: **about 20 publications**, including older trials of mechlorethamine-containing regimens such as MOPP in Hodgkin lymphoma. None of the registered **clinical trials** tests mechlorethamine directly.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data were not available in the source record. The following is based on the known class of the drug.

Mechlorethamine is a bifunctional nitrogen mustard alkylating agent. It forms DNA interstrand crosslinks, which trigger cell death (apoptosis) in rapidly dividing lymphoid cells. The topical gel targets skin-homing malignant T cells in mycosis fungoides.

The original and predicted indications are both lymphoid malignancies, so the mechanistic fit is direct. Historically, intravenous mechlorethamine was the "M" in the MOPP regimen (mechlorethamine, vincristine, procarbazine, prednisone) for Hodgkin lymphoma. Related alkylators such as bendamustine and cyclophosphamide are used in indolent B-cell lymphomas, including follicular lymphoma.

The marketed product is a topical gel for skin disease, not a systemic formulation. The prediction therefore rests on class-level plausibility and old intravenous regimens, not on evidence for the current product in nodal disease.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic fit and the historical MOPP data are plausible, but no registered trial tests mechlorethamine in lymph node cancer. The marketed product is a topical gel, so systemic nodal disease would need a different route, and the historical intravenous formulation is not the current product. The evidence pack itself rates this a research question.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- FDA package insert data on warnings, contraindications and approved indication
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- A route and formulation strategy, since the current product is a topical gel
- A comparison against current standard-of-care regimens, given that MOPP has largely been replaced
