## Lomustine: Lymphosarcoma
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Lomustine is an oral nitrosourea alkylating chemotherapy that crosses the blood-brain barrier. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **lymphosarcoma**, with **10 clinical trials** and **20 publications** compiled for this direction. However, only one cited study (a mouse model, PMID 22888657) addresses lymphosarcoma itself, so the evidence level is **L4**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, lomustine is a lipophilic nitrosourea that cross-links DNA in rapidly dividing cells. Its lipophilicity allows it to cross the blood-brain barrier, which is why it is also used in central nervous system tumours.

Lymphoid cancers are generally chemotherapy-sensitive, and lomustine has been used in oral combination regimens for lymphoma, including primary CNS lymphoma. This offers a plausible mechanistic basis for activity in lymphosarcoma.

The direct evidence is limited. The only study of lymphosarcoma itself is a mouse model of intracranial transplanted lymphosarcoma, in which lomustine and gemcitabine each extended survival and the combination extended it further. The human trials involve other lymphoma subtypes, mostly in combination regimens, so lomustine's individual contribution cannot be isolated.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-related cautions (reported relations, not label text)**: kidney disease, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, hyperbilirubinemia (including Gilbert syndrome), drug-induced liver injury, pulmonary fibrosis, and infectious disease (including fungal infection and dermatophytosis).
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found. Major ones include cimetidine, deferiprone, adalimumab and samarium (153Sm) lexidronam. Moderate ones include mercaptopurine, zidovudine, naltrexone, roflumilast, chloramphenicol, aldesleukin, alemtuzumab and several vaccines.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Lomustine has a plausible mechanism for lymphoid cancers, but direct support for lymphosarcoma is limited to a mouse study (evidence level L4). The human trials involve other lymphoma subtypes, are mostly small, and use combination regimens that hide lomustine's own contribution.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Clinical data that directly studies lymphosarcoma, ideally with lomustine's contribution separated from combination partners
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Review of the full package insert for warnings and contraindications
- A monitoring plan for myelosuppression, hepatic, renal and pulmonary toxicity
