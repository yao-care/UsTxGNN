# Duvelisib: From Follicular Lymphoma and CLL/SLL to Hodgkin Lymphoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Duvelisib is an oral dual PI3K-delta/gamma inhibitor, originally used to treat B-cell malignancies such as chronic lymphocytic leukemia/small lymphocytic lymphoma (CLL/SLL) and follicular lymphoma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Hodgkin lymphoma**, but the evidence is indirect: **10 retrieved clinical trials** and **19 publications** concern other lymphoid diseases, and none were specific to Hodgkin lymphoma.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, duvelisib inhibits PI3K-delta and PI3K-gamma. These enzymes support B-cell receptor signaling, cell survival, and the tumor microenvironment in lymphoid malignancies.

Classical Hodgkin lymphoma depends on PI3K/AKT signaling and an immune-rich microenvironment, so targeting this pathway is plausible. Duvelisib has shown activity in related lymphoid diseases (CLL/SLL, indolent non-Hodgkin lymphoma, and T-cell lymphoma).

However, the trials and publications retrieved concern indolent non-Hodgkin lymphoma, CLL and T-cell lymphoma, not Hodgkin lymphoma. The link is therefore indirect, and the prediction remains a research question rather than a supported indication. One preclinical study (PMID 29522278) examined duvelisib in Epstein-Barr virus-associated lymphoma cells, a setting that includes Hodgkin lymphoma, but it is not Hodgkin-specific clinical evidence.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction screen returned 100 interacting drugs; the first 20 are shown here. Interactions rated **Major** include clarithromycin, cisapride and eliglustat. Many **Moderate** interactions were also flagged, including corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone, budesonide), proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, esomeprazole, dexlansoprazole), cimetidine, aprepitant, pioglitazone, saxagliptin, granisetron and dolasetron. Combination use needs careful review.
- **Class toxicities**: PI3K inhibitors are known to carry serious toxicities (infections, colitis, pneumonitis, cutaneous reactions), as noted in the literature (e.g., PMID 33275709, 35899388).

For full warnings and contraindications, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The high prediction score is not backed by any Hodgkin lymphoma-specific trial or publication. The supporting evidence is from related lymphomas, several trials were withdrawn or terminated, and the only US license is cancelled. Known PI3K-class toxicities add to the caution.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Hodgkin lymphoma-specific preclinical or early clinical data (for example, a small Phase 1/2 study in relapsed or refractory classical Hodgkin lymphoma)
- Mechanism of action data and a clearer mechanistic link to Hodgkin lymphoma biology
- Package insert warnings and contraindications for a full safety review
- Clarification of the cancelled US market status and the availability of the product
- A risk-benefit assessment against existing Hodgkin lymphoma therapies, given the class toxicity profile
