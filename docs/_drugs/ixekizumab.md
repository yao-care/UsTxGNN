# Ixekizumab: From Ankylosing Spondylitis to Inflammatory Spondylopathy

## One-Sentence Summary

Ixekizumab is an IL-17A–blocking monoclonal antibody, originally used for ankylosing spondylitis and related spondyloarthropathy.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Inflammatory Spondylopathy**,
with **19 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction. Most of this evidence overlaps with the drug's original indications, so this is closer to a label-adjacent indication than true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the literature, ixekizumab is a high-affinity monoclonal antibody that neutralizes IL-17A, a cytokine considered a key driver of inflammation in spondyloarthritis.

Inflammatory spondylopathy overlaps substantially with axial spondyloarthritis and ankylosing spondylitis, which are the drug's original indications. Because IL-17A drives inflammation in these conditions, blocking it is a plausible way to treat the broader inflammatory spinal disease group. Multiple Phase 3 randomized trials in radiographic and non-radiographic axial spondyloarthritis (the COAST program) support this.

Other predictions for this drug (for example rheumatoid vasculitis, leukemias, coccyx hypermobility, Kummell disease and gingival fibromatosis) rest on the model score alone, with no supporting trials or literature. They are not recommended for further investment at this time.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction query returned 100 records. The most notable is a **major** interaction with **adalimumab**, which suggests avoiding combined use with other biologic immunomodulators. Moderate interactions include warfarin, apixaban, rivaroxaban, dicoumarol, tacrolimus, simvastatin, theophylline, aminophylline, nifedipine, fentanyl, hydrocodone, lidocaine and roflumilast. Several zinc salts have minor interactions. Interactions with a tetanus toxoid vaccine are also listed as moderate.
- **Class effects (from the literature)**: IL-17 inhibitors have been associated with paradoxical worsening of inflammatory bowel disease, and infection risk should be monitored.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications, as these were not available in the data compiled for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several completed Phase 3 randomized, placebo-controlled trials and a Phase 3 extension study support ixekizumab in axial spondyloarthritis, which is closely related to inflammatory spondylopathy. This is therefore a label-adjacent indication rather than a novel repurposing, and evidence is strong. The other nine predictions have no direct evidence and should stay on hold.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (currently missing and blocking safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Verification of US availability, since the only license on record is cancelled
- A clear definition of "inflammatory spondylopathy" against the already-studied axial spondyloarthritis populations, to confirm whether any real indication gap exists
- A monitoring plan for inflammatory bowel disease exacerbation and infections, and review of concomitant biologic use (for example adalimumab)
