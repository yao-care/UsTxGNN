## Modafinil: From Narcolepsy to Insomnia
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Modafinil is a wake-promoting medication originally used to treat narcolepsy and related excessive daytime sleepiness.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **insomnia**, but only **2 clinical trials** (one terminated with 2 participants, one small Phase 4 study) and literature that mostly addresses sleep-wake disorders rather than insomnia currently support this direction.
The mechanistic link is weak, because a wake-promoting drug could plausibly worsen insomnia.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Modafinil is generally understood as a wake-promoting agent that acts through dopamine transporter inhibition, with downstream effects on histaminergic, orexinergic and glutamatergic wake circuits. Its established role is to reduce excessive daytime sleepiness, not to induce or consolidate sleep.

The high TxGNN score most likely reflects network proximity between modafinil and sleep-wake disorders (narcolepsy, shift work disorder, sleep disturbance in neurological disease). It probably does not reflect a plausible therapeutic effect on insomnia itself.

Narcolepsy and insomnia are both sleep-wake disorders, but they lie at opposite ends of the arousal spectrum. A wake-promoting drug could worsen insomnia, so the mechanistic rationale is weak. A more realistic benefit would be on daytime sleepiness or on sleep/wake disturbances secondary to another condition, such as Parkinson's disease or neurological fatigue. One small trial of modafinil in primary insomnia, combined with cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia, targeted daytime functioning rather than sleep induction.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The predicted insomnia indication rests on a weak mechanistic link, because modafinil promotes wakefulness and may worsen insomnia. Direct clinical evidence is limited to one small Phase 4 study without reported results and one terminated study with 2 participants. The drug is marketed in the US with 7 valid licenses, and many of the knowledge-graph contraindications (cardiovascular, psychiatric) overlap with typical insomnia populations.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism-of-action data and a convincing rationale for a benefit in insomnia, or a reframing toward secondary sleep/wake disturbances (e.g., in Parkinson's disease)
- Published results of NCT00124384
- FDA package insert warnings and contraindications
- A properly powered randomized trial in a well-defined insomnia population, with safety monitoring for cardiovascular and psychiatric effects
