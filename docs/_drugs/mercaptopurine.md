## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Mercaptopurine is a purine antimetabolite: its thioguanine nucleotide metabolites are incorporated into DNA and RNA and inhibit de novo purine synthesis. This is a plausible vulnerability in rapidly dividing myeloid blasts.

Mercaptopurine is a long-established component of leukemia maintenance regimens. In myeloid disease it appears mainly as part of combinations:

- Oral maintenance with mercaptopurine and methotrexate in transplant-ineligible AML.
- ALL-like consolidation in pediatric AML.
- Maintenance in acute promyelocytic leukemia.

Because these are multi-agent regimens, the contribution of mercaptopurine alone cannot be isolated.

## Safety Considerations

- **Conditions of concern:** Conditions associated with mercaptopurine as a contraindication or caution include kidney disease, liver disease, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, cholestasis, hyperbilirubinemia (including Gilbert syndrome), thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, leukopenia, agranulocytosis, anemia, and hepatosplenic T-cell lymphoma. These are not taken from label text.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were found. Moderate-level ones include mesalazine, balsalazide, olsalazine, sulfasalazine and naltrexone. Levofloxacin is minor. Others, such as prednisone, metformin, omeprazole and morphine, have an unknown severity level.

Please refer to the package insert for the full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Mercaptopurine has a plausible mechanism and is used in myeloid leukemia regimens. Support comes from cohort and pilot studies of oral maintenance in AML, and from Phase 3 trials that test regimens rather than mercaptopurine itself. **To proceed, the following is needed:**

- Mechanism-of-action data and label warnings and contraindications.
- A myelosuppression monitoring plan, including TPMT/NUDT15 testing.
