## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, peppermint oil is a plant-derived essential oil rich in menthol and flavonoids. No approved indication is recorded, so the relationship to an original indication cannot be assessed.

Mechanistically, a link to blood pressure is plausible but unvalidated. Menthol may relax smooth muscle, possibly through calcium channel modulation. One physiological study in healthy volunteers (PMID 30070742) found that gastric cooling with menthol increased cardiac parasympathetic activity and lowered heart rate. These findings suggest a possible effect on blood pressure, but they do not show an effect on cardiovascular disease outcomes.

The evidence is also indirect. The available trial tested surrogate cardiometabolic markers in people with mild-to-moderate hypertension, not coronary events, stroke, or mortality. The high TxGNN score is a model prediction and should not be read as clinical proof.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (research question only)**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on one small, completed, non-phased trial and one related publication, both using surrogate hypertension endpoints. Evidence is at Level L4 and is not enough to support a move forward.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Published results from NCT05561543 and the 2026 placebo-controlled trial (PMID 42024666), including effect sizes on blood pressure
- Larger, adequately powered trials in hypertension, followed by studies on cardiovascular disease outcomes
- Mechanism of action data for peppermint oil, especially vascular and calcium channel effects
- Safety information from the FDA package insert (warnings, contraindications) and drug interaction data
- Clarification of the regulatory status and intended product type, since the cancelled authorizations are for topical liquids and patches, not oral formulations like those tested in the trials
