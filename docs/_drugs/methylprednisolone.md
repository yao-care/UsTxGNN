# Methylprednisolone: From Autoimmune Disease to Alopecia Areata

## One-Sentence Summary

Methylprednisolone is a potent glucocorticoid, originally used for autoimmune and inflammatory conditions such as Crohn disease, adrenocortical insufficiency and acute lymphoblastic leukemia.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **alopecia areata**,
with **1 clinical trial** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, methylprednisolone is a potent glucocorticoid that suppresses T-cell activation and pro-inflammatory cytokine signaling.

Alopecia areata is a T-cell-mediated autoimmune disease in which the immune privilege of the hair follicle collapses. Methylprednisolone is already used for autoimmune disease, so its immunosuppressive action is consistent with what is needed here. This plausibly explains the hair regrowth reported with pulse therapy.

The main limitations are relapse after withdrawal and cumulative steroid toxicity. Because the mechanistic link rests on general glucocorticoid pharmacology rather than drug-specific MOA data, it should be read as plausible rather than confirmed.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease contraindications (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: hypertension, epilepsy, hyperglycemia, congestive heart failure, herpes simplex virus keratitis, measles, urea cycle disorders (e.g., citrullinemia, hyperargininemia, carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I deficiency), and pituitary hormone deficiency conditions (e.g., hypopituitarism, Sheehan syndrome, Kallmann syndrome).
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found. The listed examples include **Major** interactions with bupropion and clarithromycin. **Moderate** interactions include antidiabetic agents (metformin, pioglitazone, acarbose, alogliptin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, canagliflozin, dulaglutide, albiglutide, saxagliptin, chlorpropamide), amphotericin B, aprepitant, acetylsalicylic acid and bisacodyl.
- In the literature, the main concerns for pulse therapy are cumulative steroid toxicity and high relapse rates after withdrawal.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
One completed Phase 4 trial (n=42) and many open-label and retrospective studies support methylprednisolone pulse therapy in severe alopecia areata. However, there are no confirmed Phase 3 RCTs, and relapse and steroid toxicity are significant limitations.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Controlled (ideally randomized) trial data comparing methylprednisolone with current options, including JAK inhibitors
- Package insert warnings and contraindications, which are currently missing
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- A relapse-management and cumulative steroid toxicity monitoring plan, with attention to hypertension, hyperglycemia and the interactions above
- Clarification of US regulatory status, since all 8 authorizations are listed as cancelled

**Other predicted indications:** Alopecia mucinosa, telogen effluvium, folliculitis decalvans, alopecia antibody deficiency, and several rare hereditary hair disorders (ranks 2–8) have no trials or supporting literature and should be on **Hold**. Alopecia universalis with onychodystrophy and vitiligo (rank 9) is an indirect extrapolation from alopecia areata and remains a research question. Idiopathic steroid-sensitive nephrotic syndrome (rank 10) has mostly review-level literature, and because glucocorticoid class therapy is already standard of care, the added value of methylprednisolone is undefined. It also remains a research question.
