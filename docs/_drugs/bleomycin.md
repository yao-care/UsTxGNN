# Bleomycin: From Lymphoma and Head and Neck Cancer to Reticulum Cell Sarcoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Bleomycin is a cytotoxic antibiotic originally used to treat lymphomas (including Hodgkin and non-Hodgkin lymphoma) and head and neck cancer.
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is **cauda equina neoplasm**, but the best-supported prediction is **Reticulum Cell Sarcoma** (an obsolete term for diffuse large B-cell or histiocytic non-Hodgkin lymphoma), which is supported by **11 clinical trials** and **20 publications**. This report focuses on that prediction because it is the only one backed by clinical trial data, though it largely overlaps with an existing indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general knowledge, bleomycin is a DNA strand-breaking cytotoxic antibiotic, which gives it a broad antineoplastic rationale.

Reticulum cell sarcoma is an outdated name for diffuse large B-cell or histiocytic non-Hodgkin lymphoma. Bleomycin is already used in non-Hodgkin lymphoma, so this is largely a re-labeling of an existing indication rather than true repurposing. Bleomycin-containing regimens (such as ProMACE-CytaBOM, MACOP-B, m-BACOD and ACVBP) have been tested in aggressive lymphomas, and a 1972 study of single-agent bleomycin included 22 patients with reticulum cell sarcoma.

A caveat applies: in the registered trials bleomycin is one component of multi-drug regimens, so its individual contribution cannot be isolated.

## Safety Considerations

- **Pulmonary toxicity**: Bleomycin-induced interstitial pneumonitis and pulmonary fibrosis are well documented in the literature compiled for this report, and risk may rise with age. Bleomycin lung toxicity may also increase the risk of radiation pneumonitis.
- **Hyperpyrexia**: A fatal acute hyperpyrexic reaction has been reported, even in a patient who had previously tolerated bleomycin; a test dose is sometimes recommended.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were recorded. The only one with a stated severity is levofloxacin (Minor). The others, such as pantoprazole, palonosetron, aprepitant, morphine, omeprazole, vancomycin, prednisone, hydrocortisone, clarithromycin and metoclopramide, have no severity rating available.

Formal label warnings and contraindications were not available; please refer to the package insert for these.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several completed Phase 3 trials and multiple Phase 2 trials test bleomycin-containing regimens in aggressive non-Hodgkin lymphoma, the modern equivalent of reticulum cell sarcoma. Bleomycin is already used in non-Hodgkin lymphoma, so this is largely an existing indication. However, no trial isolates bleomycin's own contribution, and all four US licenses are cancelled.

Other predictions are weaker: adult astrocytic tumour (L3, research question only, with early-phase or dated data), primary pulmonary lymphoma (L4, indirect evidence, research question), pulmonary blastoma (L4, case reports only, Hold), cauda equina neoplasm and fetal adenocarcinoma of the lung (L5, model prediction only, Hold).

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data and the FDA package insert warnings and contraindications
- Clarification of the current US regulatory status, since all licenses are cancelled
- A pulmonary toxicity monitoring plan (baseline and serial pulmonary function, caution in older patients and with prior or planned chest radiotherapy)
- Regimen-level comparisons that isolate bleomycin's contribution (for example, the CHVmP/BV versus CHVmP data)
