# Irinotecan: From Metastatic Colon Cancer and Pancreatic Adenocarcinoma to Female Breast Carcinoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Irinotecan is a topoisomerase I inhibitor chemotherapy originally used for metastatic colorectal cancer and pancreatic adenocarcinoma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **female breast carcinoma**, but direct evidence is thin: among **9 clinical trials** listed, only **1 small Phase 1 study (n=12)** tests irinotecan directly in breast cancer. The literature is dominated by studies of SN-38-based agents such as sacituzumab govitecan.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for irinotecan is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on established pharmacology, irinotecan is a prodrug converted to SN-38, a topoisomerase I inhibitor. SN-38 causes DNA single-strand breaks and replication-associated cytotoxicity.

This mechanism is not specific to one tumor type, so activity in breast carcinoma is biologically plausible. The strongest supporting evidence is indirect. Sacituzumab govitecan, an anti-Trop-2 antibody conjugated to SN-38, showed benefit in HR+/HER2- metastatic breast cancer (the TROPiCS-02 Phase III study design is among the publications retrieved). That supports the activity of the SN-38 payload in breast cancer but does not validate free irinotecan. Older reviews describe irinotecan and mitomycin as having only "marginal activity" in breast cancer as single agents, with a sequential combination proposed on preclinical synergy.

The high TxGNN score is a computational prediction and does not replace clinical evidence.

- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were found. The main one is **clarithromycin (Major)**. Levofloxacin is minor.

Official label warnings and contraindications were not available for this report. Please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is high, but the only trial testing irinotecan directly in breast cancer is a 12-patient Phase 1 study. Supportive data come mostly from SN-38 conjugates and liposomal or PEGylated formulations, which do not validate free irinotecan. Older reviews describe marginal single-agent activity. Official label safety data are also missing, and all US licenses are cancelled. At present this is a research question rather than a development candidate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Official FDA label warnings and contraindications, which block safety screening
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Review of the PHENOMENAL study (liposomal irinotecan in HER2-negative breast cancer with brain metastases) and related data to judge whether irinotecan formulations show breast cancer efficacy
- A defined breast cancer subtype and combination strategy, such as triple-negative disease or brain metastases
- Clarification of why US licenses are cancelled and which formulation would be pursued
