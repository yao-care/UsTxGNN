# Miconazole: From Antifungal Use to Acne

## One-Sentence Summary

Miconazole is a long-established azole antifungal, marketed in the US mainly as topical creams, sprays and powders.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **acne**, but the support is thin: **1 clinical trial** (a suspended combination-product study that does not isolate miconazole) and **4 publications**, only one of which examines an acne-related condition treated with an azole.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the record. Miconazole belongs to the imidazole class of antifungals, which inhibit fungal ergosterol synthesis (CYP51). Its efficacy against fungal skin infections is long established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to acne-related skin conditions.

The link to acne is plausible but indirect, for three reasons:
- Miconazole has shown in vitro activity against *Propionibacterium acnes* (*Cutibacterium acnes*), the bacterium implicated in acne vulgaris, and against some other Gram-positive bacteria. Azole antifungals as a class show similar activity (PMID 20045949).
- A review describes anti-inflammatory effects of miconazole on skin (PMID 18627330).
- Some acne-like eruptions are actually *Malassezia* (*Pityrosporum*) folliculitis, which responds to azoles. This subset is often misdiagnosed as acne vulgaris (PMID 8593718). Benefit here may not carry over to true acne vulgaris.

The very high TxGNN score is a model output, not clinical evidence. No dose, formulation or efficacy data for acne currently exist.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a model score and indirect evidence (in vitro anti-*C. acnes* activity and azole-responsive *Malassezia* folliculitis). The only registered trial is a suspended combination-product study that cannot support a miconazole-specific claim. This is best treated as a research question rather than a development candidate.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications, and detailed MOA data
- Clear separation of acne vulgaris from *Malassezia* folliculitis in any future study
- A controlled clinical study of miconazole in acne vulgaris, with defined formulation, dose and endpoints
- Confirmation that the miconazole component itself, not the combination partners, drives any observed effect
