# Clomipramine: From Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder to Anxiety Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Clomipramine is a tricyclic antidepressant originally used to treat obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **anxiety disorder**,
with **8 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently linked to this prediction. However, most trials are in OCD rather than in anxiety disorders directly.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, clomipramine is a potent serotonin reuptake inhibitor, and its active metabolite, desmethylclomipramine, inhibits norepinephrine reuptake. This serotonergic and noradrenergic modulation underlies its effect in OCD.

OCD is closely related to the anxiety disorders, and the same neurotransmitter systems are involved in panic disorder and agoraphobia. Network meta-analyses and the WFSBP guidelines list clomipramine among effective drugs for panic disorder. Several older controlled studies in panic disorder and agoraphobia also support this (for example, comparisons with imipramine, diazepam and paroxetine, and a dose-finding study in which about 70% of patients reached remission).

**Caveat:** most of the retrieved trials are in OCD or OCD augmentation settings, so direct randomised evidence in anxiety disorders is largely indirect. The Evidence Level of L1 is the grade assigned in the compiled data. It rests on a Phase 3 trial that did not test clomipramine as the studied agent, so it should be read cautiously.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications suggested by the knowledge graph (not label text):** epilepsy, manic bipolar disorder, liver disease, gallbladder disease, substance abuse/dependence, malignant hyperthermia, pheochromocytoma, glaucoma, hyperthyroidism/Graves disease/thyrotoxicosis, neuroblastoma, and several cancers (duodenum, small intestine, heart, adrenal gland).
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were recorded. Major-level ones among those listed include epinephrine, bupropion, lorcaserin, potassium citrate and cisapride. Moderate ones include famotidine, cimetidine, clarithromycin, morphine, acetylsalicylic acid, and several anticholinergics (atropine, hyoscyamine, glycopyrronium, clidinium).
- **Literature-reported adverse effects:** seizure risk, anticholinergic effects, cardiac effects, sexual dysfunction, rare pancytopenia, and clomipramine-induced tics in a case report.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications, which were not available in the data compiled for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Clomipramine has a plausible serotonergic mechanism and supportive older studies, guidelines and meta-analyses in panic disorder and agoraphobia. However, direct randomised evidence for anxiety disorders is limited, its tolerability profile is demanding, and all US licenses are cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- A dedicated randomised trial in a defined anxiety disorder (for example, panic disorder)
- A safety monitoring plan covering cardiac, seizure and anticholinergic risks, and a drug interaction review
- Clarification of practical availability, given that the US market status is cancelled only
