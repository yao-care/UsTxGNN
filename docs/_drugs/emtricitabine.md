## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the record. Based on known class information, emtricitabine is a cytidine nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor. Its triphosphate metabolite competes with dCTP and terminates the growing proviral DNA chain.

SIV's reverse transcriptase is closely related to that of HIV-1. A drug that blocks HIV-1 replication is therefore expected to act on SIV as well. The macaque studies below support this: emtricitabine, usually combined with tenofovir, has been tested extensively against SIV and SHIV (a chimeric SIV/HIV virus) in prophylaxis and treatment models.

SIV infects non-human primates, so this prediction is a preclinical model signal. It is not a new human repurposing indication. Emtricitabine's human counterpart, HIV-1, is already a labelled use, and the record's empty indication field does not capture it.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible and macaque studies support it, but the evidence is preclinical only (L4). Both retrieved trials are HIV-1 studies with no emtricitabine efficacy data in SIV. SIV is a non-human primate pathogen, so this is not a human repurposing indication.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A corrected original-indication record (HIV-1 infection) so the prediction is not read as a new human use
- A decision on whether SIV or SHIV macaque models are a meaningful development target, or whether this candidate should be closed as a model-organism artifact
- Direct SIV-specific studies of emtricitabine, since most of the current literature uses SHIV
