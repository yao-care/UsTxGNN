## Elagolix: From Endometriosis to Amenorrhea
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Elagolix is an oral GnRH receptor antagonist, originally used to treat endometriosis (pain associated with the disease).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Amenorrhea**, but there are currently **0 registered clinical trials** and **4 publications**, none of which evaluate elagolix as a treatment for amenorrhea itself.
Amenorrhea appears to be an expected pharmacological effect of the drug rather than a true new therapeutic indication.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, elagolix is an oral GnRH receptor antagonist. It suppresses pituitary release of LH and FSH, which lowers ovarian production of estradiol and progesterone.

This hypoestrogenic state suppresses endometrial proliferation and often reduces or stops menstrual bleeding. Amenorrhea is therefore a plausible pharmacodynamic effect, and it has been observed as a secondary outcome in uterine fibroid and heavy menstrual bleeding studies. The very high TxGNN score most likely reflects this mechanistic proximity.

However, amenorrhea is generally a symptom of an underlying condition, not a disease to be treated with a drug. Inducing it would be an intended or incidental effect of therapy, not a repurposing indication in its own right. The retrieved literature does not evaluate elagolix as a treatment for amenorrhea. The prediction should therefore be interpreted with caution.
