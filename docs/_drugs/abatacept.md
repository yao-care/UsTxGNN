## One-Sentence Summary

Abatacept is a biologic that blocks T-cell co-stimulation and is already marketed in the United States. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **ankylosing spondylitis**. Support is thin: **1 small open-label Phase 2 pilot trial** and **1 open-label pilot publication** study the disease directly. Several reviews describe abatacept as not effective in axial spondyloarthritis.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Abatacept is known as a T-cell co-stimulation blocker. It interrupts the activation of T cells, a process that drives several immune-mediated joint diseases. Ankylosing spondylitis is also an immune-mediated inflammatory arthritis, so a T-cell-directed therapy is mechanistically plausible.

The plausibility is limited. Axial spondyloarthritis is driven mainly by TNF and IL-17/IL-23 pathways, not by T-cell co-stimulation. A 2016 review (PMID 27856659) states that biologics targeting IL-1, IL-6 and T cells (abatacept) are not effective in axial spondyloarthritis, whereas TNF blockers are highly effective. The high model score is therefore not supported by the clinical literature.

Because the evidence is small, uncontrolled and mostly from reviews, the evidence level is L3. The only studies that address the disease directly are the open-label pilot trial and its publication. No controlled trial of abatacept in ankylosing spondylitis was found.

## Safety Considerations

Warning and contraindication text is not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the package insert for these.

**Drug Interactions**: 73 interactions were recorded. The main ones are:
- **Major**: adalimumab, baricitinib, certolizumab pegol, and live BCG vaccine (Tice strain).
- **Moderate**: roflumilast, alefacept, alemtuzumab, anakinra, azathioprine, canakinumab, and several live vaccines (adenovirus type 4, cholera, *Candida albicans*, *Bifidobacterium longum infantis*, anthrax vaccine).
- **Minor**: zinc salts (acetate, gluconate, sulfate, chloride).

In practice, combining abatacept with other biologic immunosuppressants or live vaccines needs particular caution.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only direct evidence is a small, uncontrolled pilot trial with unknown status and no available results. Reviews state that T-cell co-stimulation blockade is not effective in axial spondyloarthritis, while TNF and IL-17 inhibitors are established options. The high model score alone does not justify advancing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Published results of NCT00558506 and the 2011 pilot study, including response rates and safety.
- A controlled trial showing efficacy against placebo or a TNF inhibitor in ankylosing spondylitis.
- Mechanism-of-action data and a rationale for why T-cell co-stimulation blockade would work in axial disease.
- Package insert warnings and contraindications, and a monitoring plan for infection risk and interacting drugs.
