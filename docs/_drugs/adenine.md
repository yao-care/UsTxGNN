## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available for adenine. It is a purine base involved in nucleotide metabolism and adenosine signaling. Preclinical work has reported that adenosine receptors (for example A2A) can influence osteoblast and osteoclast activity, which is a possible but indirect route to bone effects.

This link is speculative. No established mechanism connects adenine to drug-induced osteoporosis, and the high TxGNN score is a computational prediction, not evidence of benefit. In rodent models, high-dose adenine is used to induce chronic kidney disease, which can itself cause mineral and bone disorder. This points to a potential safety concern rather than a benefit signal.
