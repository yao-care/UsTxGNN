## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on known information, ofatumumab is a human anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody. Its efficacy in CD20-positive B-cell malignancies such as chronic lymphocytic leukemia has been established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to follicular lymphoma.

Follicular lymphoma cells express CD20, the antibody's target. Ofatumumab binds a distinct, membrane-proximal epitope of CD20. It can trigger complement-dependent cytotoxicity and antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity, including in rituximab-refractory disease. Several Phase 2 trials, some randomized, and a multicenter monotherapy study in rituximab-refractory follicular lymphoma support this activity.

Important caveats: no Phase 3 trial in follicular lymphoma was identified. The one Phase 3 study listed (NCT01077518, ofatumumab plus bendamustine in rituximab-unresponsive indolent NHL) was terminated and no results were compiled. The US market status is "Cancelled only" for the listed product, which limits practical access.

## Safety Considerations

Representative Major interactions include deferiprone, samarium (153Sm) lexidronam, adalimumab, baricitinib, certolizumab pegol, cladribine and clozapine. Moderate interactions include alemtuzumab, azathioprine, anakinra, zidovudine and several vaccines (e.g., tetanus toxoid, anthrax, live cholera). Many of these involve additive immunosuppression or myelosuppression, or a reduced vaccine response.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several completed Phase 2 trials, including randomized ones and a multicenter study in rituximab-refractory disease, show activity of ofatumumab in follicular lymphoma, and the CD20 target is well established. However, no completed Phase 3 trial exists in this setting, single-agent response in rituximab-refractory disease was modest (about 10-13%), and the listed US license is cancelled. The drug is also already established in CLL/SLL, which is effectively its on-label use. The other predictions are weaker: CLL/SLL subtypes and the susceptibility entity have no subtype-specific data, and metastatic neoplasm rests on a single 10-patient melanoma pilot. Malignant spiradenoma and Langerhans cell histiocytosis lack a biological rationale and appear to be model artifacts.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- FDA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking data gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A review of the terminated Phase 3 trial NCT01077518 and any available results
- Clarification of access: a currently marketed US product and formulation for an oncology indication (the listed KESIMPTA license is cancelled)
- A comparison against current standard follicular lymphoma regimens (e.g., rituximab- or obinutuzumab-based)
- A safety monitoring plan, including infection and hepatitis B screening, and an interaction-management plan for concurrent immunosuppressants
