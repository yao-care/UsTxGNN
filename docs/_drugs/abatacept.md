# Abatacept: From Rheumatoid Arthritis to Rheumatoid Vasculitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Abatacept is a T-cell co-stimulation blocker (CTLA4-Ig fusion protein) originally used to treat rheumatoid arthritis, juvenile idiopathic arthritis and spondyloarthropathy.
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is **rheumatoid vasculitis**, but this direction is supported only by **1 unrelated clinical trial** and case-level reports, so the evidence is weak.
The data compiled for this report show the strongest support for the **spondyloarthritis-spectrum (psoriatic arthritis) subset** and for **polyarticular juvenile arthritis**, which overlaps an existing indication and is not true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the compiled data. Based on known pharmacology, abatacept is a CTLA4-Ig fusion protein that binds CD80/86 and blocks the CD28 co-stimulation signal needed for T-cell activation. Its efficacy in rheumatoid arthritis is established.

Rheumatoid vasculitis is an extra-articular complication of rheumatoid disease, so dampening T-cell-driven inflammation is plausible. However, the clinical signal is mixed. One case report describes abatacept as a therapeutic option (PMID 29930884) and another reports a rapid response (PMID 22124545). A different report describes new-onset rheumatoid vasculitis *during* abatacept therapy that improved after rituximab (PMID 27052429), which is a counter-signal.

Other ranked predictions give a more varied picture:

- **Inflammatory spondylopathy (rank 4):** It includes psoriatic arthritis, where a completed Phase 3 placebo-controlled trial exists. Evidence for axial disease such as ankylosing spondylitis is weak, and reviews describe abatacept as not effective there.
- **Polyarticular juvenile rheumatoid arthritis (rank 6):** Several completed Phase 3 trials exist, but this overlaps an established indication.
- **Hypermobility of coccyx, Kummell disease, brachydactyly-syndactyly syndrome, colobomatous microphthalmia-rhizomelic dysplasia syndrome:** No trials or literature, and no evident immune mechanism. These are computational predictions only.
- **Vertebral disease and spondyloarthropathy susceptibility:** Terms too non-specific, and the supporting literature does not address these entities directly.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions:** 73 interactions were recorded. Notable **Major** interactions include:
  - Adalimumab
  - Baricitinib
  - Certolizumab pegol
  - BCG live vaccine
- **Moderate interactions** include other immunosuppressive and biologic agents (alefacept, alemtuzumab, anakinra, azathioprine, canakinumab, roflumilast) and several live or attenuated vaccines.
- **Minor interactions** include zinc salts.
- The literature also flags infection risk (including tuberculosis and herpes zoster), and a meta-analysis on malignancy risk exists (PMID 39992258).

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications, as this data were not available for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top prediction, rheumatoid vasculitis, rests on isolated case reports (including a counter-signal) and one unrelated trial. The model score is very high, but the evidence level is only L4. The stronger signals lie elsewhere. Psoriatic arthritis (within inflammatory spondylopathy) has Phase 3 support and could proceed with guardrails, and polyarticular JIA is already an established use.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data and the FDA package insert (warnings and contraindications), which are currently missing
- Verification of the indication for NCT01860976 and NCT04610476 (truncated titles)
- Confirmation of the current US marketing status of abatacept
- For rheumatoid vasculitis, a prospective or well-designed observational study with a comparator, such as rituximab
- For spondyloarthritis, a clear separation of psoriatic arthritis from axial disease, where efficacy is not supported
