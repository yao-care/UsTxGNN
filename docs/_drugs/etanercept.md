## Etanercept: From Rheumatoid Arthritis to Rheumatoid Vasculitis
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Etanercept is a TNF-blocking fusion protein, originally used for inflammatory arthritis such as rheumatoid arthritis, psoriatic arthritis, ankylosing spondylitis and juvenile idiopathic arthritis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **rheumatoid vasculitis**, but only **1 loosely related clinical trial** and **20 publications** were found, and most of that literature is about etanercept *causing* vasculitis, not treating it.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the published literature, etanercept is a soluble TNF receptor (p75)–Fc fusion protein that binds TNF-alpha and blocks its activity. Its efficacy in rheumatoid arthritis is well established, and it is a mainstay of TNF-targeted therapy.

Rheumatoid vasculitis is a severe extra-articular complication of rheumatoid arthritis. TNF-alpha is central to the inflammation in both conditions, which gives a plausible mechanistic link.

There are important counter-signals, however:

- Multiple reports describe etanercept-associated cutaneous vasculitis, large-vessel vasculitis and nephropathy. TNF blockade may therefore paradoxically trigger vasculitis in some patients.
- Evidence for TNF-alpha blockade in ANCA-associated vasculitis is unfavorable or inconclusive.
- The only systematic review of biologics in rheumatoid vasculitis covers the drug class and is not specific to etanercept.

## Safety Considerations

- **Key Warnings and Contraindications**: Label-based warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the package insert.
- **Safety signals from the literature**: Etanercept has been associated with cutaneous vasculitis, large-vessel vasculitis, lupus-like events and nephropathy. It has also been linked to infection risks (such as disseminated herpes zoster, whose presentation can mimic vasculitis). This is particularly relevant when the target condition is itself a vasculitis.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were recorded. Main examples:
  - **Major**: corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, prednisone, prednisolone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, triamcinolone, budesonide), deferiprone, and radiopharmaceutical or radioimmunotherapy agents (iobenguane I-131, ibritumomab tiuxetan, tositumomab).
  - **Moderate**: metronidazole, tinidazole, rosuvastatin, simvastatin.
  - **Minor**: zinc salts (sulfate, acetate, gluconate).

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic link is plausible, since TNF-alpha drives both rheumatoid arthritis and its vasculitic complication. However, there is no direct clinical evidence for etanercept in rheumatoid vasculitis (L4). The literature repeatedly reports etanercept-induced vasculitis, and the one registered trial is unrelated to efficacy. The US license for ENBREL is also listed as cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Etanercept-specific clinical data in rheumatoid vasculitis, such as case series, cohort studies or a controlled trial, rather than class-level biologic data
- A risk-benefit analysis addressing the paradoxical vasculitis signal
- Mechanism-of-action data and label-based warnings and contraindications
- Clarification of the current US regulatory and commercial status of etanercept products
- Comparison against established rheumatoid vasculitis treatments (corticosteroids and immunosuppressants)
