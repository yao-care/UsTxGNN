## Eptinezumab: From Migraine Disorder to Migraine with Brainstem Aura
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Eptinezumab is an anti-CGRP monoclonal antibody originally used for migraine prevention.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine with brainstem aura**,
but currently there are **0 registered clinical trials** and **10 publications** (mostly indirect evidence: post hoc analyses, reviews, and case reports) supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, eptinezumab is an intravenously administered monoclonal antibody that targets calcitonin gene-related peptide (CGRP), a key mediator of the trigeminal migraine cascade. Its efficacy in migraine prevention has been established.

Migraine with brainstem aura is a subtype of migraine, so the CGRP pathway is plausibly relevant. This likely explains the very high TxGNN score. Some reports also suggest that anti-CGRP antibodies may help with aura symptoms, although the mechanism by which they might act on aura, which is believed to be linked to cortical spreading depression, is not well understood.

There are caveats. A randomized trial of PACAP38-induced migraine attacks found that these attacks occur independently of CGRP signaling, which suggests that some migraine pathways would not be addressed by CGRP blockade. In addition, the available aura-related evidence concerns general self-reported aura, not brainstem aura specifically.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

Two recent real-world cohort studies examined CGRP inhibitors for glaucoma risk and for cerebrovascular events (ischemic stroke/TIA) compared with onabotulinumtoxinA. These are observational, and their findings should be reviewed in full before drawing conclusions. No drug interaction data were found.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high, and the CGRP mechanism is plausible for a migraine subtype. However, there are no registered trials for brainstem aura, the supporting literature is indirect (general aura subgroup, reviews, case reports), and a PACAP38 trial suggests some attacks are CGRP-independent. The evidence level is L4, so this is best treated as a research question. The US regulatory record is also listed as cancelled only.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Verify the current US regulatory status of Vyepti (BLA761119), as the data show it as cancelled
- Obtain FDA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Obtain detailed mechanism of action data
- Brainstem aura-specific evidence, such as subgroup analyses or a dedicated trial
- Review of the cerebrovascular and glaucoma safety signals from the recent cohort studies
