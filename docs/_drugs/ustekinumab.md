# Ustekinumab: From Psoriasis to Dermatitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Ustekinumab is an IL-12/23 antagonist originally used for psoriasis, psoriatic arthritis and parapsoriasis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **dermatitis**, a broad term that in the available trials mostly means atopic dermatitis.
Currently **6 clinical trials** (3 directly relevant) and **20 publications** are linked to this direction, but the controlled data suggest limited efficacy.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Ustekinumab blocks the shared p40 subunit of IL-12 and IL-23, which suppresses Th1 and Th17 signaling. This is the basis for its use in psoriasis, a Th1/Th17-driven skin disease.

Some chronic inflammatory skin disease, including a subset of atopic dermatitis, involves these pathways, so the prediction has a biological basis. However, atopic dermatitis is predominantly Th2-driven, so the mechanistic fit is only partial. The Phase 2 data also appear to show limited efficacy.

The very high TxGNN score should be read with caution. "Dermatitis" covers heterogeneous conditions (atopic, contact and others), so the score alone does not establish a specific indication. Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank were not available for this report; the description above is based on known pharmacology.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Two Phase 2 randomized, placebo-controlled studies exist in atopic dermatitis (evidence level L2), but the mechanism fits only partially because atopic dermatitis is mainly Th2-driven, and efficacy in these studies appears limited. "Dermatitis" is also too broad to define a specific indication. All US licenses are listed as cancelled, and the package-insert safety data are missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Full results (primary endpoints, effect size) of NCT01945086 and NCT01806662, plus confirmation of the study populations
- A narrower target indication (e.g., a specific dermatitis subtype or Th1/Th17-high patient subset)
- FDA package insert warnings and contraindications
- Verification of current US market and licensing status
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Consideration of the parapsoriasis-related predictions (pityriasis lichenoides, acute lichenoid pityriasis) as better mechanistic fits, although they currently have no clinical evidence
