# Polymyxin B: From Gram-Negative Antibacterial Use to Bronchitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Polymyxin B is a cationic lipopeptide antibiotic that acts against Gram-negative bacteria and is marketed in the US as injectable powder.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **bronchitis**, mainly bacterial tracheobronchitis.
Support comes from **0 registered clinical trials** and **14 publications**. Most are small or older cohort studies, and several describe bronchospasm risk with inhaled use.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Polymyxin B disrupts the outer membrane of Gram-negative bacteria, including *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* and multidrug-resistant strains. Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the source record, and the approved indication text is blank in the US listings. The mechanistic reasoning below therefore rests on the drug's known antibacterial class.

The link to bronchitis is plausible only for bacterial disease. Publications describe inhaled or adjunctive polymyxin B for tracheobronchitis and pneumonia caused by multidrug-resistant Gram-negative bacilli. There is no direct support for viral or other non-infectious bronchitis.

The literature also shows a safety concern. Inhaled polymyxin B triggers bronchospasm and has been used as a non-specific bronchial provocation agent in asthma and chronic bronchitis. It has also been used to induce eosinophilic bronchitis in animal models. Any respiratory use would need close attention to airway tolerability.

## Safety Considerations

- **Respiratory risk (from literature)**: Inhaled polymyxin B can cause bronchospasm and is used experimentally to provoke airway reactivity in asthma and chronic bronchitis.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings, contraindications and drug interactions.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high (99.87%), but there are no registered trials, and the literature is mostly small or older cohort studies. Several papers describe bronchospasm risk with inhaled use, and the benefit applies at most to bacterial tracheobronchitis.

Note: among the model's other predictions, **conjunctivitis** (rank 3) has much stronger support. It has a Phase 3 and a Phase 4 trial plus several randomized comparisons of polymyxin B/trimethoprim in bacterial conjunctivitis. That evidence is for topical combination products, and it may reflect an already established use rather than true repurposing, so check it against the label.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications
- Detailed mechanism of action data (MOA)
- Original approved indication text from the US labels
- A prospective trial or controlled data on inhaled polymyxin B in bacterial tracheobronchitis, with respiratory safety monitoring
- A route and formulation plan, since only injectable products are currently listed
