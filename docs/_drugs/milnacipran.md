# Milnacipran: From Fibromyalgia to Migraine Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Milnacipran is a serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor (SNRI) originally used to treat fibromyalgia, and the knowledge graph also links it to myofascial pain syndrome.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine disorder**, supported by **2 clinical trials** (1 completed placebo-controlled trial, 1 open-label study) and **5 publications**, including one prospective open-label study.
The evidence is early-stage: no controlled efficacy results are available yet.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, milnacipran belongs to the SNRI class. Its efficacy in fibromyalgia has been established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to migraine.

Other drugs with overlapping monoaminergic activity, such as venlafaxine and amitriptyline, are used for migraine prophylaxis. Descending serotonergic and noradrenergic pathways modulate pain and are plausibly relevant to migraine and central sensitization. Fibromyalgia and myofascial pain also involve central pain processing, which gives the two indications a shared biological theme.

This link relies on the class-level SNRI mechanism rather than drug-specific mechanism data, so it should be viewed as a hypothesis.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold (treat as a research question)**

**Rationale:**
The prediction score is very high and the SNRI mechanism is plausible, but the clinical evidence consists of one small open-label pilot study and one small placebo-controlled trial with no available results. There are no completed Phase 2/3 efficacy trials. The only US NDA is cancelled, and the knowledge graph flags several cardiovascular contraindications that matter in a migraine population.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results of the completed placebo-controlled trial NCT01393522 (publication or registry results)
- An adequately powered Phase 2 randomized controlled trial in migraine prevention
- Drug-specific mechanism of action data
- FDA package insert warnings and contraindications, to complete the safety screening
- A cardiovascular and blood pressure safety assessment for migraine patients
- Consolidation of the "migraine with or without aura, susceptibility to" entry into the main migraine disorder assessment, since it has no distinct evidence
