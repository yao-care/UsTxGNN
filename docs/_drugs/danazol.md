# Danazol: From Endometriosis to Amenorrhea (Menstrual Suppression)

## One-Sentence Summary

Danazol is a synthetic androgen with antigonadotropic activity, originally used to treat endometriosis and benign fibrocystic breast disease.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **Amenorrhea** (therapeutic menstrual suppression), with **0 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications** (mostly older reviews and clinical reports, plus one 2024 cohort study) currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the published literature, danazol is a synthetic steroid derived from 17α-ethinyltestosterone. It suppresses pituitary gonadotropins (LH/FSH) and ovarian steroid production, which leads to endometrial atrophy and amenorrhea.

This is the same mechanism that underlies its use in endometriosis, a hormone-dependent disease in which lesions regress during states of ovarian down-regulation such as amenorrhea. The relationship between the original and new indication is therefore close: in endometriosis, amenorrhea is part of how the drug works, and the prediction extends that effect to menstrual suppression as a goal in its own right.

Amenorrhea here is a therapeutic effect, not a disease to be cured. A 2024 retrospective multi-site cohort study examined danazol for menstrual suppression in transgender and nonbinary individuals, and older reviews describe its use for inducing amenorrhea and for abnormal uterine bleeding. Androgenic side effects and teratogenicity require guardrails.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related conditions (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: epilepsy, porphyria (including inherited porphyria and porphyrin metabolism disease), kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, female breast carcinoma, migraine, and familial combined hyperlipidemia.
- **Drug Interactions** (100 interactions recorded; examples):
  - **Major**: Cisapride.
  - **Moderate**: antidiabetic agents (acarbose, glimepiride, glipizide, glyburide, chlorpropamide, insulin, canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, exenatide, dulaglutide), budesonide, and eliglustat.
- **Literature-reported concerns**: androgenic effects (pigmentation of vellus hairs, voice changes) and teratogenicity. One case report describes rhabdomyolysis and pancreatitis when danazol 600 mg/day was combined with lovastatin 40 mg/day.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism of menstrual suppression is the same one that underlies danazol's established use in endometriosis, and a 2024 cohort study supports its practical use. However, there are no registered trials, much of the literature is old, and all US authorizations are cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (currently a blocking data gap)
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- Prospective or controlled data on menstrual suppression, including comparison with standard options such as continuous hormonal contraception and GnRH analogues
- Safety plan covering androgenic effects, teratogenicity (pregnancy exclusion and contraception), liver function, lipids, and interacting drugs
- Clarification of product availability, since all US authorizations are cancelled
