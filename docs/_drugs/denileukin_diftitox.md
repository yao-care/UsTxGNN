# Denileukin Diftitox: From Cutaneous T-Cell Lymphoma to Metastatic Neoplasm

## One-Sentence Summary

Denileukin diftitox is an IL-2/diphtheria toxin fusion protein, originally used to treat primary cutaneous T-cell lymphoma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **metastatic neoplasm** (the highest-ranked prediction with supporting studies), with **8 clinical trials** and **7 publications** currently available. These are mostly small, early-phase studies that use the drug as an immune-modulating component rather than as a proven monotherapy.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. From what is known, denileukin diftitox is a fusion protein that joins interleukin-2 (IL-2) with diphtheria toxin. It binds cells that express the IL-2 receptor (CD25) and delivers the toxin into them. Its efficacy in cutaneous T-cell lymphoma, where malignant cells express CD25, has been established.

In solid tumours, the rationale is different. Instead of killing the cancer cells directly, the drug is studied as a way to deplete CD25-expressing regulatory T cells (Tregs). Tregs suppress the immune attack on tumours, so removing them may strengthen the effect of vaccines, adoptive T-cell or NK-cell transfer, and high-dose IL-2. Some studies report transient Treg depletion and regression of melanoma metastases (PMID 18334033, 16308572).

The results are inconsistent, however. One study found that the fusion protein failed to eliminate Tregs in melanoma patients (PMID 16224276). Efficacy as monotherapy is unproven, and "metastatic neoplasm" is a heterogeneous category, so the prediction is best viewed as a research question rather than an established use.

## Safety Considerations

**Drug Interactions:** The interaction screening returned 88 interacting drugs. Examples include:
- **Major:** adalimumab, baricitinib, certolizumab pegol, cladribine
- **Moderate:** roflumilast, chloramphenicol, zidovudine, azathioprine, alemtuzumab, ganciclovir, anakinra, canakinumab, and several vaccines (tetanus toxoid, anthrax, live cholera)
- **Minor:** levofloxacin, ciprofloxacin, gemifloxacin

Many of the major and moderate interactions involve immunosuppressive or immunomodulating agents and live vaccines. Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only supporting evidence for metastatic neoplasm is early-phase (mostly Phase 1/2) studies where the drug serves as a Treg-depleting adjunct, with inconsistent results and no Phase 3 trial. The three top-scoring predictions have no study evidence at all, and the product's US market status is "Cancelled only". The package insert safety information is also missing, so safety screening cannot proceed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- FDA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap)
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank)
- Published results from the completed melanoma and renal cell carcinoma Phase 2 trials (NCT00299689, NCT00082914)
- A narrower, well-defined target indication instead of the broad "metastatic neoplasm" category
- Clarification of the current US market and supply status of LYMPHIR
