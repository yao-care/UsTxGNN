## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the compiled data. Based on known pharmacology, ifosfamide is a prodrug activated in the liver by cytochrome P450 enzymes. Its active metabolite, isophosphoramide mustard, cross-links DNA and induces apoptosis in rapidly dividing tumour cells.

Breast cancer is a chemotherapy-responsive solid tumour, and alkylating agents are already used against it. Ifosfamide is an analogue of cyclophosphamide, which is a component of standard breast cancer regimens. Published studies report single-agent response rates of roughly 7–30% in pretreated patients. Phase 2 combination studies with docetaxel, vinorelbine, mitoxantrone, etoposide, paclitaxel and doxorubicin report higher response rates.

The supporting evidence is early-phase. It also includes literature on rarer breast tumour types such as metaplastic breast cancer and malignant phyllodes tumours.

## Safety Considerations

- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (from TxGNN knowledge-graph drug-disease edges, not label text): kidney disease, liver disease, gallbladder disease, pyoureter, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, anemia (including macrocytic anemia), hyperbilirubinemia (including hereditary forms and Gilbert syndrome), potassium deficiency, renal tubular acidosis, and Fanconi renotubular syndrome.
- **Drug interactions**: 100 interactions were found, and the first 20 returned are mostly Moderate. Examples include amphotericin B (including the lipid complex), kanamycin and neomycin (nephrotoxic or aminoglycoside agents), aprepitant, bupropion, morphine, metoclopramide, dronabinol, nabilone and scopolamine. The sulfonylurea and glinide antidiabetics (chlorpropamide, glimepiride, glipizide, glyburide, tolazamide, acetohexamide, nateglinide, repaglinide) are also listed as Moderate. Levofloxacin is listed as Minor.
- **Related literature signals**: therapy-related myeloid neoplasms after ifosfamide-containing regimens, and a case report of thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura after a paclitaxel-ifosfamide-cisplatin regimen.

Please refer to the package insert for official warnings and contraindications, which were not available in the compiled data.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The breast cancer evidence consists only of Phase 1/2 trials (several terminated or of unknown status) and small single-arm studies, with no Phase 3 RCT. All 3 US licenses are valid, but the label indications and warnings are not available. The compiled assessment rates this as a research question rather than a candidate ready to advance.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The current FDA package insert (indications, warnings, contraindications), since the safety data gap blocks safety screening
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- A comparison with current standard breast cancer regimens, including a randomized trial or a systematic review of ifosfamide combinations
- Clarification of the cancelled US status and whether any marketed ifosfamide product is available
- A safety monitoring plan covering urotoxicity (mesna), nephrotoxicity, neurotoxicity, myelosuppression and secondary myeloid neoplasm risk

---

**Note on other predictions:** Among the other predicted indications, only **rhabdomyosarcoma** has strong support (L1, with Phase 3 trials in soft tissue sarcoma). Ifosfamide is already used there as established or off-label therapy, so it is not a novel repurposing. The myelodysplastic syndrome, 5q deletion, refractory cytopenia, aregenerative anemia and monocytic leukemia predictions are likely artefacts. Ifosfamide is a known cause of therapy-related myeloid neoplasms and cytopenias, so these represent safety concerns, not treatment opportunities.
