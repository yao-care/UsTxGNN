# Bupropion: From Depression to Attention Deficit-Hyperactivity Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Bupropion is a norepinephrine-dopamine reuptake inhibitor. Published reviews describe it as used for depression and smoking cessation (the license data supplied here do not state an approved indication).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)**, with **8 registered trials (5 directly ADHD-related)** and **20 publications** on this direction, including one completed Phase 3 placebo-controlled trial and a Cochrane review.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data were not supplied. A 2023 review (PMID 37405312) describes bupropion as having no serotonergic activity and inhibiting the reuptake of norepinephrine and dopamine. These are the same catecholamine pathways targeted by established ADHD drugs. Stimulants such as methylphenidate and amphetamines increase dopamine and norepinephrine, and atomoxetine acts on norepinephrine.

Depression and ADHD often occur together, and adults with ADHD who cannot use or do not respond to stimulants need non-stimulant options. Reviews list bupropion among the non-stimulant choices (PMIDs 26601963, 38915262). The very high TxGNN score is consistent with this mechanism and with the existing clinical literature.

## Safety Considerations

Package insert warnings, contraindications and interaction data were not available. Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

Two points from the supplied material are worth noting. The candidate notes state that bupropion lowers the seizure threshold. A 2025 network meta-analysis (PMID 40203844) compares the cardiovascular safety of ADHD drugs, and it should be reviewed before any use in ADHD.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible and the score is very high. One completed Phase 3 placebo-controlled trial, several smaller ADHD studies, and a Cochrane review with multiple network meta-analyses support it. Confirmed evidence does not reach L1, and the safety data are missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications, which are blocking for safety screening
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation of the formulation and the efficacy results of NCT00048360
- Confirmation of whether NCT00061087 was randomized and controlled, to decide between L1 and L2
- Review of the Cochrane and network meta-analysis results for bupropion's effect size and tolerability versus stimulants and atomoxetine
- A seizure-risk and cardiovascular monitoring plan
- Attention to substance-use comorbidity, since most of the trials enrolled this population

**Other predicted candidates:** "ADHD, inattentive type" (rank 2) inherits the same rationale but has no subtype-specific trials (L4, research question). Ranks 3 to 10 have no plausible mechanism or no supporting evidence and should stay on Hold. "Obsolete hypertelorism" should be excluded from candidate lists.
