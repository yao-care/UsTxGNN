## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available, and no original indication is documented. No direct mechanistic link between phenylalanine and sclerosing cholangitis has been established.

The only connections found in the literature are indirect:
- Phenylalanine is converted to tyrosine in the body. One cohort study linked plasma tyrosine levels to fatigue in patients with primary biliary cirrhosis and primary sclerosing cholangitis. This is a metabolic association and does not show that phenylalanine treats the disease.
- Two records describe bacterial formylated peptides (fMLP, fMLY) that contain phenylalanine or tyrosine residues and are linked to cholangitis in animal models and to enterohepatic circulation. These are different molecules from free phenylalanine.

The very high TxGNN score (0.994) reflects graph proximity, not clinical evidence, and should be read with caution.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests only on a high model score. There are no registered trials for sclerosing cholangitis, and the four publications are indirect (metabolic association, animal work and assays). The approval records also give no original indication, MOA or safety data.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank) and a documented original indication
- Safety information (warnings and contraindications) from the package insert, which is required before any safety screening
- A plausible biological rationale linking free phenylalanine to cholangitis pathology, supported by preclinical data
- Confirmation of the product types and the routes of administration relevant to this indication
