## One-Sentence Summary

Minoxidil is a potassium channel opener with vasodilator and hair-growth-stimulating actions.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **hypotrichosis simplex of the scalp**, a rare hereditary hair-loss disorder.
Currently **no clinical trials** are registered, and support comes from **3 publications** (case reports and clinical reports, all using minoxidil in combination with other treatments).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data for minoxidil is not recorded in the data compiled for this report. From the published literature, minoxidil opens ATP-sensitive potassium channels. Its active metabolite, minoxidil sulfate, is thought to prolong the anagen (growth) phase of the hair cycle and to promote follicular vasodilation and proliferation.

Hereditary hypotrichosis simplex of the scalp is a rare, genetically determined disorder (linked to variants in the CDSN gene in one reported family) in which hair density and length are reduced. Because minoxidil acts directly on hair follicle growth, a mechanistic link to this condition is plausible.

The caveat is that every available report combines minoxidil with other treatments (growth factors, botanical extracts, or platelet-rich plasma). The effect of minoxidil alone therefore cannot be separated out.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: Moderate-level interactions are recorded with corticosteroids (hydrocortisone, triamcinolone, dexamethasone, betamethasone, budesonide, prednisolone, prednisone), bupropion, morphine, opium, SGLT2 inhibitors (canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin), dronabinol, nabilone and sapropterin. In total, 100 interactions are recorded.
- **Conditions of concern**: Minoxidil is recorded as problematic in people with cardiovascular disease (including prior myocardial infarction, pericardial effusion and cardiac tamponade), stroke or cerebrovascular disease, orthostatic hypotension, kidney disease, and phaeochromocytoma. Scalp dermatosis is also listed. These are database relations, not label text.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The model score is very high, but the evidence level is L4: only case reports, all of which combine minoxidil with other treatments, exist for hypotrichosis simplex of the scalp. No registered trials are available, so minoxidil's own contribution is unproven.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Controlled or single-agent studies that isolate the effect of minoxidil in hereditary hypotrichosis simplex
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Package insert safety data, including a plan for cardiovascular monitoring if oral dosing is considered
- A decision on route (topical versus low-dose oral) and dose for this rare, often paediatric, population
