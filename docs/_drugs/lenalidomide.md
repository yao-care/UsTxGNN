## Lenalidomide: From Plasma Cell Myeloma to Myeloid Leukemia
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Lenalidomide is an oral immunomodulatory drug originally used for plasma cell myeloma, mantle cell lymphoma and myelodysplastic syndrome (MDS).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Myeloid Leukemia**,
with **47 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction (mostly early-phase, small studies and reviews).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Lenalidomide is a cereblon E3 ligase modulator. It promotes degradation of the transcription factors IKZF1 and IKZF3, and of CK1alpha, which underlies its activity in MDS with del(5q). It also activates NK and T cells. These data come from the analysis compiled for this report rather than a structured mechanism-of-action record, which is currently unavailable.

MDS and acute myeloid leukemia (AML) are biologically adjacent myeloid malignancies, and MDS frequently transforms into AML. Because lenalidomide already has an established role in del(5q) MDS, a mechanistic extension to myeloid leukemia is plausible. A 2025 laboratory study also reported that cereblon levels govern the sensitivity of AML cells to lenalidomide, supporting this mechanism in leukemia cells.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-level cautions (knowledge graph, not label text)**: thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, pulmonary embolism, impaired renal function, cancer, embryoma.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Major-level interactions include **rosuvastatin** and **simvastatin**. Moderate-level interactions include **eliglustat** and **naltrexone**. Others (e.g., metformin, omeprazole, prednisone, morphine) are of unknown severity.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available; please refer to the package insert for complete safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible and there are many Phase 1/2 AML and MDS trials, but they are mostly small, single-arm or combination studies with no completed Phase 3 evidence in myeloid leukemia. The drug is marketed in the US with 12 valid licenses, and label safety data are missing. The high hematologic toxicity risk in an already cytopenic population further calls for caution.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- FDA package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking gap for safety screening)
- Structured mechanism-of-action data
- Efficacy readouts from the randomized trials (e.g., NCT01358734, NCT01522976, NCT04490707) in specific AML subgroups such as del(5q)
- A hematologic safety and monitoring plan, plus clarification of current US marketing status

**Other predictions (all Hold or Research Question):** unclassified MDS (L3, mostly del(5q) overlap with the existing indication), refractory cytopenia of childhood (L3, one pediatric Phase 1 study), aregenerative anemia (L3, indirect evidence), severe congenital hypochromic anemia with ringed sideroblasts (L5, prediction only), and partial deletion of chromosome 5q (L5, a cytogenetic finding that duplicates the existing del(5q) MDS use rather than a new indication).
