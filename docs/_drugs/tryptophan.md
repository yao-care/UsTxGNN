## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this drug. Tryptophan is nonetheless well known as the dietary precursor of serotonin and melatonin, two molecules that regulate the sleep-wake cycle, mood and motor control. It also interacts with dopaminergic and iron-related pathways implicated in restless legs syndrome.

**Restless legs syndrome:** Two small studies from 1986–1987 examined L-tryptophan in restless legs syndrome and periodic leg movements. They have not been confirmed by modern randomized trials. Pharmacovigilance data show that serotonergic antidepressants can worsen restless legs syndrome, so the serotonin system appears to modulate symptoms in a complex, possibly bidirectional way.

**Insomnia:** The mechanistic rationale is strong because serotonin and melatonin regulate sleep. Older clinical studies (1983, 1987) tested L-tryptophan in chronic insomnia against placebo and hypnotics. A 2025 mouse study tested a tryptophan–fructooligosaccharide combination. The supplied data do not show that the American Academy of Sleep Medicine guideline recommends tryptophan.

**Glaucoma:** Literature reports altered tryptophan metabolism and serotonin levels in glaucoma patients, but these are associative biomarker findings and do not show that supplementation helps.

**Methemoglobinemia and congenital prothrombin deficiency:** These are computational predictions without supporting literature or a plausible mechanism in the supplied data.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease contraindication signals (knowledge graph, not label text):** autoimmune disease, systemic lupus erythematosus (including pediatric), anxiety disorder, neurotic disorder, dysthymic disorder, schizophrenia, phenylketonuria and other disorders of phenylalanine metabolism, and eosinophilia-myalgia syndrome (including the form associated with tryptophan).
- **Historical concern:** Eosinophilia-myalgia syndrome was linked in 1989 to contaminated L-tryptophan products, and the literature describes the contamination as arising in production. This history, together with the cancelled US market status, warrants caution.
- **Drug interactions:** 17 interactions were identified.
  - **Major (9):** lorcaserin, dolasetron, palonosetron, ondansetron, fenfluramine, granisetron, sibutramine, methylene blue, doxepin (oral and topical), procarbazine. Most of these involve additive serotonergic effects.
  - **Moderate (6):** morphine, codeine, hydrocodone, cyproheptadine, ozanimod, ifosfamide.

Please refer to the package insert for label warnings and contraindications; these were not available for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top prediction, restless legs syndrome, rests on two small studies from the 1980s with no modern RCTs or registered trials. Insomnia has the strongest rationale and some older controlled data, but the evidence is dated, no trials are registered, the US market status is cancelled only, and L-tryptophan carries a historical safety concern (eosinophilia-myalgia syndrome). The other predictions are model outputs without credible supporting evidence. Insomnia and restless legs syndrome may be framed as research questions at best.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- FDA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data
- A systematic review of the 1980s tryptophan trials in insomnia and restless legs syndrome
- A modern, adequately powered randomized trial with pharmaceutical-grade tryptophan
- A safety plan covering eosinophilia-myalgia syndrome, product purity and serotonergic drug interactions
- Clarification of the regulatory pathway, given that the only US license is cancelled
