# Insulin Glulisine: From Diabetes Mellitus to Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

## One-Sentence Summary

Insulin glulisine is a rapid-acting insulin analogue, originally used to treat diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus**, but this is essentially an on-label use rather than true repurposing,
and it is supported by **29 registered clinical trials** and **19 publications** compiled for this report.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not currently available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, insulin glulisine is a rapid-acting human insulin analogue that binds the insulin receptor to lower blood glucose. Its modified amino acid sequence gives it a faster onset and shorter duration of action than regular human insulin, which suits mealtime (bolus) dosing.

Type 1 diabetes is a core form of diabetes mellitus, which is already the drug's original indication. The prediction is therefore consistent with the drug's established use. Because type 1 diabetes involves absolute insulin deficiency, exogenous insulin replacement is mechanistically sound.

Note that the US market status is "Cancelled only," so product availability should be verified before any recommendation.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed Phase 3 trials and randomized studies support insulin glulisine in type 1 diabetes, which meets the L1 evidence level. However, this is on-label use rather than true repurposing, and the only US license is cancelled, so availability must be confirmed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Confirm current US availability and licensing status (the only BLA is cancelled)
- FDA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking data gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A glucose monitoring plan for patients taking interacting drugs

**Other predicted indications (for reference):** The remaining nine predictions have no trials or literature in the data compiled for this report and are recommended as **Hold**. These are thiamine-responsive dysfunction syndrome, opsismodysplasia, classic stiff person syndrome, focal stiff limb syndrome, autoimmune oophoritis, drug-induced localized lipodystrophy, centrifugal lipodystrophy, pressure-induced localized lipoatrophy and pancreatic agenesis. Pancreatic agenesis is the exception, as it is a plausible research question: insulin replacement is mechanistically sound for the resulting neonatal diabetes, but it manages the diabetes rather than the agenesis. The lipodystrophy and lipoatrophy predictions are likely adverse effects of insulin injection rather than therapeutic effects, so use for these conditions is not supported.
