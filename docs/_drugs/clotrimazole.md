# Clotrimazole: From Antifungal Therapy to Acne

## One-Sentence Summary

Clotrimazole is an azole antifungal, widely marketed in the US as a topical product for fungal skin and mucosal infections.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **acne**, but the only supporting evidence is **1 suspended clinical trial** of a three-drug combination and **no publications**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the input. Clotrimazole is known to be an azole antifungal that blocks ergosterol synthesis in fungi. Its efficacy in fungal infections is well established.

The link to acne is indirect. Clotrimazole may have a minor anti-inflammatory or anti-*Malassezia* effect, but this is unproven for acne. Acne is mainly a disorder of sebaceous glands, follicular keratinization and bacterial colonization, none of which the antifungal mechanism directly targets.

The very high TxGNN score (99.86%) is a model prediction only. The single related trial tests a fixed combination, so any effect cannot be attributed to clotrimazole.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The acne prediction rests on a model score alone. The only trial is suspended, has no results, and tests a combination product, so the clotrimazole contribution cannot be isolated. No literature supports this indication.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications, which are required before safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Clotrimazole monotherapy evidence in acne, such as a randomized trial or mechanistic study
- Clarification of the original labeled indications, which are empty in the source data

**Note on other predictions:** The rank 2 prediction, vulvovaginitis, has much stronger evidence: several completed Phase 3/4 trials and multiple RCTs. However, it appears to be an established candidal use rather than true repurposing, so confirm the labeled indication before presenting it as novel. The rank 3 prediction, postmenopausal atrophic vaginitis, is not supported by clinical evidence.
