## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, losartan blocks the angiotensin II type 1 (AT1) receptor, and its efficacy in hypertension and diabetic nephropathy is established.

Malignant hypertensive renal disease is a severe kidney injury caused by extremely high blood pressure. Angiotensin II and NF-κB signaling are implicated in its pathology. A 2019 rat model study of malignant hypertensive nephrosclerosis supports this angle. The link is therefore mechanistically plausible, since the new indication overlaps with the drug's known blood-pressure and kidney-protective effects.

However, the high TxGNN score is a model prediction only. No human clinical evidence exists for this specific population.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only support for malignant hypertensive renal disease is a high TxGNN score and one preclinical rat study. No human trials exist, and the label safety information was not obtained, so the evidence is insufficient to advance.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- FDA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Clinical data in patients with malignant hypertensive nephrosclerosis, such as retrospective cohorts or a pilot trial
- Safety assessment of ARB use in renovascular disease, particularly bilateral renal artery stenosis
- Confirmation of current US marketing status, since the drug is marketed with nine valid licenses in the compiled data
