## Bupropion: From Depression to Attention Deficit-Hyperactivity Disorder
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Bupropion is an antidepressant that is also used for smoking cessation, with original indications that include major depressive disorder, anxiety disorder, and nicotine dependence.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Attention Deficit-Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD)**,
with **6 registered clinical trials** (4 completed, 1 withdrawn, 1 early phase) and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on general pharmacology, bupropion inhibits the reuptake of norepinephrine and dopamine and has no serotonergic activity. This is a general-knowledge link, not one taken from the supplied record.

ADHD is linked to dysfunction of the same noradrenergic and dopaminergic pathways. Established ADHD treatments such as stimulants and atomoxetine act on these pathways. Bupropion's efficacy in depression, together with this catecholaminergic action, gives a plausible reason for improved attention and reduced hyperactivity and impulsivity. The very high TxGNN score is consistent with this overlap.

Other predicted indications are much weaker. The inattentive subtype of ADHD has only indirect evidence (no registered trials of its own). The remaining eight predictions (for example faciodigitogenital syndrome, chondromyxoid fibroma, hypervitaminosis) have no clinical evidence and no plausible mechanistic link. They are model output only and are not pursued here.

## Safety Considerations

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the package insert for these.

- **Conditions linked to contraindication in the knowledge graph** (from a drug-disease database, not label text):
  - Neurological and psychiatric: epilepsy; psychotic disorders (schizophreniform, schizoaffective); manic bipolar disorder; anorexia nervosa; bulimia nervosa; anxiety disorder; CNS neoplasm
  - Cardiovascular: hypertension; myocardial infarction
  - Organ disease: liver failure; liver disease; cirrhosis; kidney disease; gallbladder disease
  - Substance use: substance abuse/dependence; opiate dependence
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were found, of which 20 are shown here. Major-level interactions include fentanyl, alfentanil, codeine, dihydrocodeine, tramadol, lidocaine, methylphenidate, hydrocortisone, aldesleukin, and amantadine. Moderate-level interactions include ethanol, alprazolam, nifedipine, hydrochlorothiazide, cetirizine, chlorpheniramine, acebutolol, alfuzosin, alpelisib, and topical abametapir.
- **Additional considerations for this indication:** Bupropion lowers the seizure threshold, and the knowledge graph flags epilepsy, eating disorders, and substance dependence. These conditions often co-occur with ADHD, so patient selection matters. A recent network meta-analysis also examined the cardiovascular safety of ADHD drugs, which is relevant given the hypertension and myocardial infarction flags.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
One completed Phase 3 randomised, placebo-controlled trial (NCT00048360) and one completed Phase 2/3 trial (NCT00061087) support bupropion in adult ADHD. A Cochrane review and several network meta-analyses also address this use. The mechanism is plausible, but the data compiled for this report contain no mechanism-of-action record and no label safety data. The US licences are all cancelled, so the drug's regulatory position needs clarification.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap), especially seizure risk, the boxed warning, and the neuropsychiatric profile
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Trial results for NCT00048360 and NCT00061087, including the comparator, randomisation and blinding, and efficacy and safety outcomes
- Confirmation of the current US regulatory status, since all 13 ANDAs are cancelled
- A patient selection and monitoring plan covering seizure risk, hypertension, cardiovascular disease, eating disorders, and substance use comorbidity
- A review of the major drug interactions, particularly with opioids and methylphenidate, before any combination use
