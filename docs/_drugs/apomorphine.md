## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the data compiled for this report. Apomorphine is generally known as a direct dopamine D1/D2 receptor agonist. Its efficacy in Parkinson disease comes from stimulating dopamine receptors, which compensates for the loss of dopaminergic neurons.

**Schizophrenia:** There is a biological connection through the dopamine hypothesis, which implicates dopaminergic dysfunction in the disorder. However, the direction of the pharmacology is opposite to that of approved antipsychotics, which block D2 receptors. Older studies (1975–1984) used apomorphine as a probe and in small treatment and neuroendocrine-response studies. One small study of 25 male patients using low-dose apomorphine (0.75 mg subcutaneously) found no significant improvement or deterioration. A 2001 review framed the question as a "dilemma," because apomorphine shows little evidence of inducing psychosis, even though the dopamine hypothesis would predict it should. One possible rationale is stimulation of presynaptic autoreceptors, but this is unproven.

**Other predictions:** For the remaining nine predictions, the data do not support a mechanistic link.
- Rank 1 (polymicrogyria), rank 2 (glycosylation disorder), rank 4 (Charcot-Marie-Tooth 1G), rank 7 (atypical glycine encephalopathy), and rank 10 (hydranencephaly) are congenital, metabolic, or structural conditions with no plausible dopaminergic rationale.
- Rank 3 (retinal dystrophy) has a speculative link through retinal dopamine signalling.
- Ranks 6, 8, and 9 (X-linked, female-limited, and syndromic myopia) rest on the hypothesis that retinal dopamine modulates eye growth. This is a plausible but untested idea here, with no clinical evidence in the compiled data.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- All ten predictions rest on model scores alone, or on indirect, old, small studies. None has a registered clinical trial. The top prediction (polymicrogyria) has no plausible mechanism. For schizophrenia, the small controlled studies from 1975–1984 showed no significant benefit, and psychotic disorders appear among the knowledge-graph contraindications. Schizophrenia is best treated as a research question rather than a development candidate. All US licences are also cancelled.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism-of-action data and package insert warnings and contraindications (currently missing; the latter is a blocking gap for safety screening).
- For schizophrenia, a careful review of the older negative studies and the psychosis-related contraindication signal, followed by preclinical or mechanistic work (for example, autoreceptor-selective effects) before any clinical consideration.
- For the myopia and retinal predictions, supporting preclinical evidence, since retinal dopamine is only a hypothesis here, plus a route-of-administration assessment (current products are injectables only).
- Confirmation of the current US regulatory and supply status, given that all licences are cancelled.
