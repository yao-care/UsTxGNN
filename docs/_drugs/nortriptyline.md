## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, nortriptyline is a tricyclic antidepressant that mainly inhibits norepinephrine reuptake and, to a lesser extent, serotonin reuptake. This link is inferred from general knowledge of the drug class, not from the supplied data.

Noradrenergic enhancement in prefrontal circuits is the shared mechanism of established non-stimulant ADHD drugs such as atomoxetine. A PET study in the literature (PMID 24345533) describes nortriptyline as a norepinephrine-transporter-selective tricyclic, which supports this reasoning. Reviews also describe secondary-amine tricyclics, including nortriptyline, as established alternative ADHD treatments.

The original indications (anxiety, neurotic and dysthymic disorders) are psychiatric conditions that often co-occur with ADHD. The high TxGNN score is a computational prediction only and does not replace clinical evidence.

A second predicted entry, ADHD inattentive type (score 0.9933), has no linked trials or literature. It is best treated as a subtype of the parent ADHD indication and reviewed together with it.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (from the knowledge graph, not label text):** long QT syndrome (including familial), torsades de pointes, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease, myocardial infarction and coronary thrombosis, stroke and cerebrovascular disorders, epilepsy, manic bipolar disorder, open-angle glaucoma and glaucoma, benign prostatic hyperplasia, liver failure, kidney failure and uremia, hyperthyroidism (Graves disease, thyrotoxicosis), substance abuse/dependence, and hemoglobinopathy.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were identified. Major-level examples include epinephrine, bupropion, lorcaserin, potassium citrate and dolasetron. Moderate-level examples include famotidine, cimetidine, clarithromycin, morphine, loperamide and several anticholinergics (atropine, hyoscyamine, glycopyrronium, dicyclomine).
- **Literature signals:** The ADHD literature highlights the narrow therapeutic index, cardiovascular toxicity (ECG effects) and anticholinergic burden of tricyclics. ECG monitoring and careful co-medication review are advisable.

Please refer to the package insert for the full warnings, as label text was not available.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
The literature includes a controlled pediatric study, a Cochrane review of tricyclics and several observational reports supporting nortriptyline in ADHD, with a plausible noradrenergic mechanism. However, no clinical trials are registered, the evidence is mostly older and small-scale, and the cardiac and drug-interaction risks are substantial.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- FDA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Appraisal of the RCT (PMID 11052409) and the Cochrane review for effect size and safety
- An ECG and serum-level monitoring plan, plus a co-medication review given the large interaction burden
- Regulatory pathway assessment, since the drug is marketed in the US with 4 valid licenses
- Combined review of the ADHD and ADHD inattentive-type entries as one indication
