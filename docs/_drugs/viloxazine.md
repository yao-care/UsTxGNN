# Viloxazine: From Narcolepsy to Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Viloxazine is listed in the knowledge graph as originally associated with narcolepsy-related indications, although in the US it is marketed as an extended-release product (Qelbree) for ADHD.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **attention deficit-hyperactivity disorder**, with **5 clinical trials** and **19 publications** currently supporting this direction.
Note that ADHD is already an FDA-approved use of extended-release viloxazine, so this is not a repurposing candidate in the strict sense.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, viloxazine is a selective norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor with additional serotonergic modulation. This is consistent with the noradrenergic mechanism of other non-stimulant ADHD treatments such as atomoxetine.

The original narcolepsy indications and ADHD are both conditions of impaired arousal, attention and wakefulness regulation, in which noradrenergic signalling plays a role. This provides a plausible, though indirect, link between the two.

The strongest support is direct clinical evidence: a placebo-controlled, flexible-dose Phase 3 trial in adults (NCT04016779) and several long-term extension studies. Extended-release viloxazine (Qelbree) is already described in the literature as an FDA-approved nonstimulant for children, adolescents and adults with ADHD. The prediction is therefore confirmed by existing practice rather than being a new discovery.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

Of note, the literature compiled for this report mentions that the cardiovascular safety of ADHD medications, including haemodynamic and ECG effects, has been the subject of a recent network meta-analysis (PMID 40203844). Noradrenergic reuptake inhibition may also be associated with insomnia.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
A completed Phase 3 randomized placebo-controlled trial, several long-term extension studies and multiple meta-analyses and reviews support viloxazine in ADHD. However, this is an already established use of the extended-release product, not a true repurposing finding. The US licence record shows only a single NDA with cancelled status, and the safety label data are missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- FDA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Confirmation of the current US marketing and licence status of Qelbree, given that the licence record shows "cancelled" while the literature describes it as FDA-approved
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Clarification of the link between the original narcolepsy indications and ADHD, since the original indication record does not explain the relationship
