## One-Sentence Summary

Meropenem is a broad-spectrum carbapenem antibiotic that blocks bacterial cell wall synthesis. The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **bacterial arthritis**. No clinical trials are registered for this use. The support is **19 publications**, mostly case reports, retrospective series, pharmacokinetic work and susceptibility surveys, so the evidence is limited.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Meropenem binds penicillin-binding proteins and inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis. It covers many Gram-negative and Gram-positive organisms, including resistant Gram-negative bacilli. Bone and joint infections are often caused by such organisms, so using it in bacterial arthritis is mechanistically plausible.

The literature fits this reasoning only partly:

- One case report describes septic arthritis from ESBL-producing *Klebsiella pneumoniae*. It was treated successfully with meropenem plus amikacin and early arthroscopic washout.
- A retrospective review of musculoskeletal melioidosis found that all isolates were susceptible to meropenem.

No trial has tested meropenem directly in bacterial arthritis. Detailed mechanism data beyond the general antibacterial action were not available in the data compiled for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible and a few case reports describe meropenem use in septic arthritis. There are no clinical trials, and the literature is mostly case reports, retrospective series and susceptibility or pharmacokinetic data. Efficacy in bacterial arthritis has not been shown directly.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Comparative clinical data (controlled studies or well-designed cohorts) on meropenem in bacterial arthritis, defined by causative organism.
- Joint penetration and dosing data for bone and joint infection.
- Review of the package insert warnings and contraindications.
- A stewardship assessment of when a carbapenem is justified over narrower agents, for example for resistant Gram-negative pathogens.
