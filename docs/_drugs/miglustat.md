## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Pharmacology data nonetheless identify the target of miglustat as UDP-glucose ceramide glucosyltransferase (glucosylceramide synthase, UGCG). Miglustat is an iminosugar that inhibits this enzyme, and the literature describes it as brain-penetrant.

Gaucher disease and Tay-Sachs disease are both glycosphingolipid storage disorders. Miglustat is used as substrate reduction therapy (SRT): it lowers the synthesis of glycosphingolipids so that a cell with deficient enzyme activity is less overloaded. In Tay-Sachs disease, HEXA deficiency causes GM2 ganglioside to accumulate in nerve cells. Reducing the synthesis of GM2 and its precursors is therefore a biologically coherent approach. Because miglustat crosses the blood-brain barrier, it is also of interest for neurological forms, where enzyme replacement therapy generally does not reach the central nervous system.

The evidence is mixed. One case series (PMID 16434676) found that SRT could not arrest neurological deterioration in two infantile Tay-Sachs patients, although miglustat reached the cerebrospinal fluid.

The other nine predictions (for example Krabbe disease, metachromatic leukodystrophy and prosaposin deficiency) have only a plausible but unsupported sphingolipid rationale. For several others (cholesteryl ester storage disease, Wolman disease, adrenal neoplasm, the ichthyoses), the mechanistic link is weak or speculative.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph (not label text):** peripheral nervous system disease, peripheral neuropathy, impaired renal function disease.
- **Drug Interactions:** Imiglucerase (Moderate, DDInter). A pharmacology record also links miglustat to its target, UDP-glucose ceramide glucosyltransferase (UGCG), which is its intended mechanism rather than a clinical interaction.
- **Tolerability:** the literature notes that miglustat use is limited by gastrointestinal side effects (PMID 28476546).

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications, which were not available for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Tay-Sachs disease has a coherent mechanistic rationale, a randomized study in late-onset disease, a systematic review and four registered trials. However, the trials are small, pharmacokinetic or safety focused, or terminated early, and a case series showed no arrest of neurological decline in the infantile form. Safety label data are missing and all US licenses are cancelled. The other nine predictions have no supporting evidence and remain model predictions only (L5, Hold).

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The package insert (warnings and contraindications), which is a blocking gap for safety screening
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Efficacy results from the late-onset Tay-Sachs RCT (PMID 19346952) and the GM2 systematic review (PMID 37209042), and the outcomes of the terminated trials NCT02030015 and NCT03822013
- Clarification of the regulatory pathway given that all US licenses are cancelled
- Safety assessment for patients with peripheral neuropathy or renal impairment
