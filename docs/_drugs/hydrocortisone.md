## Hydrocortisone: Alopecia areata
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Hydrocortisone is a glucocorticoid originally used for adrenocortical insufficiency, autoimmune and inflammatory diseases (such as Crohn disease), and several skin conditions.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **alopecia areata**, but only **1 clinical trial** (in which hydrocortisone was the comparator, not the test therapy) and a small body of mostly older, low-quality **literature** support this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, hydrocortisone is a glucocorticoid with anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive effects.

Alopecia areata is an autoimmune disease in which the immune privilege of the hair follicle collapses and T cells attack the follicle. Because hydrocortisone is already used in other autoimmune and inflammatory conditions, a biological rationale for its use here is plausible. Topical and intralesional corticosteroids are an established treatment class for alopecia areata.

There is an important caveat. In the only available Phase 3 trial, low-potency hydrocortisone 1% cream was the comparator, and the published result favored the higher-potency steroid clobetasol. Hydrocortisone therefore appears less effective than stronger steroids in this setting, and the trial does not show that hydrocortisone itself works.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Although one completed Phase 3 trial formally meets the L1 criterion, hydrocortisone 1% was only the comparator and performed worse than clobetasol. The other literature is mostly old case reports. There is therefore no direct evidence that hydrocortisone is effective for alopecia areata, and higher-potency corticosteroids are already the established option.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Official package insert warnings and contraindications
- Mechanism of action data
- Evidence that hydrocortisone, rather than a higher-potency steroid, offers a benefit in alopecia areata (for example, a direct controlled comparison or a rationale for a specific route or population)
- Route compatibility assessment between available hydrocortisone products and the formulation needed for scalp or intralesional use

**Other predicted indications:** The remaining nine predictions (including alopecia mucinosis, telogen effluvium, folliculitis decalvans and idiopathic steroid-sensitive nephrotic syndrome) have no clinical trials and only indirect or case-level literature, so all are on Hold.
