# Budesonide: From Approved Corticosteroid Products to Atopic Eczema

## One-Sentence Summary

Budesonide is a glucocorticoid marketed in the US as inhaled, oral and nasal-spray products. The source data does not record its approved indications.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **atopic eczema**, but **no clinical trial or paper provided tests budesonide for eczema**. Only **2 loosely related trials** and **19 mostly indirect publications** were found, so this remains a research question.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on general pharmacology, budesonide is a glucocorticoid receptor agonist with broad anti-inflammatory activity. It suppresses pro-inflammatory cytokines and immune cell recruitment in mucosal tissues.

Topical corticosteroids are a standard therapy class for atopic dermatitis, so the mechanism is plausible. One 2024 paper describes budesonide as already applied in local therapy of paediatric atopic dermatitis. It presents this only as background to a formulation study.

The TxGNN score is very high, but it is a knowledge-graph prediction and not clinical evidence. The supplied literature is mostly about contact allergy to budesonide, growth effects of topical steroids, and asthma or eosinophilic esophagitis. That is a caution signal for any dermatologic use.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information. The Evidence Pack contains no warnings, contraindications or drug-interaction records (the interaction query found no entries).

Signals from the supplied literature, relevant to dermatologic use:
- **Contact and systemic allergy to budesonide** is documented in patch-test studies and case reports. It may be more likely in atopic patients exposed to many topical agents.
- **Systemic absorption and growth suppression** were studied in children with atopic dermatitis on topical glucocorticoids.
- **Adrenal (HPA axis) suppression** is a known concern with corticosteroids across routes.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible, but there is no interventional human evidence for budesonide in atopic eczema. The supplied trials are unrelated, and the literature is mostly allergy and safety signals plus one preclinical formulation paper. The TxGNN score alone does not justify advancing (L4, stage S0).

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Approved-indication text and package insert warnings and contraindications for the existing US products
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- A targeted search for randomised trials of topical budesonide in human atopic dermatitis, and a comparison against established topical corticosteroids
- A route and formulation assessment, since no topical dermatologic budesonide product is listed
- Merging the duplicate "atopic eczema" and "dermatitis, atopic" entries
- A paediatric safety plan covering contact allergy, growth and HPA-axis suppression

*Other predictions in the pack: bronchitis (L3, Research Question), with the polyp-type predictions (vocal cord, middle ear, epulis, fibroepithelial, uterine, external auditory canal) at L4-L5 and mostly Hold. Bronchitis overlaps with existing inhaled budesonide use in asthma and COPD, so it may be an on-label extension rather than true repurposing.*
