## One-Sentence Summary

Acetylcysteine is a mucolytic and antioxidant thiol drug that is marketed in the United States as inhalant, solution and injection products.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **thrombotic disease**, with **7 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently listed for this direction.
Most of this work concerns specific subtypes (thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura, transplant-associated thrombotic microangiopathy and immune thrombocytopenia) rather than thrombosis in general.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on the published literature, acetylcysteine can reduce disulfide bonds in von Willebrand factor (VWF) multimers. This lowers their size and their ability to promote clotting. It also has antioxidant effects that may limit platelet activation and injury to blood-vessel lining cells.

This is supported by preclinical mouse and baboon models of thrombotic thrombocytopenic purpura (TTP) (PMID 28011677). Clinical signals come mainly from TTP, transplant-associated thrombotic microangiopathy (TA-TMA), and combination regimens in immune thrombocytopenia (ITP).

"Thrombotic disease" is a broad label. The evidence applies to certain subtypes only and cannot be generalized to venous or arterial thrombosis.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Several trials and reviews, including a completed 170-patient Phase 3 trial in TA-TMA, address thrombotic microangiopathies directly, which supports the L2 level. However, no outcome data from that trial were provided, and the evidence does not extend to venous or arterial thrombosis.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Published results of NCT03252925 and NCT07279610
- Confirmation of which thrombotic subtypes (TTP, TA-TMA) are the target population
- Label-based warnings and contraindications from the package insert
- Mechanism-of-action data
- Clarification of the current licence status of the US products
