# Insulin Aspart: From Diabetes Mellitus to Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus

## One-Sentence Summary

Insulin aspart is a rapid-acting insulin analogue, originally used to treat diabetes mellitus and diabetic ketoacidosis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus**, with **30 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction.
Note that type 1 diabetes is effectively part of the drug's existing (on-label) use, so this is a confirmation of a core use rather than true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Insulin aspart is a rapid-acting human insulin analogue. It activates the insulin receptor to lower blood glucose. Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report, but the drug's pharmacology is well established.

Type 1 diabetes is caused by absolute insulin deficiency, so exogenous insulin replacement is its core treatment. The predicted indication is therefore very close to the original one: it is a subtype of diabetes mellitus that the drug already covers. The high TxGNN score is consistent with this, though it mostly confirms an existing use.

Other predicted diseases are weaker. Permanent neonatal diabetes mellitus (insulin replacement is plausible, but only one literature review is available) and pancreatic agenesis (logical in principle, but the linked literature is indirect) are research questions at most. The remaining predictions, such as autoimmune oophoritis, stiff person syndrome and the lipodystrophies, have no supporting clinical evidence and are likely network-association artifacts. Drug-induced localized lipodystrophy is better read as a known injection-site safety concern than as a treatment opportunity.

## Safety Considerations

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report, and no drug interaction records were found. Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

One point from the prediction analysis is worth noting: insulin injections are a known cause of localized lipodystrophy (lipohypertrophy/lipoatrophy) at injection sites. Injection-site rotation should be considered in any development plan.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed Phase 3 trials and several systematic reviews support insulin aspart in type 1 diabetes, giving an L1 evidence level. However, this is essentially an existing core use rather than new repurposing. The safety label data is missing and the US licenses are all listed as cancelled, so the drug's regulatory status needs clarifying before proceeding.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- FDA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation of current US marketing status, since the compiled licenses are all cancelled
- Clarification of whether any true repurposing opportunity exists beyond type 1 diabetes; permanent neonatal diabetes and pancreatic agenesis are the only plausible candidates and would need aspart-specific evidence
