# Bendamustine: From Follicular Lymphoma to Mantle Cell Lymphoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Bendamustine is a bifunctional alkylating chemotherapy originally used for B-cell malignancies such as follicular lymphoma, mature B-cell neoplasms, acute lymphoblastic leukemia and Richter syndrome.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Mantle Cell Lymphoma**, with **40 clinical trials** and **20 publications** compiled for this direction, including several Phase 3 randomized trials.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, bendamustine is a bifunctional alkylating agent with purine-analog-like properties. It causes DNA cross-linking and apoptosis in B-cell malignancies.

Mantle cell lymphoma (MCL) is a mature B-cell neoplasm closely related to the original indications (follicular lymphoma and other indolent B-cell lymphomas). Because the cell biology is shared, the mechanistic rationale is direct.

Bendamustine plus rituximab (BR) is an established backbone in MCL. It is the investigational arm, comparator or backbone in several Phase 3 trials (StiL/NHL 1-2003, BRIGHT, SHINE, ECHO, and others) and in recent treatment guidelines.
