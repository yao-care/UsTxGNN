## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available in the compiled data, but the literature describes clofarabine as a second-generation purine nucleoside analog. It inhibits ribonucleotide reductase and DNA polymerase, which depletes the nucleotide pools needed for DNA replication. It also induces mitochondrial apoptosis. These actions hit rapidly dividing leukemic blasts.

Because this mechanism does not depend on the lineage of the leukemic cell, it is shared by lymphoid and myeloid leukemias. Extending clofarabine from lymphoblastic leukemia to myeloid leukemia is therefore mechanistically coherent. Reviews describe single-agent activity in acute myeloid leukemia (AML), and the drug has been combined with cytarabine, idarubicin, and other agents in many trials.

## Safety Considerations

- **Conditions of concern:** The data compiled for this report flag liver disease, gallbladder disease, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, anemia, hyperbilirubinemia, kidney failure, hypotensive disorder, capillary leak syndrome, and active infections (including fungal infections) as conditions where caution applies. These come from database relations, not from label text.
- **Kidney toxicity:** A published case report describes clofarabine-induced kidney toxicity in a patient with refractory AML.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were found, all of the 20 listed being moderate. Examples are amphotericin B (including lipid, liposomal, and cholesteryl sulfate forms), clarithromycin, levofloxacin, kanamycin, neomycin, minocycline, mesalazine, balsalazide, bupropion, naltrexone, pioglitazone, and acarbose.

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Two completed Phase 3 randomized trials (727 and 326 participants) and many Phase 2 studies directly address clofarabine in AML, and the mechanism is coherent. Safety signals, mainly myelosuppression and renal and hepatic toxicity, call for tight monitoring.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Review of the Phase 3 trial results (NCT02085408, NCT00317642) for efficacy and survival outcomes in AML
- Package insert warnings and contraindications, which are missing from the compiled data
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- A safety monitoring plan (blood counts, liver and renal function, infection risk) and a review of the drug interactions above
