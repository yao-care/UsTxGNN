# Procarbazine: From Hodgkin Lymphoma to Follicular Lymphoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Procarbazine is an oral alkylating-type chemotherapy agent, originally used to treat Hodgkin lymphoma.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **follicular lymphoma**.
Direct support is limited: **2 related clinical trials** (neither of them a clear test of procarbazine) and **12 publications**, of which only a few directly address procarbazine.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, procarbazine is an oral alkylating-type agent with established activity in lymphoid malignancies, and its efficacy in Hodgkin lymphoma has been proven. Mechanistically, it may be applicable to follicular lymphoma.

Both Hodgkin lymphoma and follicular lymphoma are lymphoid cancers, so shared lymphoma biology makes activity plausible. Procarbazine was also used in non-Hodgkin lymphoma regimens before CHOP became standard. A 2006 report describes two patients with relapsed or refractory follicular lymphoma who achieved a complete and durable remission with prolonged daily procarbazine. The low-dose oral PEP-C regimen, a metronomic combination, has also been studied in relapsed or refractory lymphoma.

This link rests on shared disease biology and small case-level evidence, not on a confirmed mechanism.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications (knowledge-graph signals, not label text)**: liver failure and liver disease, kidney disease, gallbladder disease, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, anemia (including macrocytic anemia), blood coagulation disease, peripheral neuropathy and peripheral nervous system disease, infectious disease (including fungal infection and dermatophytosis), pyoureter, and substance abuse/dependence.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found. Many are major, notably with sympathomimetics and serotonergic or anorectic agents: isometheptene, bupropion, morphine, lorcaserin, diethylpropion, phentermine, dexfenfluramine, fenfluramine, mazindol and ephedrine. Major interactions were also found with the 5-HT3 antagonists dolasetron, palonosetron, ondansetron and granisetron. Moderate interactions include epinephrine (several forms) and metoclopramide. Levofloxacin is a minor interaction.
- **Other signals**: Animal carcinogenicity studies (NCI bioassay) and survivor cohorts report secondary malignancy and gonadotoxicity risks with procarbazine-containing regimens.

Please also refer to the package insert for full safety information, as label warnings were not available in the data compiled for this report.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The evidence is limited to a small case report, a cohort description of the PEP-C regimen and background literature. The two registered trials either terminated early (6 patients) or do not test procarbazine. The US license is cancelled, the mechanism of action is unavailable, and the safety profile (myelosuppression, carcinogenicity, interactions) calls for caution. Safety screening cannot proceed without the package insert.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- FDA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank)
- Verification of whether the regimen in NCT00003113 contains procarbazine
- Prospective or retrospective data on procarbazine-containing regimens (such as PEP-C) in follicular lymphoma
- A review of the regulatory pathway, given that the only US license is cancelled
