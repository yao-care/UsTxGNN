## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Valine is an essential branched-chain amino acid (BCAA), a nutritional and metabolic building block rather than a drug with a defined target.

The link to sclerosing cholangitis is indirect. Altered plasma amino acid profiles, including tyrosine and BCAAs, have been reported in cholestatic liver disease. A Mendelian randomization study also associates blood metabolites and metabolic pathways with cholestatic liver diseases (primary biliary cholangitis and primary sclerosing cholangitis). These findings are associative or genetic-epidemiological. They do not show that giving valine would treat the disease. The high TxGNN score is a model output, and no interventional data exist.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on a high model score and indirect metabolic associations only. There are no clinical trials and no evidence that valine is therapeutic in sclerosing cholangitis. The drug has no active U.S. authorization, and the safety and mechanism data are incomplete.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank) and a hypothesis for why valine would affect cholestatic liver disease
- Package insert warnings and contraindications to allow a safety screen
- Preclinical or early interventional evidence showing benefit in sclerosing cholangitis models or patients
- Clarification of the regulatory status and intended formulation, since the only U.S. record is cancelled
