## Why This Prediction Is Reasonable

Etravirine belongs to the NNRTI (non-nucleoside reverse transcriptase inhibitor) class, a pharmacological mechanism widely recognized in the industry—this is also directly stated in the evidence package's repurposing_rationale, despite the formal `original_moa` field being marked as a data gap. NNRTIs inhibit viral replication by binding to an allosteric site on HIV-1 reverse transcriptase and are currently approved for treating treatment-experienced HIV-1 infected adults and children.

Congenital HIV infection is fundamentally an extension of the HIV infection population spectrum—the difference lies in the "pregnant and perinatal population" rather than a novel disease mechanism. The TxGNN model's reasoning for this candidate represents same-mechanism, different-population application rather than mechanism extrapolation hypothesis, which aligns highly with clinical trial evidence (see below): among the 13 existing trials, several directly evaluate etravirine pharmacokinetics in "HIV-1-infected pregnant women" (e.g., NCT00855335, NCT00042289), demonstrating that the industry has already explored medication safety and exposure adjustments for etravirine in the perinatal population, supporting the translational rationale of this prediction direction.

Special clarification: the disease label in this assessment candidate ("congenital human immunodeficiency virus") is better understood as a knowledge graph correspondence term for "vertical transmission prevention/perinatal antiretroviral treatment," not a congenital malformation-type disease; consistency in interpreting evidence must be maintained.
