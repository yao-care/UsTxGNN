# Guaifenesin: From Expectorant to Nasal Cavity Disease

## One-Sentence Summary

Guaifenesin is a widely marketed oral expectorant used to loosen chest mucus.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **nasal cavity disease**, with **1 small Phase 2 pilot trial** and **2 older narrative reviews** so far.
That is early-stage evidence and does not yet show efficacy.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Guaifenesin is generally understood to thin and loosen airway mucus. That is a general pharmacological assumption and is not confirmed by DrugBank data.

Chronic rhinitis and sinusitis involve thick or retained nasal and sinus secretions, so a mucus-thinning drug could plausibly help. The pediatric chronic rhinitis pilot trial tests this idea directly.

The TxGNN score is a model output, not clinical evidence. The other four predicted indications (acute laryngopharyngitis, faucial diphtheria, cervical disc degenerative disorder, papillary conjunctivitis) have no trials or literature. The last three have no plausible mechanistic link and are likely knowledge-graph artifacts.

## Clinical Trial Evidence

|---------|------|------|------|---------|
| [NCT01364467](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01364467) | Phase 2 | Completed | 30 | 14-day pilot of oral guaifenesin in children aged 7–18 with chronic rhinitis. It compares nasal symptoms (SN-5 survey) with nasal airway volume and secretion properties. No results were provided, so no efficacy conclusion is possible. |

The registry summary mentions 36 children, while the recorded enrollment is 30. The record also does not clearly confirm randomization, blinding or a control arm, so this is treated as exploratory evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only direct evidence is one small, completed Phase 2 pilot trial with no reported results, plus two indirect narrative reviews. The safety and label data are also missing. Guaifenesin's long marketed use gives it a familiar profile, but the nasal indication is still a research question.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Results or publication from NCT01364467, and confirmation of its design (randomization, blinding, control arm)
- Package insert warnings and contraindications, which currently block safety screening
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Route compatibility assessment, since oral guaifenesin is the only formulation tested
- A controlled trial in chronic rhinitis or sinusitis, if the pilot results are encouraging
