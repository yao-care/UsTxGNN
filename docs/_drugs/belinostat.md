## Belinostat: From T-Cell Lymphoma to Myeloid Leukemia
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Belinostat is an HDAC inhibitor that was marketed in the US as an injectable anticancer drug (the product record in the data compiled for this report does not list an approved indication).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **myeloid leukemia**,
with **6 clinical trials** (all early phase, Phase 1 to Phase 2) and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction, although single-agent activity appears limited.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Belinostat is a pan-HDAC (histone deacetylase) inhibitor. Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report, but the class mechanism is well established. In myeloid leukemia, HDAC inhibition promotes histone hyperacetylation, re-expression of silenced tumor suppressor genes, cell-cycle arrest and apoptosis.

Its approved use in T-cell lymphoma and myeloid leukemia are both blood cancers in which epigenetic dysregulation plays a role. Preclinical work shows synergy with:
- proteasome inhibitors (bortezomib),
- NEDD8 inhibitors (pevonedistat),
- WEE1 inhibitors (adavosertib),
- hypomethylating agents (azacitidine),
- EZH2 inhibitors (3-deazaneplanocin A).

The very high TxGNN score fits this picture. However, single-agent activity in AML appears limited, and combination benefit has not been confirmed in randomized studies.

  - Samarium (153Sm) lexidronam

  Examples of **Moderate** interactions include naltrexone, palifermin, strontium chloride Sr-89, fostamatinib, roflumilast, chloramphenicol, zidovudine, alemtuzumab, alpelisib, anakinra, apalutamide, asparaginase and several vaccines (anthrax, tetanus toxoid). Only 20 of the 100 interactions were provided, so the list is incomplete.
- **Clinical trial safety signal**: QTc prolongation was the only dose-limiting toxicity in the belinostat plus bortezomib Phase 1 study.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high and there is a sound mechanistic rationale, but clinical evidence is limited to small, early-phase studies (one single-agent Phase 2 study with n=12 and limited activity, plus Phase 1 combination studies, two of them terminated early). No randomized trial exists. The US NDA is cancelled, and label safety information is missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- FDA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Efficacy outcomes (response rates) from the combination studies, to identify the most promising partner
- Randomized or larger Phase 2 evidence for a specific combination
- Clarification of the regulatory and commercial availability given the cancelled NDA

*Other predicted indications:* plasma cell myeloma (score 0.9927, L3, no registered trials, literature mainly class reviews; Research Question) and indolent plasma cell myeloma (score 0.9908, L5, no trials or literature; Hold) have even weaker support than myeloid leukemia.
