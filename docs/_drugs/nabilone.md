# Nabilone: From an Antiemetic Indication to Migraine Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Nabilone is a synthetic cannabinoid capsule (marketed in the US as Cesamet) that is known as an antiemetic. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **migraine disorder**, but there are currently **0 registered clinical trials** and only **1 indirect systematic review** supporting this direction. The strongest nabilone-specific signal is a small preliminary trial in the related condition of medication overuse headache.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, nabilone is a synthetic agonist of the CB1 and CB2 cannabinoid receptors. The endocannabinoid system modulates trigeminovascular pain signaling, CGRP release and central pain sensitization, all of which are involved in migraine. This offers a plausible mechanistic link.

The original indication is not recorded in the license data. Publications describe nabilone as approved for chemotherapy-induced vomiting that is refractory to other antiemetics. That indication is not closely related to migraine, so the case for repurposing rests on the shared cannabinoid-receptor pharmacology rather than on similarity between diseases.

The only migraine-specific literature is a 2026 systematic review of cannabinoid-based interventions. It covers the cannabinoid class and is not specific to nabilone. The model also ranks related headache conditions highly: headache disorder, migraine with brainstem aura, and trigeminal autonomic cephalalgia. These likely reflect the same general migraine association.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease associations flagged by the knowledge graph (not label text):** The TxGNN knowledge graph lists contraindication links with the following conditions:
  - Cardiovascular: hypertension, hypotensive disorder, coronary artery disease, sudden cardiac arrest, heart conduction disease
  - Hepatic: liver failure
  - Psychiatric: anxiety disorder, neurotic disorder, dysthymic disorder, psychotic disorder, schizophreniform disorder, schizoaffective disorder, schizophrenia, mental disorder
  - Substance use: substance-related disorder, substance abuse/dependence

  Cannabinoid agonists may also worsen mania or psychosis risk.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were found, and all 20 listed in the data are rated Moderate. They include:
  - Opioids and CNS depressants: fentanyl, alfentanil, codeine, dihydrocodeine, tramadol, alprazolam, amobarbital, ethanol
  - Sedating antihistamines and antidepressants: chlorpheniramine, cetirizine, phenyltoloxamine, amitriptyline
  - Cardiovascular agents: nifedipine, amlodipine, acebutolol, alfuzosin, hydrochlorothiazide, amiloride
  - Other: aldesleukin, dichloralphenazone

  Additive sedation and blood pressure effects are the main concerns. This is a general reading and not a label statement.

For label warnings and formal contraindications, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is biologically plausible, but support is limited to an indirect class-level systematic review, with no registered trials and no nabilone-specific migraine data. The US license is cancelled, and the knowledge-graph contraindications (cardiovascular, psychiatric and substance-use conditions) are significant for a chronic condition like migraine. The most direct nabilone signal is a small preliminary trial in medication overuse headache, which supports further research but not a go decision.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- FDA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Nabilone-specific migraine studies or registered clinical trials
- A review of the 2026 systematic review to confirm what it reports for nabilone specifically
- Confirmation of the original indication and a clarification of the regulatory status (cancelled license) and available formulation
- A risk assessment for patients with cardiovascular, psychiatric or substance-use history, and a plan for managing CNS-depressant interactions
