# Doxycycline: From Antibacterial Therapy to Punctate Epithelial Keratoconjunctivitis

## One-Sentence Summary

Doxycycline is a tetracycline-class antibiotic that is widely marketed in the United States as oral tablets and capsules.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis**,
but **0 clinical trials** and only **1 publication** (a 1992 two-case report) currently support this direction, so the prediction rests almost entirely on the model.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Doxycycline is a tetracycline-class antibacterial with established activity against many bacteria, including *Chlamydia trachomatis*. Mechanistically, it may be applicable to punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis when an infectious trigger is involved.

The only supporting paper describes two patients with chlamydial follicular conjunctivitis. They were treated with oral tetracycline or doxycycline, and the follicles resolved. Both then developed recurrent punctate epithelial keratitis. The link is therefore treatment of the infectious cause, not a new mechanism of action. The report also shows the keratitis appeared after antibiotic treatment, so it does not show that doxycycline treats the keratitis itself.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The model score is very high, but the evidence is one 1992 two-case report with no clinical trials. That report does not show that doxycycline treats the keratitis. The safety data needed for screening are also missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking data gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data for doxycycline
- Controlled clinical evidence in punctate epithelial keratoconjunctivitis, for example from a targeted literature search
- Confirmation of a suitable route of administration, since only oral products are marketed
- As an alternative, the same Evidence Pack's "post-bacterial disorder" and "post-infectious syndrome" predictions have stronger trial support (L2). Examples are the completed Q fever fatigue syndrome trial NCT01318356 and the Phase 3 doxycycline host-directed therapy trial in tuberculosis NCT05473520. These may be worth reviewing first.
