## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

ChEMBL (EMBL-EBI) records the mechanism of action of prochlorperazine as: Dopamine D2 receptor antagonist ([ChEMBL CHEMBL728 (prochlorperazine): drug mechanism](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/chembl/explore/compound/CHEMBL728), CC BY-SA 3.0; accessed 2026-11-19.) Based on known information, prochlorperazine is a phenothiazine that acts as a dopamine D2 receptor antagonist. Its efficacy in schizophrenia and nausea is established, but nothing in the data compiled for this report connects it to inherited retinal dystrophy.

The high TxGNN score reflects a computational prediction only. No trials or literature corroborate it, and there is no supported mechanistic link between a symptomatic D2 antagonist and an inherited retinal degeneration.

The other top-ranked predictions are similarly unsupported: hydranencephaly, a congenital disorder of glycosylation, X-linked myopia, Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease type 1G, perisylvian polymicrogyria, syndromic myopia, X-linked female-limited myopia 26, and atypical glycine encephalopathy. All are rare congenital or genetic conditions with no plausible disease-modifying role for a D2 antagonist.

The one biologically plausible prediction is **manic bipolar affective disorder** (score 0.9998, rank 10). Its D2 antagonism is shared with antipsychotics used for acute mania and agitation. It is the closest to the original psychiatric use, but the supporting evidence is indirect (see below).

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindication-related conditions (from the knowledge graph, not label text):**
  - Cardiovascular: hypertension, orthostatic hypotension, long QT syndrome, torsades de pointes, coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction.
  - Neurological: epilepsy, basal ganglia disease, focal dystonia, blepharospasm.
  - Haematological: neutropenia, agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia.
  - Organ disease: liver, kidney and gallbladder disease.
  - Metabolic and other: hyperglycemia, obesity, benign prostatic hyperplasia, gastric ulcer, substance abuse/dependence, female breast carcinoma.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were recorded. Those listed include two **Major** ones: **bupropion** and **potassium citrate**. Moderate interactions include antidiabetic drugs (metformin, pioglitazone, alogliptin, acarbose, canagliflozin, chlorpropamide, albiglutide), anticholinergics (atropine, hyoscyamine, glycopyrronium), morphine, epinephrine, clarithromycin, loperamide and famotidine.
- Package-insert warnings and contraindications were not available for this report. Please refer to the package insert for the official safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top-ranked prediction (retinal dystrophy) is supported only by a model score, with no trials, no literature and no mechanistic link (Evidence Level L5). The only plausible direction, manic bipolar affective disorder, has only indirect, largely historical evidence (Level L4), and newer antipsychotics are established alternatives. The drug is marketed in the US with 11 valid licenses, and the interaction burden is large.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package-insert warnings and contraindications (currently a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data (for example from DrugBank)
- For bipolar mania: a systematic search for controlled efficacy data specific to prochlorperazine, followed by a comparison against current antipsychotics
- A mechanistic rationale and preclinical evidence before any further consideration of the retinal or other rare-disease predictions
