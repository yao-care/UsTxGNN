## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, isoflurane is a volatile anesthetic with vasoactive effects. Its efficacy as an anesthetic is established, and it may mechanistically influence vascular tone, which is relevant to vasospastic angina.

This link is speculative. The data supplied does not show isoflurane being evaluated as a treatment for vasospastic angina, and no mechanism could be verified. The prediction rests on the model score alone.

There is also a practical concern. An inhaled anesthetic given under monitored conditions is hard to reconcile with a chronic, recurrent condition like Prinzmetal angina, which is usually managed with outpatient oral therapy.
