# Budesonide: From Crohn's Disease and Asthma to Atopic Eczema

## One-Sentence Summary

Budesonide is a glucocorticoid originally used for Crohn's disease, inflammatory bowel disease, asthma and allergic rhinitis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **atopic eczema**, but there are **0 registered clinical trials** and only indirect literature (mostly allergy reviews, contact-allergy studies and one preclinical formulation study) supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, budesonide is a glucocorticoid with broad anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive activity, including suppression of Th2 cytokines and reduced eosinophil and mast cell activity. Its efficacy in allergic and inflammatory conditions such as asthma, allergic rhinitis and inflammatory bowel disease is established.

Atopic eczema is a type 2 inflammatory skin disease, so the mechanism is biologically plausible. Topical corticosteroids are already standard care for it. The very high TxGNN score likely reflects class similarity to approved topical corticosteroids rather than budesonide-specific evidence.

The retrieved literature is mostly indirect: allergy reviews, studies of contact allergy to corticosteroids, safety studies of growth and adrenal suppression, and a preclinical nanoparticle hydrogel formulation. Budesonide's incremental value over existing topical steroids would need a head-to-head rationale.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph (not label text):** hypertension, epilepsy, liver disease, gallbladder disease, hyperglycemia, congestive heart failure, measles, herpes simplex virus keratitis, urea cycle disorders (carbamoyl phosphate synthetase I deficiency, hyperargininemia, citrullinemia), and pituitary or growth hormone deficiency conditions (including hypopituitarism and Sheehan syndrome).
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were identified. Major-level examples include **adalimumab** and **fosamprenavir**. Moderate examples include zidovudine, isotretinoin, acebutolol, ketorolac, nifedipine, ibuprofen, ethinylestradiol and fluvoxamine. Minor examples include formoterol and salbutamol.
- **Class-related concerns in the literature:** contact allergy to corticosteroids, adrenal suppression, and growth effects in children with topical steroid use.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available for this report, so please refer to the package insert for label safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible, but there are no registered clinical trials and the literature is indirect or preclinical (evidence level L4). Topical corticosteroids are already standard care, so budesonide's added value is unproven. All US licenses are cancelled, and the label safety data are missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- FDA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- A targeted search for human efficacy data on topical budesonide in atopic dermatitis, plus a head-to-head rationale versus existing topical corticosteroids
- Route and formulation compatibility assessment, since no topical dermal product exists among the listed forms
- A pediatric safety plan covering adrenal suppression, growth and contact sensitization

**Other predictions of note:** Bronchitis (score 99.81%) has the stronger evidence base at L2, with completed Phase 2 trials of budesonide/formoterol in bronchiolitis obliterans and a Phase 4 trial in eosinophilic bronchitis. The evidence is adjacent rather than direct, and it may merit separate evaluation as a research question. The remaining predictions (polyps of various sites, epulis) are model-only or have tangential evidence and should be held.
