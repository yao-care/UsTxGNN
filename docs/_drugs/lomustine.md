# Lomustine: From Hodgkin Lymphoma and Brain Tumors to Lymphosarcoma

## One-Sentence Summary

Lomustine is an oral nitrosourea alkylating agent, originally used for Hodgkin lymphoma and brain tumors.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **Lymphosarcoma** (non-Hodgkin lymphoma), with **9 clinical trials** and **20 publications** currently supporting this direction. Most of this evidence comes from small Phase 2 combination-regimen studies and veterinary reports.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, lomustine is a lipophilic nitrosourea alkylating agent. It crosses the blood-brain barrier and cross-links DNA in rapidly dividing cells.

Its established use in Hodgkin lymphoma shows it is active against lymphoid malignancies. Lymphosarcoma (non-Hodgkin lymphoma) shares this biology, so activity is mechanistically plausible. Because the drug penetrates the CNS, it is also of particular interest in primary CNS lymphoma.

Most direct human evidence comes from oral combination regimens, such as lomustine + etoposide + cyclophosphamide + procarbazine in AIDS-related lymphoma. In these regimens lomustine's individual contribution cannot be isolated.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-level contraindications (from the knowledge graph, not label text)**: kidney disease, thrombocytopenia, leukopenia, hyperbilirubinemia (including Gilbert syndrome), drug-induced liver injury, pulmonary fibrosis, and infectious diseases (including fungal infections).
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were identified. Major ones include cimetidine, deferiprone, samarium (153Sm) lexidronam, and adalimumab. Moderate ones include mercaptopurine, zidovudine, chloramphenicol, aldesleukin, alemtuzumab, and several vaccines.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available and should be reviewed separately.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Human evidence for lymphosarcoma consists of small Phase 2 combination-regimen trials (the largest completed one enrolled 54 patients), several withdrawn or terminated trials, and mostly veterinary literature. All current US licenses are cancelled, and label safety data are missing. Lomustine's effect cannot be separated from its partner drugs. The Evidence Level (L2) is based on the highest-level evidence found, and it does not outweigh these gaps.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- FDA package insert warnings and contraindications
- Detailed mechanism of action data (MOA)
- Controlled human data isolating lomustine's contribution in non-Hodgkin or CNS lymphoma
- Clarification of regulatory and supply status, since both US licenses are cancelled
- Safety monitoring plan for myelosuppression and pulmonary and hepatic toxicity

*Other predictions in the data: spinal cord cancer (Phase 3 evidence is from pediatric glioma, so applicability is by extrapolation) and pediatric embryonal CNS tumors (supported by a large Phase 2/3 medulloblastoma trial) may merit separate evaluation.*
