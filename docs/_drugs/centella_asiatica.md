## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data for Centella asiatica is not available in the data compiled for this report. The plant's main constituents are triterpenoids such as asiatic acid, madecassic acid, asiaticoside and madecassoside. They have been studied mainly for wound healing, skin conditions and neuroprotective effects.

For insomnia, the only direct study is a 2024 zebrafish larvae experiment. It reported that an ethanol extract of Centella asiatica was associated with reduced orexin, ERK, Akt and p38 signalling in an insomnia model. Orexin is a wake-promoting neuropeptide, so this offers a hypothesis-level link to sleep regulation. The study has not been replicated in mammals or humans, and the high TxGNN score is a computational prediction only.

Further indirect hints come from mouse and rat work on anxiety-like behaviour and sleep-deprivation-induced stress, but these address other conditions, not insomnia itself, and one 2025 mouse study found no anxiolytic contribution from the main constituents.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only study that directly addresses insomnia is a single zebrafish experiment; there are no clinical trials and no mammalian or human data. The evidence is therefore too limited, in a model-prediction-led setting, to support moving forward.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mammalian sleep studies (e.g., rodent sleep architecture) with a standardized, characterized extract
- Mechanism-of-action data linking Centella asiatica constituents to sleep or orexin pathways
- Package insert warnings and contraindications, and a safety review for oral use, since current US products are topical
- A defined route and formulation suitable for a sleep indication
