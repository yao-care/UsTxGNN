## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

According to the Mechanism of Action section of the FDA-approved prescribing information for amantadine: "The mechanism by which amantadine exerts efficacy in the treatment of dyskinesia in patients with Parkinson's disease or as adjunctive treatment to levodopa/carbidopa in patients with Parkinson's disease experiencing 'off' episodes is unknown. Amantadine is a weak uncompetitive antagonist of the NMDA receptor." ([DailyMed: Gocovri (amantadine) prescribing information, Adamas Pharma, LLC](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=82ff865f-78f3-4c94-b6a9-727a54944b28), label version 2026-10-05; accessed 2026-11-19.) Amantadine is generally known as an NMDA receptor antagonist with dopaminergic effects, and this general pharmacology is the basis for the proposed link.

Rasmussen encephalitis is a rare, immune-mediated brain disease that causes progressive neuronal injury and intractable seizures. Glutamate-related excitotoxicity is thought to contribute to the neuronal damage, so NMDA receptor blockade offers a plausible but purely theoretical rationale.

This link is hypothetical. No registered trials, case reports or other studies were provided to show that amantadine helps patients with this condition, and the high score alone should not be read as evidence of efficacy.
