# Thioridazine: From Antipsychotic Use to Manic Bipolar Affective Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Thioridazine is a phenothiazine antipsychotic whose US licences are now all cancelled. The data compiled for this report contain no approved-indication text for it.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **manic bipolar affective disorder**, but **no clinical trials** are registered for this indication and the **20 publications** retrieved are mostly general reviews, safety reports and old case reports.
The evidence is weak, and thioridazine carries a serious cardiac (QTc) safety concern.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data are not available. Thioridazine is a phenothiazine antipsychotic, and the class acts mainly by blocking D2 dopamine receptors. That mechanism is plausible for acute mania, as with other antipsychotics.

Supporting hints in the literature are thin:
- A 1980 study reported that the dopamine blocker pimozide produced clinical improvement in manic patients similar to that seen with chlorpromazine and thioridazine.
- A 1964 French report described combined thioridazine, barbiturate and electroshock treatment of psychomotor excitation and manic reactions.
- A 1993 single-patient study described a woman with schizoaffective mania maintained on thioridazine.

None of these is a thioridazine-specific controlled study in mania. The high TxGNN score is a computational association, not proof of efficacy.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-level cautions (knowledge graph):** The drug is linked to contraindication or caution signals in the following conditions:
  - Cardiac and circulatory: long QT syndrome (including the familial form), orthostatic hypotension, postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome, and multiple system atrophy.
  - Neurological: epilepsy, Alzheimer disease, neuroleptic malignant syndrome, tardive dyskinesia, and lingual-facial-buccal dyskinesia.
  - Blood and metabolic: hematologic disease and potassium deficiency disease.

  These come from a knowledge graph, not from label text, and should be verified against the package insert.
- **Cardiac risk:** A 1971 report describes recurrent major ventricular arrhythmias with thioridazine, consistent with the QTc concern noted for this drug.
- **Drug interactions:** 100 interactions were found. Major-level interactions include **bupropion, morphine and lorcaserin**. Moderate-level interactions include antidiabetic drugs (acarbose, metformin, pioglitazone, alogliptin, canagliflozin, chlorpropamide, albiglutide), anticholinergics (atropine, hyoscyamine, glycopyrronium), epinephrine, hydrocortisone, famotidine, amphotericin B, loperamide and bisacodyl.
- **Other literature signals:** Combination with lithium has been reported to cause neurotoxicity. Combination with amitriptyline has been reported to cause severe toxicity.

Please refer to the package insert for the official warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is mechanistically plausible (D2 antagonism), but there are no clinical trials and no thioridazine-specific controlled evidence in mania. The serious QTc and arrhythmia risk is a major concern in this population, and all US licences are cancelled. Better-established antimanic agents are available.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Official package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening).
- Mechanism of action data, for example from DrugBank.
- Thioridazine-specific controlled efficacy data in acute mania.
- A cardiac safety plan covering QTc monitoring, electrolytes, and avoidance of QT-prolonging and CYP2D6-interacting drugs.
- Clarification of regulatory pathway and supply, given that no US licence is currently active.
