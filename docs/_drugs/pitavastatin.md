# Pitavastatin: From Hyperlipidemia to Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolemia

## One-Sentence Summary

Pitavastatin is a statin (an HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor) marketed in the US for lowering cholesterol.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HoFH)**.
Currently **0 clinical trials** and **2 indirect publications** support this direction, and neither tested pitavastatin in genetically confirmed HoFH.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Pitavastatin inhibits HMG-CoA reductase and upregulates hepatic LDL receptors, which lowers circulating LDL cholesterol. Its lipid-lowering use in common hypercholesterolemia is well established.

HoFH is a severe form of familial hypercholesterolemia, usually caused by profound LDL receptor dysfunction. Because statins work largely by increasing LDL receptor activity, their efficacy in HoFH is expected to be limited. Patients with residual receptor function may still respond partially.

The very high TxGNN score probably reflects graph proximity to the hypercholesterolemia cluster, not HoFH-specific evidence. The prediction is therefore mechanistically plausible but unconfirmed.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction rests on model score alone. No registered trial exists, and the two retrieved papers do not test pitavastatin in genetically confirmed HoFH. HoFH usually involves severe LDL receptor dysfunction, so statin efficacy is mechanistically expected to be limited.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Pitavastatin data in genotype-confirmed HoFH, such as trials or registries stratified by residual LDL receptor activity
- The FDA package insert warnings and contraindications, and the original approved indications
- A clear comparison with established HoFH options that do not depend on LDL receptor function

Other candidates in the same evidence pack are better supported. Cardiovascular prevention in people with HIV is backed by the REPRIEVE Phase 3 trial (NCT02344290, n=7,769) and reaches L1. That indication is a better candidate for further evaluation.
