## Raltegravir: From HIV Infection to Simian Immunodeficiency Virus Infection
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Raltegravir is an HIV-1 integrase inhibitor, originally used to treat HIV infection and AIDS.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **simian immunodeficiency virus infection**, but this is an animal-model pathogen rather than a human disease.
Currently there are **0 clinical trials** and **15 publications** (mostly preclinical) supporting this direction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, raltegravir is an integrase strand transfer inhibitor (INSTI). It blocks the step in which viral DNA is integrated into the host genome.

SIV is a close lentiviral relative of HIV-1, and its integrase is highly conserved. This makes it plausible that raltegravir acts on SIV in the same way. Studies in rhesus macaques and in cell culture (SIVmac251 and SIVmac239) show that raltegravir is active against SIV and that resistance mutations resemble those seen in HIV.

An important caveat is that SIV is a pathogen of non-human primates and a research model, not a human disease. The prediction therefore supports using raltegravir in preclinical HIV research models. It does not point to a new human indication.

The other predictions are weaker:
- **Feline acquired immunodeficiency syndrome** (score 0.9978): a veterinary condition with no trials or literature. The score probably reflects shared lentiviral classification.
- **Neurodevelopmental disorder with ataxic gait, absent speech, and decreased cortical white matter** (score 0.9977): no credible mechanistic link, and likely a TxGNN model (deep learning) artifact.

## Safety Considerations

- **Knowledge-graph contraindication signals** (not from label text): myopathy, muscular disease, skeletal muscle disease, familial periodic paralysis, rhabdomyolysis, and nodular fasciitis.
- **Drug Interactions**: 36 interactions were identified in total. The 20 listed in the compiled data are summarized below.
  - **Major**: zinc sulfate, zinc acetate, aluminum hydroxide, calcium carbonate. These are polyvalent cation products and antacids, which can chelate integrase inhibitors and reduce absorption.
  - **Moderate**: magnesium oxide, magnesium sulfate, magnesium chloride, orlistat.
  - **Minor**: acid-suppressing agents (famotidine, ranitidine, cimetidine, nizatidine, omeprazole, esomeprazole, lansoprazole, dexlansoprazole, pantoprazole, rabeprazole), pioglitazone, and dexamethasone.
- A preclinical study also suggested possible adipose tissue and insulin resistance effects (PMID 32166319).

Please refer to the package insert for label warnings and contraindications, which are not available in the compiled data.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The evidence is limited to preclinical studies (L4), with no clinical trials. SIV is an animal-model virus, not a human disease, so this prediction does not translate into a human repurposing opportunity. Raltegravir's marketed HIV use is already established, and all three of its US licenses in the data are valid.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Redefine the goal. SIV-related findings are best used to support preclinical HIV research models, not a new human indication.
- Obtain the FDA package insert warnings and contraindications. This is a blocking gap for safety screening.
- Obtain the mechanism of action data from DrugBank.
- Confirm current US marketing status; all three listed licenses are valid.
- Do not pursue feline AIDS or the neurodevelopmental disorder prediction without new evidence, since no supporting data or credible mechanism was found.
