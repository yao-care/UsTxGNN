# Cefoxitin: From Bacterial Infections to Infectious Otitis Media

## One-Sentence Summary

Cefoxitin is a cephamycin-class injectable antibiotic that is marketed in the United States. The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **infectious otitis media**, but there are currently **0 registered clinical trials** and only **2 publications**, a case report and a review. Neither publication tests cefoxitin as a treatment, so this remains a model-only prediction.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in DrugBank for this record. From known pharmacology, cefoxitin is a cephamycin beta-lactam that inhibits bacterial cell wall synthesis by binding penicillin-binding proteins (PBPs). It also resists many beta-lactamases. Bacterial otitis media is caused by organisms that a cell-wall-active antibiotic can target, so the prediction is biologically plausible.

The support is much weaker than the score suggests. The two retrieved papers are a *Mycobacterium fortuitum* case report and an MRSA review. Neither evaluates cefoxitin as a treatment for otitis media. MRSA is intrinsically resistant to cefoxitin, and cefoxitin is mainly used as a laboratory marker of methicillin resistance. The prediction therefore looks like graph-based proximity to other infection indications rather than a demonstrated treatment effect.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The 99.30% score is not backed by any registered trial or by literature that tests cefoxitin for otitis media. The two papers are indirect, and MRSA is intrinsically cefoxitin-resistant. The evidence level is L4, and the package-insert safety review is still blocking.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (FDA label), which are currently a blocking data gap
- Detailed mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Comparative or controlled clinical data on cefoxitin for otitis media, or a targeted literature search for it
- Dosing and route evaluation for the ear indication, since route compatibility has not been assessed (cefoxitin is available only as an injectable)
- Note that other predicted indications for cefoxitin have stronger evidence than otitis media. Gonococcal urethritis has multiple 1979-1983 clinical studies, and urinary tract infection has small registered trials. These may be better candidates for follow-up.
