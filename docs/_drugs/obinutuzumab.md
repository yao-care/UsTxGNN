# Obinutuzumab: From Acute Lymphoblastic Leukemia and Richter Syndrome to Follicular Lymphoma

> **Note on candidate selection:** The two highest-scoring predictions (99.21% each) are molecular subtypes of chronic lymphocytic leukemia/small lymphocytic lymphoma (CLL/SLL): pregerminal center CLL/SLL and IGHV-mutated CLL/SLL. No trials or publications were compiled for either, and they are nomenclature subtypes of a disease where obinutuzumab is already established. They are not distinct repurposing opportunities. This report therefore focuses on **follicular lymphoma** (99.18%), the highest-ranked prediction with substantial supporting evidence.

## One-Sentence Summary

Obinutuzumab is an anti-CD20 antibody, associated in the source knowledge graph with acute lymphoblastic leukemia and Richter syndrome.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **follicular lymphoma**, with **more than 40 registered clinical trials** and **19 publications**, including several Phase 3 and Phase 2 randomized studies, supporting this direction.
Follicular lymphoma is likely already an established obinutuzumab use, so this is better described as confirmation of a known use than as novel repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the compiled data. Based on the literature, obinutuzumab is a glycoengineered, type II anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody. Compared with rituximab, it produces enhanced direct cell death and greater antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity and phagocytosis.

Follicular lymphoma is a CD20-positive B-cell malignancy, so the drug's target matches the disease directly. The original leukemia-type indications are also B-lymphoid malignancies, which makes the prediction biologically coherent.

Phase 3 data (GALLIUM) support first-line use in follicular lymphoma. Phase 2 studies (GALEN, ROSEWOOD) support combinations in relapsed/refractory disease. The "Cancelled only" US market status should be verified, since it may reflect one product listing rather than the drug's overall availability.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions:** 81 interactions were identified.
  - **Major:** deferiprone, samarium (153Sm) lexidronam, adalimumab, baricitinib, certolizumab pegol, cladribine, clozapine.
  - **Moderate:** other immunosuppressive or biologic agents (e.g., alemtuzumab, azathioprine, anakinra, canakinumab), several vaccines including live and inactivated types, chloramphenicol, zidovudine, roflumilast and strontium chloride Sr-89.
  - Most of these likely reflect additive immunosuppression or myelosuppression, and vaccine interactions need particular attention.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Obinutuzumab's anti-CD20 mechanism directly matches follicular lymphoma, and randomized evidence (GALLIUM, ROSEWOOD) plus a large trial pipeline support its use. Follicular lymphoma is probably an established indication, so the main work is confirming regulatory status rather than proving efficacy. The two top-ranked CLL/SLL subtype predictions have no subtype-specific evidence and should stay on **Hold**.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (required before safety screening)
- Detailed mechanism-of-action data
- Verification of the "Cancelled only" US market status and the approved indication text for BLA125486
- A review of the major interaction pairs, especially with other immunosuppressants and vaccines, for the intended patient population
- Confirmation that the Phase 3 evidence (GALLIUM, GADOLIN) is accepted for the intended population and setting
