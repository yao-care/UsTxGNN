The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **variably protease-sensitive prionopathy (VPSPr)**,

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available from the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, droxidopa is a synthetic precursor of norepinephrine, converted by aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase (DOPA decarboxylase). Its established role is in the noradrenergic system, where it helps treat neurogenic orthostatic hypotension in conditions such as Parkinson disease, multiple system atrophy and related autonomic failure.

The link to VPSPr is weak. VPSPr is a rare sporadic prion disease involving misfolded, partially protease-sensitive prion protein. Noradrenergic replacement does not target prion protein misfolding, aggregation or clearance, and no plausible mechanistic connection is documented.

The very high TxGNN score is a computational prediction from the TxGNN model (deep learning). It may reflect similarities the model learned between related diseases, such as droxidopa's neurodegenerative and parkinsonian indications and VPSPr, not biological evidence. No clinical or experimental data back it. At best, droxidopa could be considered for symptomatic management of autonomic dysfunction in a prion disease patient. That would not modify the disease and is unsupported by evidence.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The prediction is model-only (evidence level L5) with no clinical trials or publications, and no plausible mechanistic link between noradrenergic replacement and prion protein pathology. The high TxGNN score alone is not enough to justify further investment. The drug is marketed in the US, but package insert safety data are missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
