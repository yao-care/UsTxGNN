# Donepezil: From Alzheimer Disease to Chronic Tic Disorder

## One-Sentence Summary

Donepezil is a cholinesterase inhibitor originally used to treat Alzheimer disease.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **psychogenic movement disorders** (highest score). However, the best-supported prediction is **chronic tic disorder**, which has **0 registered clinical trials** and **5 publications** (mostly small, uncontrolled or preclinical studies).

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, donepezil is a cholinesterase inhibitor that raises synaptic acetylcholine. Its efficacy in Alzheimer disease is established, and mechanistically it may be applicable to disorders involving cholinergic signalling.

**Top-ranked prediction (psychogenic movement disorders).** The score is very high (0.992), but no trials or publications were found. A possible link is cholinergic modulation of basal ganglia motor circuits, but this is speculative. Psychogenic movement disorders have no established pharmacologic basis for cholinesterase inhibition.

**Best-supported prediction (chronic tic disorder).** Donepezil may modulate striatal cholinergic interneurons and the dopaminergic/serotonergic balance implicated in tic generation. Mouse studies show donepezil affects the DOI-induced head twitch response, a model of tics, which supports biological plausibility. Clinical support is limited to small uncontrolled reports in children with tics and ADHD.

**Other predictions** in the movement-disorder family (extrapyramidal and movement disease, lingual-facial-buccal dyskinesia) rest on the idea of cholinergic-dopaminergic imbalance. The literature is mixed, as cholinesterase inhibitors can also induce or worsen movement symptoms.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease associations from the knowledge graph (not label text):** Donepezil is flagged as contraindicated or to be used with caution in epilepsy, asthma (including intrinsic and allergic asthma), bladder neck obstruction, active peptic ulcer disease, sick sinus syndrome (including the familial form) and atrioventricular block.
- **Movement-disorder safety signal:** Case reports, a pharmacovigilance study and a 2025 systematic review indicate cholinesterase inhibitors can induce or worsen movement symptoms (for example Pisa syndrome and jaw tremor). This is directly relevant to any repurposing for movement disorders.
- **Drug interactions (100 recorded; examples):**
  - Major: bupropion.
  - Moderate: anticholinergics (atropine, hyoscyamine, glycopyrronium, dicyclomine, clidinium), H2 blockers (famotidine, ranitidine, cimetidine), loperamide, dexamethasone, metronidazole, dolasetron.
  - Minor: aspirin, aprepitant, clarithromycin, lorcaserin.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available; please refer to the package insert for full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold** (psychogenic movement disorders and other prediction-only candidates); **Research Question** for chronic tic disorder.

**Rationale:**
- The top-scoring prediction has no clinical or literature evidence and no established pharmacologic basis.
- Chronic tic disorder has plausible biology and small uncontrolled pediatric reports, but no controlled trials. Movement-disorder indications also carry a safety signal, as cholinesterase inhibitors can cause or worsen extrapyramidal symptoms.
- All US licenses are cancelled, which affects practical development.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Mechanism of action data for donepezil
- Package insert warnings and contraindications
- A properly powered, randomized controlled trial in chronic tic disorder (children and adolescents)
- Review of the movement-disorder safety signal before any further development
- Assessment of route compatibility and the market and licensing situation
