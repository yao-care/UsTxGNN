# Nisoldipine: From Hypertension to Pulmonary Hypertension Owing to Lung Disease and/or Hypoxia

## One-Sentence Summary

Nisoldipine is a dihydropyridine calcium channel blocker, originally used to treat hypertension.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **pulmonary hypertension owing to lung disease and/or hypoxia**,
but **no clinical trials** are registered and the 19 retrieved publications are almost entirely **preclinical**, so support is indirect.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, nisoldipine is a dihydropyridine L-type calcium channel blocker. Its efficacy in hypertension rests on relaxing vascular smooth muscle, and mechanistically this may be applicable to the pulmonary circulation.

Hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction depends partly on calcium influx through L-type channels and on calcium release from intracellular stores in pulmonary arterial smooth muscle. This gives a plausible link between calcium channel blockade and the predicted indication. Older animal work, such as a 1985 canine study comparing nisoldipine, nifedipine and bepridil in experimental pulmonary hypertension, is consistent with this idea.

There is an important caution. Calcium channel blockers are generally not recommended for pulmonary hypertension associated with lung disease or hypoxia, because they may worsen ventilation/perfusion mismatch and cause systemic hypotension. The high TxGNN score therefore should not be read as clinical support.

Other predictions in the list are weaker:
- Pulmonary hypertension with unclear multifactorial mechanism: computational prediction only (L5).
- Malignant renovascular hypertension: only a general 1987 review, and the condition is an emergency usually managed with revascularization and intravenous agents (L4).
- Malignant hypertensive renal disease: computational prediction only (L5).
- Braddock syndrome: no clear mechanistic rationale (L5).

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanistic link is plausible, but the evidence is limited to old preclinical studies (L4) with no registered clinical trials. Calcium channel blockers are generally not recommended in hypoxia-related pulmonary hypertension, and the only US authorization is cancelled. The other four predictions rest on computational scores alone or on non-specific references.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- FDA package insert warnings and contraindications, which are currently missing and block safety screening
- Mechanism of action data (for example, from DrugBank)
- Clinical evidence in pulmonary hypertension, ideally human hemodynamic or controlled studies, addressing the risk of worsened V/Q mismatch and hypotension
- Review of the drug's cancelled market status and the availability of the extended-release formulation
- A comparison against current standard therapy for pulmonary hypertension associated with lung disease
