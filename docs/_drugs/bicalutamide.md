## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Bicalutamide is, however, known as a non-steroidal androgen receptor (AR) antagonist. Androgen signaling influences hair follicle activity, so blocking the AR is biologically plausible for conditions of excess hair growth.

The only linked publication is a 2022 comment on a retrospective review of bicalutamide for minoxidil-induced hypertrichosis in female pattern hair loss. That is indirect: it concerns a drug-induced, acquired form of hypertrichosis in a specific setting. It does not show that bicalutamide treats hypertrichosis in general, and it does not address congenital or generalized forms. The comment letter has no abstract available, and the underlying study is not part of the data compiled here. Because no cited study directly establishes efficacy in the predicted disease, the evidence level is held at L5.
