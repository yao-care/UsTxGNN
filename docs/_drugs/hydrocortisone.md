## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available for this drug. Based on general pharmacology, hydrocortisone is a glucocorticoid with anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive actions.

Alopecia areata is an autoimmune disease in which T-cell-mediated attack on hair follicles follows a collapse of follicular immune privilege. A drug that suppresses this immune response is biologically plausible, and topical and intralesional corticosteroids are an established treatment class for the condition.

There is an important caveat. In the available Phase 3 trial, low-potency hydrocortisone 1% cream was the comparator and not the test therapy. It appears less effective than the higher-potency steroid, clobetasol. The prediction therefore rests more on the drug class than on direct proof that hydrocortisone works.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The only Phase 3 trial used hydrocortisone 1% as a low-potency comparator, and its result favored a stronger steroid. The remaining support is mostly old case reports, so the model's high score is not backed by direct proof of benefit.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A trial that tests hydrocortisone itself (by topical or intralesional route) in alopecia areata, with a comparison against higher-potency corticosteroids
- Mechanism of action data
- Label warnings and contraindications from the package insert
- A safety plan for long-term or scalp use, especially in children
