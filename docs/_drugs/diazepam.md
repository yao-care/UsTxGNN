## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism-of-action data are not available for this drug in the compiled data. The literature does describe diazepam as a positive allosteric modulator of GABA-A receptors. By enhancing inhibitory neurotransmission, it produces sedative and hypnotic effects.

This mechanism fits insomnia, because benzodiazepines are used clinically as hypnotics. A 1981 double-blind trial studied diazepam directly in outpatients with sleep disorders, which is the strongest direct support found.

The same evidence also shows the limits of this idea. In that trial, diazepam was compared with another benzodiazepine, not with placebo. The more recent literature mostly discusses class-wide risks: dependence, altered sleep architecture, and a clinical push towards reducing benzodiazepine use rather than expanding it.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interacting drugs are on record.
  - **Major**: morphine (additive central nervous system and respiratory depression is the usual concern).
  - **Moderate**: bupropion, aprepitant, omeprazole, esomeprazole, cimetidine, clarithromycin, cisapride, clotrimazole, miconazole, dronabinol, metoclopramide.
  - **Minor**: dexamethasone, betamethasone, and several antacids (magnesium oxide, aluminum hydroxide, calcium carbonate, magaldrate, magnesium carbonate, magnesium hydroxide).
- **Literature-reported concerns**: Dependence and tolerance, altered sleep architecture, cognitive decline and falls in older adults, and a possible association with higher breast cancer risk with long-term use.

Please refer to the package insert for warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The mechanism is plausible and one double-blind trial in insomnia patients exists (L2). However, that trial compared diazepam with another benzodiazepine, not with placebo, and diazepam did worse on the sleep measures reported. The wider literature stresses dependence and long-term harms, and newer evidence focuses on reducing benzodiazepine use.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Placebo-controlled or modern active-comparator trials of diazepam in insomnia, with clear dosing and duration limits
- Direct comparison with current first-line options for insomnia
- Detailed mechanism of action data
- Package insert warnings and contraindications
- A risk plan covering dependence, falls and cognitive effects, especially in older adults
