## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. Oxymetazoline is generally known as a topical alpha-adrenergic agonist (mainly alpha-1/alpha-2A). It constricts the blood vessels of the nasal mucosa, which reduces swelling and congestion. That mechanism fits diseases of the nasal cavity well.

Because every US product listed is a nasal spray and the label text is missing, the "new" indication may already be an approved use (nasal congestion). If so, the prediction is confirmation rather than repurposing. The label should be checked before this is treated as a new indication.

The trial evidence supports the mechanism only indirectly. Oxymetazoline mostly appears as a procedural decongestant, for example before nasoendoscopy or sinus surgery, not as the drug being tested for a nasal disease endpoint. That is why the evidence level is capped at L3.
