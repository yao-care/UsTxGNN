# Danazol: From Endometriosis to Amenorrhea (Menstrual Suppression)

## One-Sentence Summary

Danazol is an oral synthetic steroid approved by the FDA for endometriosis, benign fibrocystic breast disease and hereditary angioedema.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be useful for **amenorrhea**, which is better understood as menstrual suppression than as a disease to treat.
There are **0 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications**, mostly older studies, reviews and one 2024 retrospective cohort.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the Evidence Pack. General pharmacology from the literature applies instead. Danazol inhibits gonadotropins, suppresses gametogenesis and steroidogenesis, and has direct effects on ovarian and adrenal enzyme systems (PMID 2404115). It is also androgenic, and it suppresses ovarian function (PMID 6819580).

Amenorrhea is therefore an expected pharmacologic effect of danazol, not a separate disease target. In endometriosis, lesions become inactive and regress during amenorrhea (PMID 16280355). This is why the knowledge-graph link probably appears. A 2024 retrospective cohort reports danazol used for menstrual suppression in transgender and nonbinary individuals (PMID 39051650). The prediction is plausible, but it is best treated as a therapeutic endpoint. Examples are menstrual suppression, endometriosis and abnormal uterine bleeding.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information. The Evidence Pack has no label warnings or contraindications, and the drug interaction query found nothing.

Two signals appear in the literature only and are not from the label. Danazol has androgenic side effects, such as pigmentation of vellus hairs and voice changes (PMID 39051650). A case report describes rhabdomyolysis and pancreatitis when danazol 600 mg/day was combined with lovastatin 40 mg/day (PMID 18691993).

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Amenorrhea is an expected pharmacologic effect, so the prediction confirms danazol's known hormonal suppression and does not point to a new disease. There are no registered trials and no completed Phase 3 RCTs, so evidence is L3. The package insert safety data is missing, which blocks the safety screening step.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- The package insert warnings and contraindications for the US danazol products
- Mechanism of action data, for example from DrugBank
- A decision on whether to pursue menstrual suppression, endometriosis or abnormal uterine bleeding as the indication. Benign mammary dysplasia (rank 2) has a randomized double-blind trial of 80 women (PMID 3074777) and may be the stronger repurposing candidate.
- A benefit-risk assessment of androgenic adverse effects for any menstrual suppression use
