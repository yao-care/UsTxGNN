# Tryptophan: From Amino Acid Supplement (No Approved Indication on Record) to Restless Legs Syndrome

## One-Sentence Summary

Tryptophan is an essential amino acid and a precursor of serotonin and melatonin. The US records available here list it only as liquid supplement-type products with no approved indication text.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **restless legs syndrome (RLS)**, but there are **no registered clinical trials** and only **2 small 1980s studies** that directly examine tryptophan.
Evidence is early-stage and unreplicated.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on known information, tryptophan is the dietary precursor of serotonin and melatonin. It also interacts with dopaminergic pathways thought to be involved in RLS and periodic limb movements.

No original indication is recorded, so the relationship between an original and a new indication cannot be assessed. The mechanistic argument rests on neurotransmitter biology. RLS is believed to involve the dopaminergic system, as shown by its response to dopamine agonists, and serotonergic signalling is plausibly linked to it.

The direction of effect is uncertain. Retrieved reports describe RLS and movement disorders arising during treatment with serotonergic antidepressants and with lithium. These papers show that serotonergic modulation is associated with RLS, but not that tryptophan helps. Two 1980s papers (PMID 3953904, 3659737) examine tryptophan directly. Their designs cannot be confirmed from the supplied data, and they appear small and unreplicated.

The same model also ranks insomnia, glaucoma and methemoglobinemia-related conditions among its top predictions. Insomnia has the most supporting material (L3), including a 1983 comparative study against flurazepam and secobarbital. The glaucoma, methemoglobinemia and prothrombin-deficiency candidates have no plausible mechanistic link in the supplied data.

## Safety Considerations

Please refer to the package insert for safety information.

One safety-relevant item appears in the retrieved records. A registered NCT record (NCT00001918) states that in 1989 more than 1,500 people taking L-tryptophan supplements developed eosinophilia-myalgia syndrome, and that impurities in the supplements are suspected. Product quality and purity are therefore a safety consideration for any tryptophan product.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The RLS prediction has a high model score but no registered trials, and its only direct evidence is two unreplicated 1980s studies of unconfirmed design. Product safety information is also missing, which is a blocking gap for safety screening.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (blocking data gap)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Full texts of PMID 3953904 and 3659737 to confirm design, dose, sample size and results
- A search for modern controlled trials of tryptophan or 5-HTP in RLS
- Review of whether serotonergic effects could worsen RLS, given the antidepressant and lithium reports
- Confirmation of the regulatory status of the listed products, since no authorization numbers or approved indications are recorded
- Comparison with the insomnia candidate, which has stronger supporting material but needs its guideline position verified. The pack's own rationale notes that the 2017 AASM guideline may advise against tryptophan for chronic insomnia.
