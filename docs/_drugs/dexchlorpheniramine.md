# Dexchlorpheniramine: From Allergic Rhinitis and Urticaria to Allergic Urticaria

## One-Sentence Summary

Dexchlorpheniramine is a first-generation antihistamine, originally used for allergic rhinitis, conjunctivitis, urticaria and angioedema.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **allergic urticaria**, with **0 registered clinical trials** and **3 publications** (two histamine-challenge pharmacodynamic studies and one 1979 case-based study) currently related to this direction.
Because urticaria is already among its original indications, this is closer to an on-label indication than a novel repurposing target.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known pharmacology, dexchlorpheniramine is a first-generation H1 receptor antagonist (inverse agonist).

Allergic urticaria is driven by mast cell histamine release acting on H1 receptors in skin blood vessels and sensory nerves, producing wheals and flare. Blocking H1 receptors is therefore a direct mechanistic fit.

The drug's original indications already include urticaria and angioedema, which is consistent with the very high TxGNN score. This prediction mainly confirms an existing use rather than revealing a new one.

A second prediction, **cold urticaria** (score 99.61%), has no specific trials or publications in the compiled data. Its rationale rests on class effect: H1 antagonists are a standard symptomatic approach across urticaria types. Its evidence level is L4 and the recommendation is Hold.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
H1 blockade clearly fits allergic urticaria, and urticaria is already an original indication, so there is little novelty in this prediction. There are no registered clinical trials, the available literature is limited to histamine-challenge and case-based studies, and no US license is currently valid.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- FDA package insert warnings and contraindications (currently missing, which blocks safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data from DrugBank
- Clinical efficacy trials in patients with urticaria, including cold urticaria
- Confirmation of the current US regulatory status and available formulations, since both licenses are cancelled
