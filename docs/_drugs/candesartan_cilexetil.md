## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

According to the Mechanism of Action section of the FDA-approved prescribing information for candesartan cilexetil: "Angiotensin II is formed from angiotensin I in a reaction catalyzed by angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE, kininase II). Angiotensin II is the principal pressor agent of the renin-angiotensin system, with effects that include vasoconstriction, stimulation of synthesis and release of aldosterone, cardiac stimulation, and renal reabsorption of sodium. […] Candesartan blocks the vasoconstrictor and aldosterone-secreting effects of angiotensin II by selectively blocking the binding of angiotensin II to the AT 1 receptor in many tissues, such as vascular smooth muscle and the adrenal gland." ([DailyMed: Atacand (candesartan cilexetil) prescribing information, ANI Pharmaceuticals, Inc.](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=94a59260-d2b6-464a-b5b9-bbc599e57e10), label version 2025-12-22; accessed 2026-11-19.) Based on the known drug class, candesartan blocks the angiotensin II type 1 (AT1) receptor, which lowers blood pressure by limiting angiotensin II-driven vasoconstriction.

Malignant hypertension can injure the small blood vessels of the kidney and raise pressure inside the glomeruli. Blocking AT1 receptors could plausibly reduce this vascular and glomerular stress, which gives a biologically reasonable link between the drug and this kidney condition.

This link is a prediction only. No trial or publication in the compiled data studies candesartan in malignant hypertensive renal disease, so the rationale has not been tested directly.
