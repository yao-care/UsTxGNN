# Simvastatin: From Hyperlipidemia and Atherosclerotic Disease to Familial Hypercholesterolemia

## One-Sentence Summary

Simvastatin is a statin (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor) originally used for hyperlipidemia, hypertriglyceridemia, atherosclerosis and stroke-related conditions.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **familial hypercholesterolemia**, with **8 clinical trials** and **19 publications** currently supporting this direction.
The prediction largely overlaps with the drug's existing uses (homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia is already among its listed indications), so it is closer to on-label use than true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Simvastatin inhibits HMG-CoA reductase, which lowers hepatic cholesterol synthesis and upregulates LDL receptors. This increases LDL-C clearance from the blood. Detailed mechanism-of-action data were not available in the data compiled for this report, but this is the well-established pharmacology of the statin class.

Familial hypercholesterolemia is a genetic disorder of LDL-C clearance, and statins act on the same pathway. The disease is already close to simvastatin's existing indications (homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia and hyperlipoproteinemia). The prediction is therefore mechanistically sound but is not a novel repurposing opportunity.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications and disease associations (knowledge graph, not label text):** The knowledge graph flags a number of conditions, including liver disease, pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, myopathy and other muscle diseases, rhabdomyolysis, diabetes and hyperglycemia, diabetic ketoacidosis, gout, thrombocytopenia, blood coagulation disease, peptic ulcer disease, hypotensive disorder, substance abuse/dependence, and several coronary conditions (acute coronary syndrome, coronary artery disease, myocardial infarction). These need verification against the current package insert.
- **Drug Interactions:** 100 interactions were recorded. Major-level ones include **amiodarone, amlodipine and fenofibrate**. Moderate-level ones include nifedipine, omeprazole, paclitaxel, aprepitant, alpelisib, apalutamide, zidovudine and ethanol. Strong CYP3A4 inhibitors, such as ritonavir- or cobicistat-boosted HIV regimens, are of particular concern for myopathy and rhabdomyolysis.
- **Dose-related risk:** The 80 mg dose carries a higher myopathy risk. Pediatric dosing and heterozygous versus homozygous disease also need attention.

Package insert warnings and contraindications were not available in the data compiled for this report. Please refer to the package insert for the full safety information.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Multiple completed Phase 3 trials and a large body of literature support simvastatin in familial hypercholesterolemia, and the mechanism is well understood. However, this is largely an existing use rather than new repurposing, and the supporting trials mostly test simvastatin in combination or as background therapy. Safety information from the package insert is missing.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank
- Confirmation that a valid US licence exists, since all six listed licences are cancelled
- Guardrails for heterozygous vs homozygous disease, pediatric dosing, high-dose myopathy risk, and CYP3A4 interactions
