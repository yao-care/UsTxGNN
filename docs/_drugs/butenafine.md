## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Butenafine belongs to the benzylamine class of antifungals, which act by inhibiting squalene epoxidase and so disrupting fungal cell membrane synthesis. Its efficacy against dermatophyte skin infections is the basis for the prediction.

Endothrix infections, such as those caused by *Trichophyton tonsurans*, are dermatophyte infections in which the fungus grows inside the hair shaft. Because the causative organisms are dermatophytes, an antifungal that targets squalene epoxidase is mechanistically plausible.

There is an important limitation. Topical butenafine penetrates the hair follicle poorly, and infections involving the hair shaft usually need systemic therapy. The prediction is therefore mechanistically reasonable but practically uncertain, and no study retrieved for this report tests it.
