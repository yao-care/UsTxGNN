# Goserelin: From Endometriosis to Amenorrhea

## One-Sentence Summary

Goserelin is a GnRH agonist, originally used to treat endometriosis.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be relevant for **Amenorrhea**, but this is largely a pharmacological effect of the drug rather than a new therapeutic use. The prediction is currently supported by **1 clinical trial** (indirect) and **15 publications**.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Goserelin is a GnRH agonist. With continuous dosing, it desensitizes pituitary GnRH receptors, which suppresses LH/FSH and ovarian estradiol. The result is a reversible, drug-induced amenorrhea. This effect is well documented in breast cancer ovarian suppression and in endometriosis treatment.

The prediction is therefore pharmacologically consistent, but it largely reflects a side effect or pharmacodynamic action rather than a distinct disease-modifying use. Treating pathological amenorrhea with goserelin would be counterproductive, since the drug causes the condition. More plausible framings are:

- **Menstrual suppression** (e.g., a 1992 report discusses it for female military personnel).
- **Protection of ovarian function during chemotherapy** (the OPTION trial).

These framings differ from the predicted "amenorrhea" indication and would need to be defined as separate research questions.

The data compiled for this report contains no detailed mechanism-of-action entry for goserelin. The description above is based on its known pharmacology.

Two other predictions (renal hypoplasia and bilateral renal hypoplasia, both scoring about 99.06%) have no clinical trials, no literature, and no plausible biological rationale. They are evidence level L5 and should be held.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
Amenorrhea is mainly an expected pharmacological effect of goserelin, not a distinct therapeutic opportunity. The only trial is indirect (oncologic endpoint, unknown status). All US licences are cancelled, and the key safety documents are missing. The renal hypoplasia predictions have no supporting evidence at all.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- A clear therapeutic framing, such as menstrual suppression or ovarian protection during chemotherapy, rather than treatment of pathological amenorrhea.
- FDA package insert warnings and contraindications.
- Mechanism-of-action data from DrugBank.
- Clarification of the regulatory status of the cancelled Zoladex NDAs and confirmation of the approved indications.
- Direct clinical evidence (trials with amenorrhea or ovarian protection as the primary endpoint).
