## One-Sentence Summary

Dactinomycin is a cytotoxic antineoplastic originally used for paediatric and germ cell tumours such as Ewing sarcoma, Wilms tumour, rhabdomyosarcoma and gestational trophoblastic neoplasm.
The TxGNN model's top-ranked prediction is **relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis**, but there are **0 clinical trials** and **0 publications** supporting this direction. ## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available. Based on general pharmacological knowledge, dactinomycin is a DNA-intercalating transcription inhibitor used as a cytotoxic antineoplastic, and its efficacy in paediatric sarcomas and germ cell tumours is well established.

For **relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis**, no supporting mechanism has been identified. A nonspecific immunosuppressive or antiproliferative rationale is conceivable. However, the toxicity profile (myelosuppression, hepatotoxicity, extravasation injury) makes it unattractive for a chronic autoimmune disease that has many safer approved options. The score of 0.9958 is a computational prediction only.

The remaining top predictions are mostly rhabdomyosarcoma variants (botryoid vaginal, extrahepatic bile duct, embryonal bile duct, parameningeal and prostatic) and head and neck or hepatic sarcomas. Mechanistically these are close to the existing indications, because dactinomycin is a core component of the VAC and VA regimens. Broader categories such as "head and neck cancer" and "upper aerodigestive tract neoplasm" are supported only by evidence in sarcoma histologies, not in the epithelial carcinomas that dominate those categories.

## Safety Considerations

- **Disease-level contraindications (knowledge graph, not label text)**: liver disease, gallbladder disease, thrombocytopenia, neutropenia, agranulocytosis, anaemia (including macrocytic and aplastic anaemia), leukopenia, varicella zoster infection, herpes zoster, hepatic vein thrombosis, Budd-Chiari syndrome.
- **Hepatotoxicity**: Published cohorts report hepatopathy with VAC therapy, with age as a risk factor (PMID 15143082). Further toxicity data come from a COG retrospective study (PMID 21671362).
- **Late effects**: Treatment-related dental developmental abnormalities have been reported in paediatric survivors (PMID 33032302).
- **Drug interactions**: 100 interactions were found. Examples of major ones are **deferiprone** and **samarium (153Sm) lexidronam**. Moderate ones include palifermin, strontium chloride Sr-89, fostamatinib, mercaptopurine, roflumilast and chloramphenicol. A minor one is levofloxacin.

For label warnings and contraindications, please refer to the package insert.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The top-ranked prediction, relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis, rests on a model score alone (L5) with no trials, no literature and no plausible mechanism. The severe toxicity of dactinomycin is a poor fit for a chronic autoimmune disease. None of the head and neck or upper aerodigestive predictions shows efficacy beyond sarcoma histologies. All four US ANDAs are valid and the drug is marketed.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- FDA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data for dactinomycin
- For multiple sclerosis: any preclinical or mechanistic rationale, and a benefit-risk justification against approved therapies
- For rhabdomyosarcoma subtypes: confirmation of whether they are already covered by the labelled indication, and review of the unverified RCT classification of PMID 19770373
- Current US supply and authorization status
