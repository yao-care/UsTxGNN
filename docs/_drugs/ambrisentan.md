## Ambrisentan: From Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension to PAH Associated with Connective Tissue Disease
{: .text-alpha }

## One-Sentence Summary

Ambrisentan is an endothelin receptor antagonist originally used to treat pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH).
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **PAH associated with connective tissue disease (CTD-PAH)**,
with **2 clinical trials** and **19 publications** currently supporting this direction. Because CTD-PAH is a subgroup of the already-approved PAH indication, this is closer to an approved-indication subgroup than true repurposing.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the data compiled for this report. Based on known information, ambrisentan is a selective endothelin receptor type A antagonist. Endothelin-1 drives vasoconstriction, vascular remodeling and fibrosis in the pulmonary vasculature.

CTD-PAH, particularly PAH associated with systemic sclerosis, involves the same endothelin-pathway vasculopathy that ambrisentan targets in its original indication. Literature reviews describe endothelin as playing a key role in the development of systemic sclerosis-related PAH. Subgroup analyses of pivotal trials (ARIES-E, AMBITION) examined ambrisentan in CTD-PAH patients.

Evidence in this group is generally weaker than in idiopathic PAH, and patients with systemic sclerosis have historically shown an attenuated response to PAH therapy in many trials. Trials are small, so benefit in this subgroup is plausible but not firmly established.

## Safety Considerations

- **Contraindications flagged in the knowledge graph (not label text)**: congestive heart failure, anemia, macrocytic anemia, pulmonary veno-occlusive disease.
- **Drug Interactions**: 100 interactions were found. Moderate-level interactions include canagliflozin, dapagliflozin, empagliflozin, ertugliflozin, eliglustat and naltrexone. Minor-level interactions include clarithromycin, miconazole and omeprazole. Others, including metformin, simvastatin, prednisone and morphine, have unclassified severity.
- **Guardrails from the repurposing analysis**: embryo-fetal toxicity, liver function, edema and anemia, and caution in interstitial lung disease. These are not drawn from label text, so please refer to the package insert for confirmed warnings.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Proceed with Guardrails**

**Rationale:**
Two completed trials (a Phase 2 and a Phase 4), two meta-analyses and pivotal-trial subgroup analyses support ambrisentan in CTD-PAH. However, the trials are small (n=38 and n=25), one is single-arm, and this is largely a subgroup of the approved PAH indication rather than a new disease area. There is no completed Phase 3 trial specific to this population.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- FDA package insert warnings and contraindications (a blocking gap for safety screening)
- Mechanism of action data confirmed from DrugBank
- Clarification of US market status, since all listed authorizations are cancelled
- Larger controlled data specific to CTD-PAH, especially systemic sclerosis
- A monitoring plan for embryo-fetal toxicity, liver function, edema and anemia, plus interstitial lung disease screening

**Other predictions (for reference):**
- PAH associated with congenital heart disease (99.37%, L3): mostly observational data, including an ambrisentan-specific cohort study (PMID 21371683); Proceed with Guardrails.
- PAH associated with HIV infection (99.30%, L4): case series and registry data only; a research question, with antiretroviral interactions needing review.
- Pulmonary arteriovenous malformation (99.41%, L4), PAH with schistosomiasis, PAH with chronic hemolytic anemia (anemia is a known ambrisentan adverse effect), and the unrelated malformation or hair disorders (L5): Hold. Ambrisentan is teratogenic, so the developmental-malformation predictions are not credible.
