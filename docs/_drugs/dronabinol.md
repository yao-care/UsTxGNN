## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Currently, detailed mechanism of action data for the original indication is not available (DG002, High severity gap). Based on the pharmacological information available in the evidence pack, dronabinol is a synthetic Δ9-tetrahydrocannabinol (THC) and a CB1/CB2 cannabinoid receptor agonist.

The rationale for a migraine indication rests on the "clinical endocannabinoid deficiency" hypothesis: CB1 receptors are expressed on the trigeminovascular system and trigeminal ganglion, and endogenous cannabinoids (e.g., anandamide) are thought to modulate CGRP release and central sensitization — both central to migraine pathophysiology. This gives the prediction moderate-to-strong mechanistic plausibility.

However, most of the supporting clinical and literature evidence comes from inhaled/vaporized cannabis or multi-cannabinoid combinations (THC+CBD, THC+CBD+CBG) rather than purified dronabinol itself, so there is a meaningful gap between the drug entity being evaluated and the interventions actually studied. Only one completed trial (NCT00123201) and one case report (PMID 19220500) specifically name dronabinol.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
- The migraine indication has moderate mechanistic plausibility and one completed dronabinol-specific Phase 2 RCT (NCT00123201), supporting an L2 evidence level. However, a Blocking-severity data gap (DG001 — TFDA label warnings/contraindications unavailable) prevents this candidate from entering the safety pre-screening stage, and most supporting trials/literature studied cannabis or multi-cannabinoid products rather than dronabinol itself.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- TFDA/regulatory label data (warnings, contraindications) to unblock the safety initial evaluation (DG001)
- Confirmed mechanism of action and original approved indication for dronabinol in this dataset (DG002)
- Drug interaction (DDI) data — current query returned no results
- Clarification of drug-specificity: prioritize dronabinol-specific trial data over cannabis/multi-cannabinoid combination studies when assessing efficacy
