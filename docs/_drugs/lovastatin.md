# Lovastatin: From Statin Therapy for Hypercholesterolemia to Homozygous Familial Hypercholesterolemia

## One-Sentence Summary

Lovastatin is an HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor (statin) marketed in the US as an oral tablet for lowering cholesterol.
The TxGNN model predicts it may be effective for **homozygous familial hypercholesterolemia (HoFH)**.
This direction has **3 registered clinical trials** and **20 publications**, but none of the trials tests lovastatin and the lovastatin-specific papers are small studies and case reports, so the evidence is weak and partly negative.

## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Lovastatin inhibits HMG-CoA reductase, which lowers cholesterol synthesis in the liver. This upregulates hepatic LDL receptors and increases LDL clearance. Detailed mechanism data were not available from DrugBank for this report. The description above comes from the candidate's rationale analysis.

HoFH is a severe form of familial hypercholesterolemia with extremely high LDL-C. Because statins act mainly by upregulating LDL receptors, the mechanism fits only partly. Patients with some residual receptor function may respond. In receptor-negative patients there is little receptor activity to upregulate, so little effect is expected.

The literature matches this pattern:
- A 1988 study of three children with receptor-negative HoFH found no LDL-C reduction on lovastatin.
- Small reports from 1986 and 1990 show variable responses, mostly when lovastatin was combined with liver transplantation or LDL plasmapheresis.
- Overall, response appears to depend on residual receptor function.

## Safety Considerations

- **Drug Interactions**: The interaction query returned no records. The literature retrieved for this report does note severe myopathy and rhabdomyolysis with lovastatin plus gemfibrozil (12 FDA case reports, PMID 2355431) and with lovastatin plus nicotinic acid (PMID 3421570).

Please refer to the package insert for full warnings and contraindications.

## Conclusion and Next Steps

**Decision: Hold**

**Rationale:**
The TxGNN score is very high, but no registered trial tests lovastatin in HoFH. The lovastatin-specific evidence is limited to small studies and case reports, including a negative result in receptor-negative patients. The mechanism depends on residual LDL receptor function, so any benefit would likely be limited to a subset of patients. Guideline HoFH regimens rely on other agents, such as ezetimibe and PCSK9 inhibitors.

**To proceed, the following is needed:**
- Check the current US label for on-label status and the approved indication text, which is blank in the supplied data.
- Get lovastatin-specific data in HoFH stratified by LDL receptor status (receptor-negative vs. receptor-defective).
- Obtain the package insert warnings and contraindications, which are not in the data supplied.
- Obtain mechanism data from DrugBank for a fuller mechanistic analysis.
- Consider prioritizing the **familial hypercholesterolemia** (heterozygous) prediction from the same pack. It has stronger lovastatin-specific support: RCTs in adolescent boys and girls and a Phase 2/3 placebo-controlled study (NCT00116870, whose study population still needs confirming).
