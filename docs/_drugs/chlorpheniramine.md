## Why is This Prediction Reasonable?

Detailed mechanism of action data is not available in the source record. Chlorpheniramine (chlorphenamine) is a potent alkylamine first-generation H1-receptor antagonist, in use since the 1950s. It is commonly used for allergic conditions and over-the-counter cough and cold products.

In allergic urticaria, histamine released from mast cells drives the wheal and flare response. Blocking the H1 receptor therefore acts directly on the disease mechanism. H1 antihistamines are the mainstay of urticaria treatment, and a 2024 review lists chronic urticaria among chlorpheniramine's documented uses.

The missing original indication and MOA look like a source data gap rather than evidence of a novel use. The prediction is best read as confirming an existing, mechanistically direct use. Current practice favors second-generation antihistamines, so chlorpheniramine's value here is largely as a low-cost, long-established option.

## Safety Considerations

- **Hypersensitivity:** Rare cases of chlorpheniramine-induced anaphylaxis and immediate hypersensitivity are reported in the literature, one with aspirin intolerance. Screen for prior antihistamine hypersensitivity.

Package insert warnings, contraindications and interaction data were not obtained. Please refer to the package insert for safety information.
